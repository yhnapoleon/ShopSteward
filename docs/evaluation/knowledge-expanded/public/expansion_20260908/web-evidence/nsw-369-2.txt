L434@P11: • There must be separately located storage facilities for items such as chemicals, clothing and
L435@P11: personal belongings, which may contaminate food or food contact surfaces.
L436@P11: • Food including packaged drinks and alcohol should be stored at least 150mm above the floor
L437@P11: and kept out of direct sunlight.
L438@P11: • Potentially hazardous foods like poultry, meat, dairy products, seafood and egg-based products
L439@P11: must be stored under temperature control. If intended to be stored frozen, the food must remain
L440@P11: frozen during storage. (See Potentially hazardous foods: Foods that require temperature control
L441@P11: for safety) (PDF, 352 KB).
L442@P11: • Refrigeration facilities should be large enough to hold potentially hazardous foods under
L443@P11: temperature control at all times. This may require the use of portable coolrooms. Do not
L444@P11: overstock refrigerators or portable coolrooms, as the air will not be able to circulate freely
L445@P11: around the foods. Food must be stored off the floor in cool rooms and freezers.
L446@P11: • Coolrooms, fridges and freezers should be located out of direct sunlight. For multi-day events,
L447@P11: access to coolrooms should be via a hard surface, such as duckboards.
L448@P11: • Cold foods should be stored at or below 5°C.
L449@P11: • Hot food appliances (bain maries and display cases) should keep hot foods at or above, 60°C at
L450@P11: all times.
L451@P11: Food display
L452@P11: When displaying food, a business must take all practicable measures to protect the food from likely
L453@P11: contamination by customers, dust, fumes or insects. This may mean using plastic food wrap, sealed
L454@P11: containers, sneeze barriers, food covers or other effective measures.
L455@P11: Crockery, wrappings and packaging
L456@P11: • Single-use, disposable eating and drinking utensils may only be used if they meet the
L457@P11: requirements of the Plastics Reduction and Circular Economy Act 2021. More information is
L458@P11: available at epa.nsw.gov.au/Your-environment/Plastics/Bans-guidance.
L459@P11: • If re-usable dinnerware or tableware is used, a business must ensure that an appropriate method
L460@P11: for cleaning and sanitising is used (machine dishwashers are recommended).
L461@P11: • Crockery items that are chipped, cracked, broken or are in a state of disrepair must not be used
L462@P11: for food.
L463@P11: • Packaging material must be food-grade and unlikely to cause food contamination. Only clean
L464@P11: and unprinted paper, food wrap or packaging must be used for wrapping or storing food.
L465@P11: Single-use items
L466@P11: The Plastics Reduction and Circular Economy Act 2021 bans the supply of single-use plastic straws,
L467@P11: cutlery, stirrers, bowls, plates and other items.
L468@P11: Permitted single-use straws, eating utensils and other items that come into contact with food and a
L469@P11: person’s mouth must be protected from contamination until use and not re-used.

L486@P12: Food handler training requirements
L487@P12: All food businesses must ensure their food handlers have skills and knowledge in food safety and
L488@P12: food hygiene matters appropriate to the type of foods they are preparing/handling and their work
L489@P12: activities.
L490@P12: To meet their obligations, businesses can choose how food handlers are trained, but it should cover:
L491@P12: • safe handling of food
L492@P12: • understanding of food contamination
L493@P12: • cleaning and sanitising of food premises and equipment
L494@P12: • personal hygiene.
L495@P12: Various online food safety training programs, courses from vocational training providers, past
L496@P12: experience, or training developed by the business, may be used or recognised.
L497@P12: For more information on food handler training requirements, and to access the Food Authority’s free
L498@P12: Food Handler Basics training course, see foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/training/food-handler-basicstraining
L499@P12: Showing that your food is safe
L500@P12: Food businesses that process unpackaged, potentially hazardous food into a food that is ready-toeat and potentially hazardous must be able to show their food is safe under Standard 3.2.2A of the
L501@P12: Code. These businesses must be able to demonstrate they are monitoring and managing key food
L502@P12: safety risks related to receiving, storing, processing, displaying and transporting potentially
L503@P12: hazardous food, and cleaning and sanitising.
L504@P12: The requirements can be met by either:
L505@P12: • demonstrating safe food handling practices, such as having a written instruction sheet or
L506@P12: standard operating procedure, and/or being able to walk and talk an authorised officer through
L507@P12: their food handling practices, or
L508@P12: • keeping records.
L509@P12: For more information on this requirement, see Showing food is safe.

L511@P12: A person who is feeling unwell or suffering from a contagious illness must not handle food for sale.
L512@P12: Symptoms may include cold or flu symptoms, diarrhoea, vomiting, sore throat, fever, jaundice and

L517@P13: Requirements for food handlers
L518@P13: When engaged in any food handling operation, a food handler must:
L519@P13: • not contaminate food or food contact surfaces with their body or clothing
L520@P13: • prevent unnecessary contact with ready-to-eat food
L521@P13: • wear only clean outer clothing
L522@P13: • cover all dressing and bandages on exposed body parts with a waterproof dressing
L523@P13: • not eat over uncovered food or food contact surfaces
L524@P13: • not sneeze, blow or cough over uncovered food or surfaces likely to come into contact with food
L525@P13: • not spit, smoke or use tobacco while working in the food stall
L526@P13: • not urinate or defecate except in a toilet.
L527@P13: Hand washing
L528@P13: A food handler must wash their hands using soap and warm water, then dry them with single use,
L529@P13: towels:
L530@P13: • before commencing or re-commencing handling food
L531@P13: • immediately before handling ready-to-eat food after handling raw food
L532@P13: • immediately after using the toilet
L533@P13: • immediately after smoking, coughing, sneezing, using a handkerchief or tissue, eating, drinking
L534@P13: or touching their hair, scalp or a body opening, and before using disposable gloves for handling
L535@P13: food.
L536@P13: Food handlers wearing gloves should change gloves as often as they are required to wash their
L537@P13: hands.
L538@P13: Money handling/touching customers’ hands when serving food
L539@P13: While the likelihood of contamination from customers’ hands when exchanging money is low,
L540@P13: consideration should be given to minimising the risk. Examples include using a disposable glove or
L541@P13: nominating one staff member to handle money only.
L542@P13: Cleaning and sanitising
L543@P13: • The food stall must be maintained to a standard of cleanliness where there is no accumulation of
L544@P13: garbage or recycled matter (except in appropriate containers), food waste, dirt, grease or other
L545@P13: visible matter.
L546@P13: • All fixtures, fittings and equipment must be maintained and cleaned so there is no accumulation
L547@P13: of food waste, dirt, grease or other visible matter.
L548@P13: • Eating and drinking utensils must be in a clean, sanitary and undamaged condition immediately
L549@P13: before each use.
L550@P13: • Food contact surfaces (such as equipment and utensils) must be kept in a clean and sanitary
L551@P13: condition to reduce the likelihood of contaminating food. ‘Sanitary’ means cleaning first,
L552@P13: followed by heat and/or chemical treatment at the right concentration, or some other process to
L553@P13: reduce the number of bacteria to a level unlikely to compromise the safety of the food. ‘Foodgrade’ chlorine-based sanitisers can be used for this purpose.
L554@P13: • The containers used for chemical storage should be appropriately labelled.
L555@P13: • Chemicals must not be stored near food or any packaging likely to come in contact with food to
L556@P13-14: avoid the risk of contamination. Guidelines for food businesses at temporary events

L569@P14: either display allergen information next to the food or provide allergen information if requested by a
L570@P14: customer.

L590@P14: • pecan
L591@P14: • sulphites**

L607@P15: Electricity and gas supplies, fire extinguishers and work safety
L608@P15: • Food business operators (and event management) should ensure there is a sufficient supply of
L609@P15: electricity for food handling activities, particularly for hot and cold food holding and heating
L610@P15: water.
L611@P15: • Electricity should be supplied through proper supply poles equipped with all necessary safety
L612@P15: devices (as required by legislation). All work should be carried out by a licensed electrician and
L613@P15: conform to ‘AS/NZS 3002-2008: Electrical Installations – Shows and Carnivals’. SafeWork NSW
L614@P15: requires electrical appliances and leads to be tested at least annually and identification tags
L615@P15: attached.
L616@P15: • Fixed gas installations (such as in mobile food trucks) must be installed by a licensed gasfitter
L617@P15: and comply with the appropriate provisions of ‘AS/NZS 1596-2014: The Storage and Handling of
L618@P15: LP Gas’ and ‘AS 5601-2022: Gas Installations’. A current compliance plate should be attached to
L619@P15: the vehicle for new installations or any changes made to existing gas appliances.
L620@P15: • All portable gas appliances and appliances connected to Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG)
L621@P15: cylinders must be certified to comply with Australian Standards by a certifier accredited by NSW
L622@P15: Fair Trading. Refer to the NSW Fair Trading webpage on gas appliances:
L623@P15: fairtrading.nsw.gov.au/buying-products-and-services/product-and-service-safety/gas-safety
L624@P15: • Portable gas appliances should be located on a non-combustible surface with safe clearance
L625@P15: distances from combustible materials and in a well-ventilated location. LPG cylinders should be
L626@P15: secured so they remain upright and protected from damage. Refer to Energy Safe Victoria’s Gas
L627@P15: safety at public events brochure available at
L628@P15: www.energysafe.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-05/code-of-practice-lpg-public-events.pdf
L629@P15: (PDF, 3.8 MB) or contact SafeWork NSW for further information.
L630@P15: • Cartridge-operated appliances are not approved for use at public events. These appliances are
L631@P15: commonly used in domestic situations and are fuelled by a disposable butane gas cartridge.
L632@P15: • A fire extinguisher and fire blanket should be supplied in any vehicle or stall where cooking or
L633@P15: heating processes take place. Operators should be able to extinguish small fires if needed.
L634@P15: • Fire safety equipment should be easily accessible. The extinguisher should be suitable for
L635@P15: dealing with the type of combustible materials present.
L636@P15: • Fire safety equipment should be tested annually and have current tagging in accordance with
L637@P15: ‘AS 1851-2012: Routine Service of Fire Protection Systems and Equipment’. Contact Fire and
L638@P15: Rescue NSW for more information.
L639@P15: • All measures must be taken to comply with the requirements of SafeWork NSW to protect the
L640@P15: health, safety and welfare of workers and visitors at the event. Contact SafeWork NSW for more
L641@P15: information.

L735@P20: Key definitions
L736@P20: Adequate supply of water
L737@P20: Potable water available at a volume, pressure and temperature adequate for the purposes for which
L738@P20: the water is used.
L739@P20: Australian/New Zealand Standards
L740@P20: Australian/New Zealand Standards are documents which are referenced by legislation to provide
L741@P20: more detail on requirements and technical procedures. These standards can be purchased from
L742@P20: Standards Australia on 1300 654 646 or by visiting its website at www.standards.com.au.
L743@P20: Coving
L744@P20: A curved junction between the floor and the wall.
L745@P20: Equipment
L746@P20: A machine, instrument, apparatus, utensil or appliance — other than a single use item — used or
L747@P20: intended to be used or in connection with food handling; includes any equipment used or intended to
L748@P20: be used to clean the food premises or equipment.
L749@P20: Food business
L750@P20: A business, enterprise or activity (other than primary food production) that involves:
L751@P20: • handling of food intended for sale, or
L752@P20: • sale of food regardless of whether the business enterprise or activity concerned is of a
L753@P20: commercial, charitable or community nature or whether it involves the handling or sale of food
L754@P20: on one occasion only.
L755@P20: Food handler
L756@P20: Any person who directly engages in the handling of food, or who handles surfaces likely to come
L757@P20: into contact with food for a food business.
L758@P20: Food premises/market stall
L759@P20: Any premises including land vehicles, parts of structures, tents stalls and other temporary
L760@P20: structures, boats, pontoons, including premises used principally as a private dwelling but not food
L761@P20: vending machines or vehicles used only to transport food.
L762@P20: Food Safety Standards
L763@P20: These are part of the Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code (enacted in NSW under the NSW
L764@P20: Food Act 2003 and NSW Food Regulation 2025). They define requirements for food premises
L765@P20: (including vehicles), food handling practices, structural requirements and labelling.
L766@P20: Food Safety Supervisor
L767@P20: A Food Safety Supervisor is a person who:
L768@P20: • is formally trained to recognise and prevent risks associated with food handling in food
L769@P20: businesses
L770@P20: • holds a current NSW Food Authority FSS certificate (no more than 5 years old) as per the NSW
L771@P20-21: Food Act Section 106B(1a)Guidelines for food businesses at temporary events

L773@P21: • is not an FSS for any other food premises or mobile catering business
L774@P21: • trains and supervises other people in the business about safe food handling practices, and
L775@P21: • has the authority and ability to manage and give direction on the safe handling of food.
L776@P21: Handling of food
L777@P21: Includes the making, manufacturing, producing, collecting, extracting, processing, storing,
L778@P21: transporting, delivering, preparing, treating, preserving, cooking, thawing, serving or displaying of
L779@P21: food.
L780@P21: Home jurisdiction
L781@P21: The home jurisdiction rule recognises the enforcement agency (usually the local council) in whose
L782@P21: area food is prepared before sale at a temporary event or in the case of a mobile food vendor the
L783@P21: area where the mobile food vending vehicle is garaged.
L784@P21: Potable water
L785@P21: Water suitable (safe) for drinking.
L786@P21: Potentially hazardous food
L787@P21: Food that must be kept at certain temperatures to minimise the growth of any pathogenic microorganisms that may be present in the food and/or to prevent the formation of toxins. This may
L788@P21: include meat, seafood, dairy products, orange juice and cooked rice.
L789@P21: Sanitising
L790@P21: A process that significantly reduces the number of micro-organisms present on a surface. This is
L791@P21: usually achieved by using both heat and water or chemicals.

L822@P22: Unsafe and unsuitable food
L823@P22: Food is considered unsafe if it is likely to cause physical harm to a person who might later consume
L824@P22: it, assuming they treated the food correctly after purchase.
L825@P22: Food is considered unsuitable if it is damaged, deteriorated or perished to an extent that affects its
L826@P22: intended use, e.g. out of date, poor maintenance or poor storage of chemicals or contains a
L827@P22: substance that is foreign to the nature of the food (i.e. foreign matter or chemicals which may have
L828@P22: fallen into the food).
