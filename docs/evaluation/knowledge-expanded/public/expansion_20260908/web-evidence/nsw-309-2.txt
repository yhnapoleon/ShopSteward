L482@P21: Businesses exempt from Fast Choices
L483@P21: Not all business es that meet the criteria for a standard food outlet are captured. The legis lation
L484@P21: exempt s some businesses from display ing kJ due to the nature of the food products they sell, or the
L485@P21: way the food products are sold .
L486@P21: The businesses exempt by the legislation are:
L487@P21: • convenience stores ( that is, not a supermarket)
L488@P21: • service stations selling fuel for motor vehicles
L489@P21: • food businesses that primarily provide food catering services
L490@P21: • food businesses that only sell food for consumption on the premises at which it is sold ( for
L491@P21: example , dine -in only restaurants and cafés)
L492@P21: • food sold by retail at a healthcare facility
L493@P21: • vending machines selling prepackaged food labelled with a NIP .
L494@P21: Voluntary display of nutritional information
L495@P21: Food businesses may voluntarily display nutritional information.
L496@P21: If a food business choose s to display nutritional information, it must be displayed on all standard
L497@P21: food items . The kJ content must be:
L498@P21: • legible and displayed as ‘kJ’
L499@P21: • adjacent to, or near the item so as to be clearly associated with the item
L500@P21: • in the same font, and at least the same font size as the price of the standard food item, or the
L501@P21: same font size as the name of the item if no price is displayed .
L502@P21: Penalties apply for standard food outlets that voluntarily display this information without complying
L503@P21: with the full requirements of the legislation.

L544@P24: What is a menu?
L545@P24: A menu is any display that identifies the available food items for the consumer to choose from when
L546@P24: purchasing at a point of sale.
L547@P24: A menu is:
L548@P24: • physically printed or a digital display
L549@P24: • any display that shows the name or price of a standard food item. It may or may not include a
L550@P24: picture or an ingredient list.
L551@P24: • visible to prospective consumers at the point of sale. This means all advertising at an outlet that
L552@P24: shows standard food items with name or price of the item is captured, as well as item and price
L553@P24: tags for products on display, and mounted boards – whether overhead, on walls, counters, or
L554@P24: mobile /digital frames.
L555@P24: • digital advertising that directs potential customers to a point of sale through embedded
L556@P24: hyperlinks . The digital advertising itself may not be a menu, however if the hyperlink enables
L557@P24: food ordering – nutritional information must be displayed on the menu accessed through the link.
L558@P24: • self -serve displays and kiosks, and apps or webpages accessed via QR code. The QR code i s not
L559@P24: a menu, however if the QR code leads to a platform where a customer can order food from a
L560@P24: standard food outlet – nutritional information must be displayed on the menu accessed through
L561@P24: the QR code.

L597@P28: Information that needs to be displayed
L598@P28: Meaning of ‘legible’
L599@P28: The nutritional information must be displayed so that it can be easily read by customers.
L600@P28: The legislation requires that it is in the same font, and at least the same size as the price and name
L601@P28: of the item . The font colour may be different to the price and name of the corresponding standard
L602@P28: food item . The Food Authority has determined that the colour of the nutritional information should
L603@P28: be contrasted with the background colour and not be less than 70% opacity.
L604@P28: Note: Queensland legislation requires that kJ are displayed in the same colour as the price. Food
L605@P28: businesses operating with more than 50 outlets should confirm they comply with other states’
L606@P28: legislation. The NSW Food Authority is supportive of businesses who choose to make the kJ
L607@P28: information displayed in NSW stores the same colour, size and font a s the price and name of the
L608@P28: standard food item so that they only have to implement one system across Australia.
L609@P28: Vision Australia provide s colour accessibility tools and resources to check colour functionality and
L610@P28: avoid colour barriers . Visit : Colour accessibility tools and resources | Vision Australia. Blindness and
L611@P28: low vision services (visionaustralia.org/business -consulting/digital -access/resources/colour -
L612@P28: contrast -analyser).
L613@P28: Meaning of ‘adjacent to’ or ‘ near ’
L614@P28: The legislation requires :
L615@P28: • the average kJ content to be located adjacent to or near the price or name (if no price is
L616@P28: displayed) of the standard food item. This means that the kJ must be near, close to or next to the
L617@P28: price or name of the standard food item.
L618@P28: • the reference statement to be located adjacent to or near the name of the standard food item.
L619@P28: This means that the reference statement must be near or close to the name of the standard food

L678@P34: Kilojoule information
L679@P34: Kilojoule (kJ) is a unit of measurement for the energy content in food. For Fast Choices, energy
L680@P34: content must be displayed in kJ. Kilojoules are used because total energy intake is easiest
L681@P34: comparison for consumers to consider the health effect of a broad range of food .
L682@P34: How to display the average kJ content
L683@P34: When displaying the average kJ content for a standard food item it must be:
L684@P34: • legible
L685@P34: • expressed as ‘kJ’
L686@P34: • in the same font, and at least the same font size, as the price displayed for the item ; or if no price
L687@P34: is displayed, as the name of the item
L688@P34: • adjacent to or near the name or price of the item.
L689@P34: If standard food items are displayed on the menu and a tag, the average kJ content needs to be
L690@P34: displayed on both the menu and the tag.
L691@P34: Where the numerals in the price vary in size, the font size used for the kJ content must be at least
L692@P34: the same size as the largest numeral in the price.
L693@P34: Calculating the average kJ content
L694@P34: The legislation requires the average kJ content for each standard food item to be determined using
L695@P34: Standard 1.2.8 and section S11 -2 of Schedule 11 of the Food Standards Code.
L696@P34: The average kJ content can be determined by calculating the average kJ content of the whole item
L697@P34: or per 100g. If the value must be displayed for the whole item, a business must make the necessary
L698@P34: adjustments to ensure that the calculation is done in relati on to the whole item.
L699@P34: There are numerous ways to calculate the average kJ content. These include:
L700@P34: • online Nutrition Panel Calculator developed by Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ)
L701@P34: www.foodstandards.gov.au/business/labelling/nutrition -panel -calculator
L702@P34: • nutritional analysis software
L703@P34: • laboratory analysis
L704@P34: • food composition tables and databases.
L705@P34: The legislation allows the kJ content to be rounded to the nearest 10 kJ.
L706@P34: The average kJ content displayed for a standard food item may be based on average analysis of the
L707@P34: item with a standard recipe. A business may demonstrate the accurate calculation of the kJ by
L708@P34: maintaining the analysis data for standard recipes.
L709@P34: It is recommended that the business determines the average kJ content from a couple of analyses of
L710@P34: each food item (for example, a burger following the standard recipe made by three different food
L711@P34-35: handlers).Fast Choice s progra m: nutritional information menu labelling
L712@P35: FA698/2509 36
L713@P35: The legislation and the Food Standards Code do not apply an acceptable variance to the displayed
L714@P35: average kJ content. The Food Authority understands that a food item purchased by a consumer may
L715@P35: have slightly lower or higher kJ compared to the displayed kJ value due to unintentional error during
L716@P35: making the food. The Food Authority will take a practical approach to minor kJ discrepancies during
L717@P35: verification sampling.
L718@P35: Businesses also need to ensure that when they display nutritional information it complies with other
L719@P35: relevant legislation relating to claims (for example, the Competition and Consumer Act 2010 ).
L720@P35: Other considerations
L721@P35: Wall posters
L722@P35: Nutrition information provided on wall posters do not replace the need to display the required
L723@P35: nutritional information on all menus. A nutrition information poster with additional content , for
L724@P35: example, protein, salt, fat is a business decision for the outlet.
L725@P35: Businesses may provide additional or supporting information, but this does not remove the
L726@P35: requirement to display nutritional information in line with the legislation.
L727@P35: Displaying the name and price of standard food items
L728@P35: The legislation does not dictate the readability , font, font size or font colour for the name or price of
L729@P35: standard food items. The legislation does appl y requirements to How to display the average kJ
L730@P35: content of standard food items.
L731@P35: Thumbnails, colour guide or print size
L732@P35: The NSW legislation does not apply requirements to thumbnail s (front -of-pack display information),

L740@P37: Approval of menu s
L741@P37: Businesses are not required to submit their menus to the Food Authority for approval to ensure they
L742@P37: comply with the legislation.
L743@P37: However, businesses implementing the legislation for the first time or businesses updating their
L744@P37: menus are encouraged to consult with the Food Authority to ensure their menus comply with the

L746@P37: Verification activities
L747@P37: The NSW legislation is enforced in a variety of ways including:
L748@P37: • checks by local government authorised officers for the presence or absence of nutritional
L749@P37: information during routine inspections of retail food businesses. Any issues are reported to the
L750@P37: Food Authority to follow -up.
L751@P37: • ongoing Food Authority product testing program to verify the accuracy of the average kJ content
L752@P37: displayed on menus
L753@P37: • a compliance program to verify compliance with the legislation, including in -store, online, apps,
L754@P37: and takeaway menus by Food Authority authorised officers
L755@P37: • complaints reported to the Food Authority.
L756@P37: Non -compliance with the legislation
L757@P37: The proprietor is responsible for complying with the legislation as they are the operator of the
L758@P37: ‘standard food outlet’ . There fore, the proprietor will be penalised for non -compliance following an
L759@P37: inspection of a retail outlet .
L760@P37: However, t he Food Authority will also work with the franchisor or head office of the company to
L761@P37: address issues , as menus and tags are usually developed and distributed from a central office.
L762@P37: If the store owner develops their own marketing, for example posters, it is their responsibility to
L763@P37: ensure these comply with the nutritional information labelling requirements (if applicable).
