L4@P0: • preparing food for sale at markets or school canteens in a domestic kitchen

L9@P0: Notification
L10@P0: When a food business sells directly to consumers
L11@P0: Local councils regulate all domestic kitchens preparing food sold directly to the final consumer, either
L12@P0: from the premises or elsewhere - such as at a market or school canteen.
L13@P0: These businesses need to notify their details, including trading name, contact details, location(s),
L14@P0: business owner details and the nature of the food business.
L15@P0: Councils collect this information in different ways – a good place to start is your local council’s website.
L16@P0: • Find your local council.
L17@P0: When businesses do not sell food directly to consumers
L18@P0: The NSW Food Authority regulates domestic kitchens that only sell food to other businesses, such as
L19@P0: cafes, restaurants and supermarkets.
L20@P0: These businesses need to notify the NSW Food Authority of their business and food activity details.

L31@P1: Before a food business can start operating, owners need to make sure the proposed activities are
L32@P1: approved by their local council. Some councils may not approve operations involving high risk foods.
L33@P1: Standard 3.2.2A – Food Safety Management Tools
L34@P1: Home-based businesses that sell or serve food that is unpackaged, potentially hazardous and readyto-eat, directly to consumers, need to implement 2 or 3 food safety management tools, depending on
L35@P1: their activities. These tools are:
L36@P1: • have a qualified Food Safety Supervisor
L37@P1: • ensure all food handlers are trained
L38@P1: • be able to show food is safe.
L39@P1: Some businesses, including school canteens and home-based children’s services, have until 8
L40@P1: December 2024 to comply with Standard 3.2.2A.
L41@P1: For more information see Standard 3.2.2A - Food Safety Management Tools.
L42@P1: Issues to consider
L43@P1: Anyone in charge of a food business needs to identify food safety issues and implement measures to
L44@P1: control risks.
L45@P1: Premises design and construction
L46@P1: Standards for flooring in kitchens and storerooms, and requirements for personal hygiene areas, such
L47@P1: as adequate hand washing facilities, need to be met. Check with the local council about what is
L48@P1: acceptable. They can also advise on zoning restrictions, development planning, construction and fitout standards, waste disposal, environmental controls and any other local regulations.
L49@P1: Keep cold foods properly refrigerated
L50@P1: Adequate refrigeration capacity is essential to ensure cold food remains safe. It is important to keep
L51@P1: certain food such as meat, eggs and dairy under 5°C at all times, including during transport.
L52@P1: Overloading domestic refrigerators and constantly opening the door means food takes longer to cool
L53@P1: or does not stay cold enough, meaning harmful microorganisms have more chance to grow. Always
L54@P1: use a fridge thermometer to check your fridge is cold enough.
L55@P1: Cook food thoroughly
L56@P1: Cook food thoroughly without overloading the oven. Cool down cooked food as quickly as possible –
L57@P1: for example refrigerate in small portions to allow proper cooling. Use a food thermometer to make sure
L58@P1: what you are cooking reaches the required temperature.
L59@P1: Handle food hygienically
L60@P1: It is very important to keep ready-to-eat food and raw food or ingredients separate. This means that
L61@P1: food contact surfaces, utensils, containers, tea towels and the like should not be used for both raw and
L62@P1: ready-to-eat foods without being thoroughly cleaned in between.
L63@P1: Everyone who handles food for sale must have food safety skills and knowledge appropriate to their
L64@P1: activities. Top of the list is proper hand washing, especially after using the toilet. Single-use towels are
L65@P1: the safest way to dry hands, as tea towels can transfer dangerous organisms between hands and
L66@P1-2: food.Home-based food business

L68@P2: Young children, pets, and people who are sick should stay out of food preparation areas. Food must
L69@P2: be protected from pests and vermin at all times, including raw ingredients.
L70@P2: Cleaning and sanitising
L71@P2: Food premises, including fixtures, fittings and equipment must be maintained in a clean condition and
L72@P2: all food contact surfaces be cleaned and sanitised.
L73@P2: Cleaning and sanitising are 2 separate and important tasks. These critical processes help prevent the
L74@P2: growth and spread of organisms that cause food poisoning and help reduce the activity of pests.
L75@P2: A food business must also ensure that eating and drinking utensils, and food contact surfaces of
L76@P2: equipment, are clean and sanitised. Garbage and recycled matter needs to be stored in appropriate
L77@P2: containers.
L78@P2: The factsheet Cleaning and sanitising in food businesses outlines requirements.
L79@P2: Store food safely
L80@P2: Food containers and other containers should not be re-used if they are not rated for multiple use by
L81@P2: the manufacturer. Some food containers can transfer harmful chemicals to food if not used correctly.
L82@P2: Product labelling
L83@P2: Labels of packaged foods must show:
L84@P2: • name and/or description of the food
L85@P2: • identification of the ‘lot’ number
L86@P2: • name and Australian street address of the manufacturer or supplier
L87@P2: • the list of ingredients
L88@P2: • ‘best before’ or ‘use-by’ date, as appropriate
L89@P2: • batch numbering for traceability
L90@P2: • a Nutrition Information Panel (unless exempt)
L91@P2: • the country of origin of the product and its ingredients
L92@P2: • warning and advisory statements and declarations
L93@P2: • any other requirements of the Food Standards Code.
L94@P2: Our Labelling fact sheet outlines the requirements.
L95@P2: There are also labelling restrictions to comply with, such as declaring ‘characterising ingredients’,
L96@P2: making health claims in product marketing and other prohibitions.
L97@P2: Keep records
L98@P2: Keep batch records of ingredients so these can be traced if an ingredient is recalled by another
L99@P2: producer.
