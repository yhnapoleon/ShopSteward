L0@P0: Food Authority
L1@P0: If not made hygienically, doner kebabs can pose a food safety risk for customers
L2@P0: because some of the ingredients can allow the rapid growth of disease-causing
L3@P0: bacteria. Food businesses must ensure the food they sell is safe and properly
L4@P0: handled.
L5@P0: Making kebabs safely
L6@P0: Keep everything clean
L7@P0: • Wash hands in a proper hand washing facility whenever they are a likely source of contamination
L8@P0: (such as after hanging a kebab block, after using the toilet.).
L9@P0: • Cover food in the refrigerator.
L10@P0: Control temperature
L11@P0: • It is easy to contaminate kebab ingredients such as hummus, tabouli and cheese. Only use such
L12@P0: ingredients if they are properly refrigerated (at or below 5°C), packaged and labelled with date
L13@P0: marks.
L14@P0: • If you thaw frozen kebab meat before cooking, thaw it under refrigeration.
L15@P0: • Keep fresh minced meat for making kebabs under refrigeration until ready for use. Refrigerate
L16@P0: freshly made kebab meat while it is setting.
L17@P0: • Store potentially hazardous food, including dairy-based sauces, at or below 5°C. Check the
L18@P0: temperature using a thermometer.
L19@P0: • The bacteria that cause food poisoning grow between 5°C and 60°C – the temperature danger
L20@P0: zone. Potentially hazardous foods that have been in the temperature danger zone for more than
L21@P0: four hours must be thrown out. Keep cold food in the fridge until you are ready to cook or serve it,
L22@P0: and serve hot food steaming hot.
L23@P0: • Do not overload refrigerators as this reduces their cooling efficiency.
L24@P0: Cook thoroughly
L25@P0: • Start cooking the kebab immediately after removing it from cold storage.
L26@P0: • Ensure meat sliced from the kebab is properly cooked. Once cooked, keep above 60°C until
L27@P0: served. Use a thermometer to check temperatures.
L28@P0: • Best practice is to use a second cook step by heating the cooked, kebab block. Any leftover kebab
L29@P0: meat that has been fully cooked may be used the next day provided it is cooled to less than 21°C
L30@P0: within two hours from when cooking stops, and then to 5°C or less within the next four hours.

L33@P1: • Monitor temperatures with a probe thermometer. A quick way to bring the temperature down is to
L34@P1: put the meat in the freezer.
L35@P1: • Before serving the meat, reheat it until it is above 60°C.
L36@P1: Prevent contamination
L37@P1: • Keep raw and cooked food separate.
L38@P1: • Remember to wash your hands thoroughly in hot soapy water and dry them before preparing food
L39@P1: and after touching raw meat, especially chicken, and other raw foods.
L40@P1: • Thoroughly clean all utensils, equipment and surfaces after preparing raw food and before contact
L41@P1: with other food.
L42@P1: • Store raw meat, chicken and seafood at the bottom of the fridge so it can’t drip onto other foods.
L43@P1: • Keep pets and animals out of the kitchen.
L44@P1: • Do not handle food if you have symptoms of a foodborne illness (see related factsheet Health and
L45@P1: hygiene requirements of food handlers).
