L56@P2: Introduction
L57@P2: These guidelines inform businesses selling food at temporary events in NSW of their legal
L58@P2: requirements in relation to the NSW Food Act 2003 (the Act) and the Food Standard Code (the Code).
L59@P2: They provide information on requirements such as power supply and waste disposal and suggest
L60@P2: ways to maintain food safety at the event.
L61@P2: Businesses and local council environmental health officers (EHOs) can use this guide to assess
L62@P2: compliance with the Act and the Code.
L63@P2: The primary aim of these guidelines is to make food businesses aware of their legal requirements
L64@P2: and to suggest ways to assure food safety when selling food. It also provides businesses with a
L65@P2: guide to applicable fees and charges that councils may levy.
L66@P2: Food businesses wishing to sell food at temporary events should contact the council where the
L67@P2: event is based prior to operating to check if a permit to trade on public land is required, if an
L68@P2: inspection must be completed, if there are any applicable fees or if there are any other
L69@P2: requirements.
L70@P2: What is a temporary event?
L71@P2: A temporary event is any occasion of limited duration or periodic in nature where food is sold to
L72@P2: consumers from a temporary structure or vehicle. Examples include fairs, festivals, markets and
L73@P2: shows.
L74@P2: These guidelines cover all types of food businesses at temporary events and provide guidance for
L75@P2: additional requirements when the temporary event continues over several days, introducing
L76@P2: additional risks. The checklist provided in the document can be used to self-assess compliance with
L77@P2: general requirements.
L78@P2: While mobile food vending vehicles often operate at temporary events, it is recommended that they
L79@P2: refer to the Food Authority’s Guidelines for mobile food vending vehicles (PDF, 422 KB)).
L80@P2: Obligations on selling food
L81@P2: A person handling or selling food or operating stalls used for selling food for human consumption,
L82@P2: including drinks, produce, fruit and vegetables or pre-packaged food, is deemed to be a ‘food
L83@P2: business’. This includes not-for-profit operations.
L84@P2: A food business is required to sell safe and suitable food in accordance with the Act, which also
L85@P2: mandates compliance with the national Code. Of particular relevance for temporary events are parts
L86@P2: 1.2 (labelling) and 3.1.1, 3.2.2, 3.2.2A and 3.2.3 (food safety standards) of the Code, which can be
L87@P2: accessed at www.foodstandards.gov.au.
L88@P2: Failure to comply with the requirements may lead to enforcement action. Depending on the food
L89@P2: safety risk identified, this action may include a warning letter, improvement notice, penalty notice,
L90@P2: seizure, prohibition or prosecution. The NSW Food Authority and councils generally follow an
L91@P2: escalating enforcement policy. For more information see foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/ip/audits-andcompliance/compliance.
L92@P2: Which agencies enforce the Act and Code?
L93@P2: In most cases, local councils are responsible for the surveillance of the retail food sector and
L94@P2: enforce the requirements of the Act and Code; this includes food for sale at temporary events.
L95@P2: The Food Authority is only responsible for surveillance if there is processing of products that require
L96@P2: a licence at that premises, such as:

L105@P3: Charities and community groups
L106@P3: Charities and community groups are those that do not derive funds for personal financial gain but
L107@P3: direct all profits back to the community (such as local sports clubs, and Lions and Rotary clubs).
L108@P3: The Act and Code apply to all food businesses including those selling food for charity or community
L109@P3: purposes. It is always an offence to sell food that is unsafe or unsuitable.

L112@P3: example, scones, or is to be consumed immediately after thorough cooking, such as a sausage
L113@P3: sizzle.

L115@P3: Code’s Standard 3.2.2A, do not apply if the food is handled or sold for the purpose of raising
L116@P3: funds solely for community or charitable causes.

L144@P4: Administration
L145@P4: Notification
L146@P4: Food businesses that trade at temporary events must notify their details to the local council before
L147@P4: trading for the first time. Businesses must notify their home jurisdiction council where food is
L148@P4: prepared before sale at a temporary event, and the local councils where they intend to trade.
L149@P4: Notification is a compulsory requirement.
L150@P4: The only exceptions are:
L151@P4: • businesses licensed or notified with the Food Authority, or
L152@P4: • not-for-profit fundraising events where there is sale of low-risk food (tea/coffee and biscuits) or
L153@P4: sale of food that is thoroughly cooked immediately before consumption, such as a sausage
L154@P4: sizzle.
L155@P4: Appropriate details for notification include:
L156@P4: • contact details for the food business, including the name of the food business and the name and
L157@P4: business address of the proprietor
L158@P4: • the nature of the food business
L159@P4: • the location of all food premises of the food business that are within the jurisdiction of the
L160@P4: enforcement agency.
L161@P4: Notification is a one-off process unless your details change, in which case you are required to
L162@P4: provide an update to the appropriate authority.
L163@P4: Licensing
L164@P4: Some retailers at temporary events require a Food Authority licence if there is an activity that
L165@P4: requires a licence in the business, such as:
L166@P4: • businesses that conduct food service to vulnerable persons
L167@P4: • high-risk plant product businesses
L168@P4: • businesses that handle or process meat
L169@P4: • businesses that further process seafood
L170@P4: • businesses that handle shellfish
L171@P4: • dairy producers, factories and vendors
L172@P4: • businesses that produce or process eggs and egg-related products.
L173@P4: If you carry out any of these activities, contact the Food Authority well before the event to
L174@P4: determine whether you require a licence.
L175@P4: Council approvals
L176@P4: Local councils are generally responsible for the approval of temporary events and the stalls
L177@P4: retailing food (including alcohol) at that event. Some councils require development consent for
L178@P4: temporary events, so you may be required to lodge a development application. Some councils
L179@P4: require the temporary site itself to be registered/approved (for example, s68 approval to operate
L180@P4: issued under the Local Government Act 1993).
L181@P4: It is important to check specific requirements with the local council well before the event.
L182@P4: To avoid unnecessary interruption to your business, have your valid council approval available so
L183@P4-5: that inspecting officers (and customers) can retrieve information about the stall operator if required. Guidelines for food businesses at temporary events

L185@P5: Training requirements
L186@P5: Food businesses selling unpackaged, potentially hazardous ready-to-eat food at temporary events
L187@P5: will need to appoint a Food Safety Supervisor. Ensure this is done well before the event (refer to
L188@P5: page 12 for details). In addition, all food handlers must have skills and knowledge in food safety and
L189@P5: hygiene commensurate with their food handling activities (see page 13 for details).
L190@P5: Inspections
L191@P5: The inspection of food stalls at temporary events are conducted by the local council’s environmental
L192@P5: health officers (EHOs) who are authorised officers under the Act. The frequency of inspection can
L193@P5: vary and may depend on any food safety risk of the food being handled or any previous enforcement
L194@P5: history. They check that good food safety practices are in place, such as temperature control,
L195@P5: cleanliness, hand washing and labelling (refer to the checklist at the end of this document).
L196@P5: Small events where low-risk foods are sold may not require inspection. Large events, particularly
L197@P5: where high-risk foods are sold or where businesses have a poor compliance history, are likely to be
L198@P5: inspected.
L199@P5: If stall operators trade across council boundaries, most councils will use a home jurisdiction rule.
L200@P5: This means the stall operator will be inspected by the council local to where that business
L201@P5: originates. That council is known as the ‘home council’.
L202@P5: Other councils in which the stall operator trades are entitled to request to see a copy of the most
L203@P5: recent inspection report (less than 18 months old) from the proprietor. If the report is satisfactory
L204@P5: (only minor issues identified), the council EHO should not conduct a further inspection, unless there
L205@P5: is a perceived risk to food safety and public health.
L206@P5: If a recent inspection report is not provided by the stall operator, is more than 18 months old, or has
L207@P5: a major noncompliance issue outstanding, then the council EHO has the discretion to carry out an
L208@P5: inspection and charge an inspection fee.
L209@P5: Stall operators/proprietors are responsible for organising to have an inspection when they begin to
L210@P5: trade and providing a current inspection report to officers from another council in whose area they
L211@P5: trade.
L212@P5: Carry your most recent inspection report with you whenever you are trading
L213@P5: from your temporary stall.
L214@P5: Councils adopt a risk-based approach to determine when to inspect food businesses at temporary
L215@P5: events. The risk factors considered are:
L216@P5: • the number of food businesses trading at the event
L217@P5: • whether the businesses provide (in advance) evidence of a satisfactory inspection by their home
L218@P5: jurisdiction or other enforcement agency
L219@P5: • whether there has been poor compliance history of businesses at the event
L220@P5: • any food safety complaints made against food businesses trading at the event
L221@P5: • the type of food being sold (potentially hazardous food vs non-potentially hazardous food)
L222@P5: • estimated number of visitors to the event
L223@P5: • duration of the event
L224@P5: • access to facilities and services such as potable water, to wastewater management staff toilets,
L225@P5-6: waste management, electricity. Guidelines for food businesses at temporary events

L227@P6: It is recommended that a business keeps a copy of their last inspection report at their premises to
L228@P6: demonstrate compliance history.
L229@P6: Fees and charges
L230@P6: The local council may charge fees for inspecting your food handling activities under the Local
L231@P6: Government Act 1993. The fee amount can vary between councils.
L232@P6: Check with the local council/s in which you trade to find out what fees apply to you. There may be
L233@P6: other fees and charges levied by councils for approval to trade under other legislation.
L234@P6: Councils can levy an inspection fee against the event organiser, or against the individual temporary
L235@P6: food businesses. The inspection fee can be a fixed fee or based on the council’s hourly food
L236@P6: inspection rate.
L237@P6: If the council decides an additional inspection is required for a business (because of poor
L238@P6: compliance), an inspection fee can be levied against the food business.
L239@P6: Conditions of operation
L240@P6: Location of food stalls
L241@P6: • Stalls should be set up on sealed ground and away from drainage lines to avoid cross
L242@P6: contamination and maintain stall cleanliness. If only unsealed sites are available or where
L243@P6: potentially hazardous food is being prepared on site, the ground where food is being prepared
L244@P6: and stored should be completely covered.
L245@P6: • Multi-day events that remain committed to trading regardless of weather conditions will need to
L246@P6: implement further measures such as raised floors. Floor coverings must be easily cleaned and
L247@P6: non-absorbent.
L248@P6: • The open side of the stall should not face prevailing winds to reduce dust, odour and insect
L249@P6: problems.
L250@P6: • Stalls should be located away from toilets and garbage areas to prevent airborne contamination.
L251@P6: Toilets
L252@P6: Food business operators (and event management) must ensure that adequate toilet facilities are
L253@P6: available for food handlers. This includes adequate hand washing facilities including the provision of
L254@P6: warm water, soap and an effective means of drying hands (such as paper towels). Multi-day events
L255@P6: should have an appropriate ratio of staff only toilets to the number of food stalls trading. Using the
L256@P6: Building Code of Australia table F2.3 the recommended ratio for toilets in class 6 buildings
L257@P6: (restaurant/café) is 1 toilet per 15 employees.
L258@P6: Maintenance
L259@P6: The food stall and its fixtures, fittings and equipment, toilets and hand washing facilities, as well as
L260@P6: parts of vehicles used to transport food, must be kept clean, sanitised (where appropriate) and in
L261@P6: good, working order (free from dirt, fumes, smoke, odours and other contaminants). Timber surfaces
L262@P6: should be avoided and if used must be maintained so they are non-absorbent and easily cleaned.
L263@P6: Garbage and recyclable matter
L264@P6: Food business operators (and event management) must ensure there are:
L265@P6: • adequate rubbish dumpsters located away from food stalls so that vermin and flying insects are
L266@P6: not attracted to food preparation areas
L267@P6-7: • adequately sized, enclosed rubbish bins at each food stall Guidelines for food businesses at temporary events
L268@P7: FA721/2509 8
L269@P7: • arrangements to dispose of garbage at the end of the day
L270@P7: • recyclable, re-useable or compostable products wherever possible.
L271@P7: Animals and pests
L272@P7: Take all practicable measures to prevent pests (including birds, spiders, wildlife and flying insects)
L273@P7: from entering the food stall or coming into contact with any fixtures, equipment or parts of vehicles
L274@P7: used to transport food.
L275@P7: Animals are not permitted to enter a food stall at any time. ‘Assistance animals’, as defined in
L276@P7: section 9 of the Disability Discrimination Act 1992, are permitted in public dining and drinking areas.
L277@P7: Assistance animals include Guide Dogs trained to help people with disability.
L278@P7: Children in food stalls
L279@P7: For health and safety reasons, children should not be allowed to enter the preparation area of a food
L280@P7: stall.
L281@P7: Camping in or near food stalls is not permitted. A designated area is usually set aside for staff
L282@P7: camping at multi-day events.
L283@P7: Water and ice
L284@P7: • Potable water (safe for human consumption) must be used for washing and preparing food, or as
L285@P7: an ingredient in food. Town water supplies are considered potable. Using water from other
L286@P7: sources may be suitable but this should be checked with the local council.
L287@P7: • Only food-grade materials should be used to store water.
L288@P7: • Ice used to keep food cool or to add to food or drink must be made from potable water and
L289@P7: treated as food. Do not break up ice bags on bare ground, instead use a food preparation sink or
L290@P7: counter.
L291@P7: Facilities
L292@P7: Facilities should be constructed to ensure a good standard of food hygiene and allow for easy
L293@P7: cleaning and maintenance (see diagrams on page 17).
L294@P7: Construction of temporary food stalls
L295@P7: Temporary food stalls include any structure set up for an occasional event such as a fete, fair,
L296@P7: market or festival, where it can be demonstrated that food safety will not be compromised.
L297@P7: Standard of construction
L298@P7: • If there are walls and a ceiling needed to protect food, ensure there is adequate ventilation if
L299@P7: using appliances connected to Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG) cylinders. This can be achieved by
L300@P7: using a fine mesh wall material.
L301@P7: • Screen the entire food premises (including barbeques and cool rooms) to reduce the risk of food
L302@P7: contamination and to restrict public access.
L303@P7: • Floors are to be in the form of a non-absorbent easily cleaned material (such as vinyl) cut larger
L304@P7: than the floor area to enable it to be turned up at the wall and clipped or fixed into position.
L305@P7: Floors in multi-day events should be raised to account for wet weather and should consider high
L306@P7: traffic areas, that is, between the stall and coolroom. The event organiser may coordinate the
L307@P7-8: construction of stalls and should consider these requirements.Guidelines for food businesses at temporary events

L309@P8: • Walls are to be non-absorbent and easily cleaned. The framework of the wall panels should
L310@P8: support the fabric taut and rigid. No part of the walls should flap in the breeze or be unsecured.
L311@P8: • The ceiling is to be of similar construction to the walls.
L312@P8: • The whole structure is to be securely fixed together when assembled and protected against

L314@P8: For pre-packaged and low-risk foods at single-day events, there may be exemptions from certain
L315@P8: construction requirements due to the reduced food safety risk. Examples of pre-packaged and lowrisk foods are pre-bottled or sealed jams, honey, pickles and drinks; pre-wrapped and sealed cakes,
L316@P8: toffees and biscuits; whole fruit, vegetables and nuts intended to be washed or peeled before
L317@P8: eating. Contact the local council to enquire about exemptions.
L318@P8: Fixtures
L319@P8: Food preparation surfaces (such as counters, shelves, stands) must be made from rigid, smooth and
L320@P8: durable material, free of cracks or joints. Timber surfaces should be painted, laminated or clear
L321@P8: finished. Shelves should be at least 150mm off the floor to avoid cross contamination and facilitate
L322@P8: effective cleaning and sanitising. Metal end sections should be sealed.
L323@P8: The preparation and display of food, including unpackaged ready-to-eat food, must be protected
L324@P8: from likely contamination from customers. Sneeze barriers or other enclosures should be
L325@P8: considered.
L326@P8: For pre-packaged and low-risk foods, there may be exemptions from certain facility requirements
L327@P8: due to the reduced food safety risk. Contact the local council for details.
L328@P8: Cleaning, sanitising and hand washing facilities
L329@P8: • A hand washing facility, separate from other facilities and used only for that purpose, must be
L330@P8: provided where it is easily and readily accessible, for example at the staff entrance to the stall.
L331@P8: Warm water is needed for effective hand washing and delivered through a single outlet to a
L332@P8: dedicated hand basin. Soap and single-use towels must be provided at the hand washing facility.
L333@P8: Bars also require hand washing facilities.
L334@P8: • A sealed container of warm potable water (minimum capacity 10 litres) with a tap and suitable
L335@P8: bowls or containers should be provided for cleaning, sanitising and hand washing. Clean singleuse towels, detergent and food-grade surface sanitiser must be available for use. For prepackaged and low-risk foods, there may be exemptions from certain facility requirements due to
L336@P8: the reduced food safety risk. You will need approval in writing from the local council if you wish
L337@P8: to be exempt from providing warm potable water for hand washing. Multi-day events will require
L338@P8: reticulated potable water supply usually provided by the event organiser.
L339@P8: • Reusable eating and drinking utensils, dinnerware and tableware must be washed and sanitised
L340@P8: in separate receptacles used only for that purpose. All food preparation utensils, equipment and
L341@P8: eating utensils (reusable and disposable) should be stored a minimum 150mm off the ground.
L342@P8: • A suitable sanitising agent must be available for sanitising food utensils and food contact
L343@P8: surfaces. Where utensils are stored in a sanitising solution between uses, the solution should be
L344@P8: changed frequently to keep it clean.
L345@P8: • At multi-day events stall holders must include a separate sink for food preparation only.
L346@P8: Waste disposal
L347@P8: Wastewater must be disposed of lawfully. Contact your local water authority prior to the event for

L355@P9: Food handling
L356@P9: The requirements for handling food for sale for human consumption are outlined in the following
L357@P9: sections of the Food Standards Code:
L358@P9: • Standard 3.2.2 – Food Safety Practices and General Requirements
L359@P9: • Standard 3.2.2A – Food Safety Management Tools
L360@P9: • Standard 3.2.3 – Food Premises and Equipment.
L361@P9: These are on the Food Standards Australia New Zealand website at www.foodstandards.gov.au.
L362@P9: Some of these requirements also apply to pre-packaged food and low-risk food. Factsheets and
L363@P9: user guides (including for charitable and community not-for-profit organisations) are available on
L364@P9: the Food Authority website at foodauthority.nsw.gov.au
L365@P9: Food suppliers
L366@P9: Obtain all food products from reputable suppliers, as generally they operate under strict quality
L367@P9: guidelines. Keep copies of invoices to trace back if needed in the future.
L368@P9: Preparing food at home
L369@P9: Notification of food business details, construction of facilities, labelling and food handling
L370@P9: requirements of the Act apply to the preparation of food for sale from a home kitchen. They also
L371@P9: apply to pre-packaged and low-risk produce and foods. Approval to use home kitchens as part of a
L372@P9: food business may be required by local councils. Contact the local council for more information. See
L373@P9: also the Food Authority’s Home-based food businesses factsheet (PDF, 141 KB).
L374@P9: Potentially hazardous foods and temperature control
L375@P9: One of the most common causes of foodborne illness is the storage and display of potentially
L376@P9: hazardous foods at inadequate temperatures for extended periods. This can lead to the rapid and
L377@P9: sustained growth of food poisoning bacteria.
L378@P9: Examples of potentially hazardous foods include:
L379@P9: • cooked meat
L380@P9: • dairy products
L381@P9: • seafood

L402@P10: Cross contamination
L403@P10: Adequate measures must be taken to prevent cross contamination from raw foods to cooked foods.
L404@P10: This includes:
L405@P10: • ensuring there are separate utensils for cooked and raw meats, poultry and seafood
L406@P10: • covering all food unless it is being processed or prepared – unprotected food must not be left
L407@P10: unattended
L408@P10: • keeping cooked meat and salads separate from raw meat, raw poultry and raw seafood
L409@P10: • washing hands after handling raw meats, raw poultry, raw seafood and raw vegetables
L410@P10: • storing food, cooking and eating utensils off the floor
L411@P10: • having appropriate cleaning equipment (mops, buckets, brooms) for multi-day events and allweather conditions
L412@P10: • changes of footwear so that in wet weather circumstances muddy shoes are not worn in the stall
L413@P10: • stall holders should consider allocating single tasks (food preparation, cleaning, serving or
L414@P10: handling money) to individuals as a strategy to reduce the potential for cross contamination
