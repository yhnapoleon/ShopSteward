L4@P0: Standard 1.2.5 of the Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code (the Code)
L5@P0: outlines how packaged foods must be marked to indicate their shelf life.
L6@P0: Packaged food s must be date marked to show their shelf life when stored according to specific
L7@P0: storage instructions. Manufacturer s are usually responsible for determining date mark s and
L8@P0: proving they are accurate. They must also list any conditions that need to be follow ed to ensure
L9@P0: the food will keep for the period indicated, such as ‘refrigerate after opening’.
L10@P0: Where directions for use are required for health and safety reasons, such as cooking instructions,
L11@P0: the y must also be included on the label.
L12@P0: Use by date
L13@P0: A ‘use by’ is the date after which food in an intact package , stored as specified on the label,
L14@P0: should not be eaten for health and safety reasons. It is illegal to sell food past its ‘use by’ date.
L15@P0: Best before date
L16@P0: Foods marked with a ‘best before’ date may be safe to eat after that date , as long as it is not
L17@P0: damaged, deteriorated or perished . If stored as directed in an unopened package, the food can be
L18@P0: expected to retain its colour, taste, texture and flavour up to the best before date .
L19@P0: Food marked with a ‘best before’ date can be sold after that date if fit for human consumption .
L20@P0: When a date mark is not required :
L21@P0: • when the ‘best before’ date is 2 years or more
L22@P0: • individual portion s of ice cream or ice confection
L23@P0: • foods contained in a small package ( less than 100 square centimetres), except where the food
L24@P0: should have a use by date
L25@P0: • foods exempt from the general labelling provisions of the Code (see factsheet Labelling –
