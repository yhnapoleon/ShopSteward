L0@P0: Food Authority
L1@P0: A food handler is anyone who works in a food business and handles food, or
L2@P0: surfaces that are likely to come into contact with food, such as cutlery and
L3@P0: plates.
L4@P0: A food handler may be involved in food preparation, production, cooking,
L5@P0: display, packing, storage or service.
L6@P0: Responsibilities of food handlers
L7@P0: Under the Food Standards Code, a food handler must take all reasonable measures not to handle
L8@P0: food or food surfaces in a way that is likely to compromise the safety and suitability of food.
L9@P0: Food handlers also have specific responsibilities relating to health and hygiene.
L10@P0: Health requirements
L11@P0: Any food handler with symptoms or a diagnosis of an illness (such as vomiting, diarrhoea or fever)
L12@P0: must:
L13@P0: • report that they are ill to their employer or supervisor
L14@P0: • not handle food if there is a reasonable likelihood of food contamination as a result of the illness
L15@P0: • if continuing to engage in other work on the food premises, take all practicable measures to
L16@P0: prevent food from being contaminated
L17@P0: • notify a supervisor if they know or suspect they may have contaminated food.
L18@P0: Effective hand washing
L19@P0: Hand washing is one of the most important actions you can take to prevent foodborne illness.
L20@P0: Food handlers must:
L21@P0: • wash their hands using hot, soapy water and dry them thoroughly with single-use paper towels
L22@P0: • wash their hands whenever they are likely to be a source of contamination (after using the toilet,
L23@P0: smoking, coughing, sneezing, using a handkerchief, eating, drinking or touching the hair, scalp or
L24@P0: body)
L25@P0: • wash their hands before handling ready-to-eat food and after handling raw food.
L26@P0: Hygiene requirements
L27@P0: Food handlers must:
L28@P0: • not eat, sneeze, blow, cough, spit or smoke around food or food surfaces
L29@P0: • take all practicable measures to prevent unnecessary contact with ready-to-eat food

L38@P1: Use of gloves
L39@P1: The Food Standards Code does not require food handlers to use gloves. Even when wearing gloves,
L40@P1: in many situations it may be preferable to use utensils such as tongs or spoons.
L41@P1: Gloves must be removed, discarded and replaced with a new pair in the below circumstances:
L42@P1: • before handling food
L43@P1: • before handling ready-to-eat food and after handling raw food
L44@P1: • after using the toilet, smoking, coughing, sneezing, using a handkerchief, eating, drinking or
L45@P1: touching the hair, scalp or body.
L46@P1: Employer responsibilities
L47@P1: A food business must:
L48@P1: • ensure food handlers do not handle food if there is a possibility of contamination
L49@P1: • maintain easily accessible handwashing facilities and supplies of hot running water, soap and
L50@P1: single-use paper towels
L51@P1: • ensure all food handlers have appropriate skills and knowledge in food safety and food hygiene.
L52@P1: This can be done by either completing a food handler training course, or being able to clearly
L53@P1: demonstrate their skills and knowledge.
L54@P1: Food Safety Supervisor
L55@P1: Under the Food Standards Code (Standard 3.2.2) all food handlers must have general skills and
L56@P1: knowledge in food safety and hygiene. The Code also requires that certain businesses in the
L57@P1: hospitality and retail food service sector appoint at least one trained Food Safety Supervisor (FSS)
L58@P1: who can advise and supervise food handlers.
L59@P1: FSS training is tied to nationally recognised units of competency in the Vocational Education and
L60@P1: Training (VET) System.
L61@P1: For more information how to become a Food Safety Supervisor, including training requirements, visit
L62@P1: foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/fss
