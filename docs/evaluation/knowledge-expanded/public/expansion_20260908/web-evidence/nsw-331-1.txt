L43@P2: Introduction
L44@P2: In Australia, foodborne illness caused by Salmonella has significantly increased over the past 20 years
L45@P2: and more than seventy percent of salmonellosis is thought to be due to contaminated food (Davis et al.,
L46@P2: 2022; Ford et al, 2016). Mishandling of eggs and egg-based products is a significant contributor to
L47@P2: foodborne illness in Australia, and in NSW.
L48@P2: Salmonella is known to be present on the surfaces of eggs and improper handling can lead to raw egg
L49@P2: products becoming contaminated.
L50@P2: There are a number of factors that contribute to the strong association between foodborne illness
L51@P2: outbreaks caused by Salmonella and food that is sold which contains raw egg. These include:
L52@P2: • Contamination of egg contents by Salmonella from the shell,
L53@P2: • Failure to process raw egg foods sufficiently (through acidification or heat) to remove any Salmonella
L54@P2: risk,
L55@P2: • Failure to clean and sanitise equipment and food contact surfaces,
L56@P2: • Temperature abuse of products (i.e. storage above 5°C), and
L57@P2: • Keeping raw egg products beyond the recommended storage life (maximum 24 hours) at refrigerated
L58@P2: temperature.
L59@P2: The practice of pooling eggs to produce raw egg foods significantly increases the likelihood of
L60@P2: Salmonella contamination of a product which does not receive any further cooking or treatment to
L61@P2: eliminate harmful bacteria.
L62@P2: Many large outbreaks of Salmonella food poisoning linked to raw egg foods have occurred in NSW and
L63@P2: nationally, particularly where business hygiene and temperature control issues were apparent.
L64@P2: Raw egg products that are most commonly implicated in Salmonella food poisoning include:
L65@P2: • Sauces and spreads made with raw egg – for example mayonnaise, aioli, egg butter.
L66@P2: • Desserts made without an effective cook step – for example tiramisu, mousse, fried ice cream.
L67@P2: • Drinks containing raw egg – for example egg flip, raw egg high protein smoothies.
L68@P2: The easiest solution to reduce the risk of a retail food business being implicated in a foodborne illness
L69@P2: outbreak caused by Salmonella is to avoid selling food containing raw egg. If a food business decides to
L70@P2: prepare and sell raw egg products, the business chooses to accept the inherent food safety risk and is
L71@P2: responsible for effectively managing the risk and producing safe food.
L72@P2: All food business should be able to identify and control food safety risks to ensure that they can meet the
L73@P2: requirements of the Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code (Food Standards Code). In order to
L74@P2: protect customers from the risk of foodborne illness, businesses must comply with Standard 3.2.2,
L75@P2-3: Division 3, Clause 7 (see below) to ensure that only safe and suitable food is processed.Food Safety Guidelines for the Preparation of Raw and Lightly Cooked Egg Products

L77@P3: 7 Food processing
L78@P3: 1. A food business must –
L79@P3: (a) take all practicable measures to process only safe and suitable food; and
L80@P3: (b) when processing food –
L81@P3: (i) take all necessary steps to prevent the likelihood of food being contaminated; and
L82@P3: (ii) where a process step is needed to reduce to safe levels any pathogens that may be
L83@P3: present in the food – use a process step that is reasonably known to achieve the
L84@P3: microbiological safety of the food.
L85@P3: 2. A food business must, when processing potentially hazardous food that is not
L86@P3: undergoing a pathogen control step, ensure that the time the food remains at
L87@P3: temperatures that permit the growth of infectious or toxigenic microorganisms in the food
L88@P3: is minimised.
L89@P3: This is particularly important for food businesses such as restaurants, cafés, bakeries and caterers that
L90@P3: prepare and sell food containing raw egg.
L91@P3: Understanding the risk in the preparation of raw egg products, the NSW Food Authority has developed
L92@P3: these guidelines to assist businesses to undertake practices that will ensure that they comply with food
L93@P3: safety requirements when making products with a known history of causing salmonellosis.
L94@P3: The following guidance is recommended:
L95@P3: 1. Use safer alternatives to raw eggs in foods which are not cooked. Alternatives include commercially
L96@P3: produced dressings and sauces, or pasteurised egg products.
L97@P3: 2. If using a raw egg product is the only option, then all of the following controls must be in place:
L98@P3: a. Egg receival – only buy eggs from reputable suppliers with appropriate delivery and storage
L99@P3: conditions. No cracked, dirty or unstamped eggs,
L100@P3: b. Storage – correct storage and display of ingredients and products, including proper temperature
L101@P3: control,
L102@P3: c. Processing – correct handling such as good personal hygiene (including good hand washing
L103@P3: practices and proper use of gloves if used), use of a sanitised egg separator, and proper
L104@P3: temperature control,
L105@P3: d. Premises – clean premises, fixtures, fittings and equipment, compliant hand wash facility,
L106@P3: sanitised equipment (including egg separator) and food contact surfaces,
L107@P3: AND for foods containing raw egg, with a history of causing salmonellosis or of a very similar
L108@P3: nature to those foods, steps e) and f) below:
L109@P3: e. The product is to be acidified to a pH of 4.2 (or less) or effectively heat treated.
L110@P3: Foods containing raw eggs must be acidified to a pH of 4.2 (or less) through the addition of acidic
L111@P3: ingredients such as lemon juice or vinegar
L112@P3: OR
L113@P3: Receive effective heat treatment such as sous vide or pasteurisation to eliminate Salmonella, if present.
L114@P3: f. The treated raw egg product should be stored at or below 5˚C for no longer than 24 hours and
L115@P3: should be discarded at the end of the day. A fresh batch should be made daily.
L116@P3: Note:
L117@P3: Under the Food Standards Code Standard 3.2.2 Division 3, to prevent disease causing bacteria from
L118@P3: growing or producing toxins, the temperature of products must be either at or below 5˚C during transport,
L119@P3: storage and display. The Food Standards Code allows for alternative compliance methods provided the
L120@P3: businesses can demonstrate the product’s safety (Standard 3.2.2, Clause 25).
L121@P3: The ‘2-hour/4-hour rule’ is used by Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ) as an example of
L122@P3: an alternative method for compliance (see page 14). If a business uses the ‘2-hour/4-hour rule’, then a
L123@P3-4: documented system must be in place to demonstrate evidence that it is being used effectively.Food Safety Guidelines for the Preparation of Raw and Lightly Cooked Egg Products

L125@P4: Purpose and scope
L126@P4: The purpose of these guidelines is to give food retail businesses that sell food containing raw egg
L127@P4: specific safety steps for its preparation and clear guidance and advice on how to meet food safety
L128@P4: regulations. These businesses are strongly advised to follow this guidance document.
L129@P4: The document covers areas from receipt of eggs through to preparation of raw egg products. These
L130@P4: areas can all potentially affect the safety of the product. It also includes an example of a monitoring
L131@P4: system for use when acidifying raw egg products.
L132@P4: This document applies to the foods that contain raw or lightly cooked egg known to have caused cases
L133@P4: of salmonellosis, including:
L134@P4: • Sauces and spreads such as mayonnaise, aioli and egg butter
L135@P4: • Desserts such as tiramisu, mousse and fried ice cream
L136@P4: • Drinks such as shakes and smoothies.
L137@P4: Recommendations 2(e) and 2(f) (on page 5) as laid out in this guideline do not apply to certain products
L138@P4: that contain raw or lightly cooked egg but have little or no history of causing salmonellosis due to their
L139@P4: traditional method of preparation, use or storage including:
L140@P4: • cooked egg sauces for example hollandaise and béarnaise sauce
L141@P4: • cooked breakfast style eggs such as scrambled or poached eggs and omelettes
L142@P4: • cakes and soufflés (baked)
L143@P4: • meringues (oven baked)
L144@P4: • icing (high sugar content)
L145@P4: • marshmallows (boiled during preparation)
L146@P4: • frozen desserts such as ice cream or frozen mousse (frozen immediately after preparation)
L147@P4: • traditional dishes that incorporate a raw egg added when serving such as tartare, congee, and
L148@P4: soups.
L149@P4: All food businesses must meet the requirements of the Food Standards Code to ensure they follow safe
L150@P4: food handling practices. This document does not cover all requirements of the Food Standards Code, in
L151@P4: particular requirements relating to premises and equipment. Business operators must read the Food
L152@P4: Standards Code and ensure they comply with all aspects of the Food Standards Code as it relates to
L153@P4: their business.

L258@P8: Receiving and storing eggs & other food
L259@P8: Food safety begins by ensuring only safe and suitable ingredients are purchased from a supplier and
L260@P8: that these items are stored correctly. The following information is provided to assist meeting the
L261@P8: requirements of the Food Standards Code regarding the receipt and storage of food.
L262@P8: Receiving eggs
L263@P8: • Only accept eggs that are:
L264@P8: — clean,
L265@P8: — free from visible cracks and not leaking,
L266@P8: — stamped,
L267@P8: — supplied in clean packaging, and
L268@P8: — correctly labelled (i.e. with the supplier’s name and address, date marking).
L269@P8: • Check the eggs when they arrive and always reject eggs that are broken, cracked or dirty, or that are
L270@P8: supplied in dirty, defective, re-used or improperly labelled packaging. Accepting eggs in poor
L271@P8: condition places your business at risk.
L272@P8: Supplier and food information
L273@P8: • A list of food suppliers should be maintained in case they need to be contacted.
L274@P8: • For all foods, the label or receipt needs to contain details of suppliers’ names and addresses. Phone
L275@P8: contact details should also be kept.
L276@P8: • Items that do not meet the above receiving and information requirements should be returned to the
L277@P8: supplier.
L278@P8: Storage of whole eggs
L279@P8: • Store whole eggs (egg in shell) in a refrigerator or cool room.
L280@P8: • Avoid temperature fluctuations and only take out what is required for service.
L281@P8: • Don’t store fresh egg pulp that has been collected (pooled) in a container.
L282@P8: • Ensure proper stock rotation so that the oldest stock is used first (as long as they are still within the
L283@P8: best before or use-by-date).
L284@P8: • Discard any cartons that contain spilt raw egg to avoid contamination.
L285@P8: • Eggs should not be re-packed into cartons with labels that do not correctly reflect the origin, weight or
L286@P8: egg type.
L287@P8: Other foods
L288@P8: Potentially hazardous foods must be stored under refrigeration at or below 5°C. This includes meat,
L289@P8: chicken, seafood, dairy products and eggs.
L290@P8: • Only receive foods that are within their ‘Use-by’ date or ‘Best Before’ date.
L291@P8: • Only receive potentially hazardous food that has been transported under temperature control.
L292@P8: • Once received, all potentially hazardous foods must be placed under refrigerated storage at or below
L293@P8: 5°C.
L294@P8: • Refrigerated raw ingredients must be stored separately from ready-to-eat foods and ingredients.
L295@P8: • Raw foods such as uncooked chicken and meat must not be placed above ready-to-eat foods in the

L303@P9: Egg handling practices – practical steps to control Salmonella
L304@P9: Preparing foods can involve a great deal of handling of both raw and cooked foods. Because raw egg
L305@P9: products are eaten without any further cooking it is important that they are prepared correctly and safely.
L306@P9: Raw foods can contain bacteria and if the food is not handled correctly, the numbers of bacteria can
L307@P9: grow. Poor handling of cooked foods can result in cross-contamination from raw foods and if not stored
L308@P9: correctly, the number of bacteria can also grow.
L309@P9: Below are some main points to assist in proper preparation of foods, particularly raw egg products, and
L310@P9: to help avoid cross-contamination. For further information, refer to Standard 3.2.2 of the Food Standards
L311@P9: Code.
L312@P9: Premises
L313@P9: The cleanliness of the premises, fixtures and fittings assist in minimising cross contamination. In
L314@P9: particular it is important to have an acceptable hand washing facility that includes:
L315@P9: — warm running water,
L316@P9: — soap,
L317@P9: — single-use hand towels, for example paper towels.
L318@P9: Equipment, utensils, and food contact surfaces
L319@P9: • All equipment and utensils must be in good condition and able to be easily cleaned and sanitised.
L320@P9: • Mechanical equipment, such as blenders and food processors, should be able to be easily taken
L321@P9: apart for cleaning and sanitising. If they cannot be taken apart, equipment like stick blenders may not
L322@P9: be appropriate for processing raw eggs.
L323@P9: • Food contact surfaces such as equipment, benches and utensils must be cleaned and sanitised
L324@P9: before use.
L325@P9: • Boards and utensils must be cleaned and sanitised between preparing different foods, especially
L326@P9: when preparing foods that will not be further cooked (for example raw egg products).
L327@P9: • Use a sanitised egg separator to separate egg yolk from egg whites. Eggs must not be separated
L328@P9: using their shells as the shells may be contaminated with Salmonella on the outside surface.
L329@P9: Personal Hygiene
L330@P9: All food handlers must take all reasonable measures not to handle food or surfaces likely to come into
L331@P9: contact with food in a way that is likely to compromise the safety and suitability of food.
L332@P9: Food handlers must ensure they follow good personal hygiene practices:
L333@P9: • People who are sick with vomiting, diarrhoea or fever must not prepare or serve food.
L334@P9: • Take all practicable measures to prevent unnecessary contact with ready-to-eat food.
L335@P9: • Wash hands prior to preparing food and after handling non-food articles, using the toilet, smoking,
L336@P9: drinking, eating and touching hair, scalp or body.
L337@P9-10: • Wash hands between handling of raw ingredients and ready-to-eat foods.Food Safety Guidelines for the Preparation of Raw and Lightly Cooked Egg Products

L339@P10: Use of disposable gloves
L340@P10: It is not mandatory for food handlers to use disposable gloves, although if used correctly they can assist
L341@P10: with minimising contamination.
L342@P10: When using disposable gloves, they must be:
L343@P10: • Only used for one continuous task and then discarded.
L344@P10: • Regularly changed to avoid cross-contamination – this is especially the case when changing from
L345@P10: preparing raw ingredients to handling ready-to-eat foods.
L346@P10: • Always discarded and not kept for later use once taken off.
L347@P10: • Removed and discarded before using the toilet, smoking, eating, drinking or touching the hair, scalp
L348@P10: or body.
L349@P10: Temperature control
L350@P10: • Temperature control throughout the operation is critical in minimising microbial growth, for example
L351@P10: raw egg product must be at or below 5˚C. This includes all operations during receipt, processing,
L352@P10: storage and display.
L353@P10: • If a raw egg product is out of temperature control (i.e. not at or below 5˚C), there must be
L354@P10: documented evidence that the ‘2-hour/4-hour’ rule is being met.
L355@P10: 2-hour/4-hour rule
L356@P10: When potentially hazardous ready-to-eat food has been kept between 5˚C and 60˚C for:
L357@P10: up to 2 hours, it can be refrigerated below 5˚C, or kept hot above 60˚C, or used
L358@P10: immediately,
L359@P10: between 2 and 4 hours, it must be used immediately, or
L360@P10: up to a total of 4 hours or more, it must be thrown out.
L361@P10: Safe Food Australia – Appendix 2 (2016)
L362@P10: For a more detailed information on the 2-hour/4-hour rule, refer to the ‘Guidance on the 2-hour/4-hour
L363@P10: rule’ document on the NSW Food Authority’s website www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/resource-centre.
L364@P10: Temperature measuring device
L365@P10: • Businesses handling potentially hazardous foods must have a temperature measuring device.
L366@P10: • Thermometers must be easily accessible and able to accurately measure temperatures to +/- 1°C.
L367@P10: • Thermometers should be calibrated to ensure accuracy.
L368@P10: • Appendix 4 provides information on calibrating thermometers.
L369@P10: Handling of eggs
L370@P10: Because eggs can be contaminated with Salmonella on the surface, the handling of eggs is critical.
L371@P10: When handling eggs, follow these guidelines:
L372@P10: • Do not use dirty, cracked or unstamped eggs.
L373@P10: • Do not wash eggs. Washing makes them more susceptible to contamination. Discard dirty and
L374@P10: cracked eggs.
L375@P10: • Visually inspect eggs before use to ensure there are no hairline cracks.
