L311@P12: Sizes
L312@P12: Consumers are often given the option to choose from several sizes of a food with different price s,
L313@P12: for example small, medium, large . Sizes are still standardised for portion and content, so each size is
L314@P12: a standard food item. A standard food item that displays size options must include the kJ value for
L315@P12: each size.
L316@P12: Examples include servings of fries, salads, or drinks with small, medium and large options; each of
L317@P12: which need s to display its own nutritional information.

L319@P12: Multi -serve items
L320@P12: Food sold unpackaged, but intended for multiple portion serves , such as whole cakes, pizzas and
L321@P12: loaves of fruit bread , are standard food item s for Fast Choices if they are sold by a business that is a
L322@P12: standard food outlet .
L323@P12: To reduce the risk of confusion for customers, where a product is offered as a series of sizes ( for
L324@P12: example, small, medium or large) and some of those sizes may be intended for multiple serves ( for
L325@P12: example, extra -large chips, whole pizzas or whole cakes), the product should display the total kJ
L326@P12: value and not include a per -serve calculation. The business may inform customers how many
L327@P12: serves/slices the entire product provides.

L330@P13: Combination meals
L331@P13: Many businesses sell standard food items as a grouped selection. These ‘meal deals’ are considered
L332@P13: standard food items and should be displayed with the total kJ content of all products in the
L333@P13: combination for the default selection. This includes any prepackaged food item , such as a can or
L334@P13: bottle of drink.
L335@P13: For example :
L336@P13: • a burger meal with a cheeseburger, fries and a milkshake – the total kJ value should be
L337@P13: calculated and displayed .
L338@P13: • a salmon bento box with 2 salmon sushi rolls, 5 slices of salmon , a nigiri and a soy sauce sachet –
L339@P13: the total kJ value of all items should be calculated and displayed.
L340@P13: A combination add -on is also a standard food item and should display kJ values where the default
L341@P13: items in the add -on are known.
L342@P13: A ‘coffee and cake’ deal, where the customer must choose a coffee style and a cake selection is not
L343@P13: a combination, it is considered a category , as there is no default coffee and cake to calculate the kJ .

L346@P14: Categories
L347@P14: Some businesses display a range of similar standard food items for a single price (for example,
L348@P14: different flavours of ice cream) .
L349@P14: These may be items sold per serve, like ice cream or cakes with a variety of flavours, or standard
L350@P14: food items that are designed by the customer at the point of sale, like burritos or other made -toorder foods.
L351@P14: The requirement to display nutritional information is dependent on how the category information is
L352@P14: provided. For example:
L353@P14: • If the items are listed individually, either on display tags or menu boards, nutritional information
L354@P14: must be displayed for each flavour or ingredient.
L355@P14: • If a menu board lists a price for a category ( for example, 1 scoop or 2 scoops of ice cream)
L356@P14: without listing the flavours or variations, no nutritional information is required on the price board,
L357@P14: but nutritional information must be displayed with each flavour or variation in the cabinet .
L358@P14: • For a ‘coffee and cake ’ poster , where the customer must choose a coffee style and a cake from
L359@P14: the display , nutritional information is not required on the poster, but nutritional information must
L360@P14: be displayed on the menu boards and display tag s.
L361@P14: An outlet may have both types of menus, so businesses must display nutritional information on the
L362@P14: menus that meet the requirements, even though the price board may not need to display nutritional
L363@P14: information.

L370@P16: Customisable food
L371@P16: Customisable food items are not considered standard food items. These products are displayed with
L372@P16: the intent that customers will select most , or all , of the ingredients as part of the ordering process.
L373@P16: Customisable food items include build -your -own pizzas, sandwiches , burritos and wraps made by
L374@P16: the customer selecting individual ingredients from a display or list of options. These types of food do
L375@P16: not require nutritional information labelling as there is no ‘standard food item’ to use as the default .
L376@P16: Th is is different to modification of a ‘standard food item ’ by a customer (for allergen management or
L377@P16: preference) where an ingredient is added or subtracted from a standard food item sold by a
L378@P16: standard food outlet. The standard food item requires nutritional information labelling , even though
L379@P16: it may be sold in a modified form . Nutritional information labelling is not required for the modified
L380@P16-17: food .Fast Choice s progra m: nutritional information menu labelling

L385@P17: Sale by weight
L386@P17: Unpackaged products sold by weight (per kilogram, per 100g) are not standardised for portion size
L387@P17: as the consumer can purchase any quantity of the product . These products are not required to
L388@P17: display nutritional information .

L390@P17: salads or hot chicken portions in kilojoules per 100g if this is a more suitable method to display
L391@P17: nutritional information. They may also display kJ for the entire item sold if this is desired.

L403@P19: Standard food outlets
L404@P19: Food businesses that meet the following criteria must comply with nutritional information menu
L405@P19: labelling:
L406@P19: • the business operates as part of a chain of food businesses that share a consistent or uniform
L407@P19: menu that includes standard food items, and
L408@P19: • the standard food items include RTE , takeaway products, and
L409@P19: • trade under a shared trading name and branding, and
L410@P19: • the trading name and menu is used in 20 or more outlets in NSW, or in 50 or more outlets
L411@P19: nationally.
L412@P19: The legislation requires individual outlet proprietors to comply with nutritional information display
L413@P19: requirements .
L414@P19: For businesses with less than 20 outlets in NSW, but 50 or more nationally, all outlets in NSW are a
L415@P19: standard food outlet and must comply with the NSW legislation .
L416@P19: Standard food outlets include a broad range of food, from burgers and pizza to café s offering
L417@P19: coffee and pastries, to ice creameries, sandwich shops, sushi, salad – any outlet selling RTE food
L418@P19: that is standardised for portion size and content and is sold unpackaged.
L419@P19: A non -exhaustive list of the types of standard food outlets include:
L420@P19: • quick service restaurants
L421@P19: • shopping centre food court outlets
L422@P19: • pizza chains
L423@P19: • hot and cold beverage chains
L424@P19: • bakery chains
L425@P19: • ice cream chains
L426@P19: • doughnut chains
L427@P19: • salad chains
L428@P19: • sushi chains
L429@P19: • sandwich chains
L430@P19: • café chains
L431@P19: • supermarkets .
L432@P19: Dine -in only premises
L433@P19: Restaurants that only provide a dine -in service and do not offer takeaway food are not in -scope of
L434@P19: the Fast Choices program scope and do not need to display kJ . If a business provides a container for
L435@P19: diners to take home leftover food from a dine -in restaurant experience , this does not bring the
L436@P19: business into scope . L eftover food from a dine -in only restaurant is not a standard food item.
L437@P19: However if one of these businesses voluntarily chooses to implement Fast Choices, it must be
L438@P19: implemented in a manner con sistent with section 106O of the Food Act 2003 .
L439@P19: The expansion of online menus and ordering with takeaway or home delivery options , including
L440@P19: third -party ordering platforms and third -party delivery drivers, has brought some dine -in restaurants
L441@P19: into scope of the Fast Choices program.
L442@P19: Dine -in restaurant chains with third -party app takeaway and delivery options should determine their
L443@P19-20: number of outlets, and if required , update menus and apps to include nutritional information.Fast Choice s progra m: nutritional information menu labelling

L448@P20: • bread
L449@P20: • breakfast cereal
L450@P20: • butter

L452@P20: • fresh fruit and vegetables
L453@P20: • fresh milk
L454@P20: • meat
L455@P20: • rice

L459@P20: Display of the average kJ content
L460@P20: Supermarkets may display the average kJ content for the whole standard food item or per 100g.
L461@P20: Products captured
L462@P20: If a supermarket does not sell standard food items, they do not need to comply with the legislation.
L463@P20: Examples of supermarket standard food items captured by Fast Choices legislation include:
L464@P20: • portioned hot chickens ( for example, whole, halves, quarters) sold without a NIP
L465@P20: • RTE hot deli meals and snacks ( for example, lasagne, potatoes, sausage rolls, nuggets)
L466@P20: • deli salads sold by size ( for example, small, medium, large)
L467@P20: • bakery items packed and sold without a NIP ( for example , cheese and bacon rolls, hot cross
L468@P20: buns, custard tarts, doughnuts, muffins, cakes ).
L469@P20: Examples of supermarket products excluded from Fast Choices legislation include:
L470@P20: • packaged hot chicken, sandwiches, sushi and salads sold with a NIP
L471@P20: • cold meals and snacks that are not RTE (for example , non-heated pies, sausage rolls, curries,
L472@P20: hash browns, nuggets, quiches)
L473@P20: • packaged grocery, dairy and bakery items sold with a NIP
L474@P20: • fresh meat, raw seafood, fruit and vegetables, and nuts
L475@P20: • packaged bread rolls and loaves of bread with a NIP
L476@P20: • items sold by weight or with variable weight ( for example , deli meats, seafood)
L477@P20: • locally sourced ‘cottage industry’ food ( for example , home -made lasagne) sold in 5 or less
L478@P20: stores.

L482@P21: Businesses exempt from Fast Choices
L483@P21: Not all business es that meet the criteria for a standard food outlet are captured. The legis lation
L484@P21: exempt s some businesses from display ing kJ due to the nature of the food products they sell, or the
L485@P21: way the food products are sold .
L486@P21: The businesses exempt by the legislation are:
L487@P21: • convenience stores ( that is, not a supermarket)
L488@P21: • service stations selling fuel for motor vehicles
L489@P21: • food businesses that primarily provide food catering services
L490@P21: • food businesses that only sell food for consumption on the premises at which it is sold ( for
L491@P21: example , dine -in only restaurants and cafés)
L492@P21: • food sold by retail at a healthcare facility
L493@P21: • vending machines selling prepackaged food labelled with a NIP .
L494@P21: Voluntary display of nutritional information
L495@P21: Food businesses may voluntarily display nutritional information.
L496@P21: If a food business choose s to display nutritional information, it must be displayed on all standard
L497@P21: food items . The kJ content must be:
L498@P21: • legible and displayed as ‘kJ’
L499@P21: • adjacent to, or near the item so as to be clearly associated with the item
L500@P21: • in the same font, and at least the same font size as the price of the standard food item, or the
L501@P21: same font size as the name of the item if no price is displayed .
L502@P21: Penalties apply for standard food outlets that voluntarily display this information without complying
L503@P21: with the full requirements of the legislation.

L544@P24: What is a menu?
L545@P24: A menu is any display that identifies the available food items for the consumer to choose from when
L546@P24: purchasing at a point of sale.
L547@P24: A menu is:
L548@P24: • physically printed or a digital display
L549@P24: • any display that shows the name or price of a standard food item. It may or may not include a
L550@P24: picture or an ingredient list.
L551@P24: • visible to prospective consumers at the point of sale. This means all advertising at an outlet that
L552@P24: shows standard food items with name or price of the item is captured, as well as item and price
L553@P24: tags for products on display, and mounted boards – whether overhead, on walls, counters, or
L554@P24: mobile /digital frames.
L555@P24: • digital advertising that directs potential customers to a point of sale through embedded
L556@P24: hyperlinks . The digital advertising itself may not be a menu, however if the hyperlink enables
L557@P24: food ordering – nutritional information must be displayed on the menu accessed through the link.
L558@P24: • self -serve displays and kiosks, and apps or webpages accessed via QR code. The QR code i s not
L559@P24: a menu, however if the QR code leads to a platform where a customer can order food from a
L560@P24: standard food outlet – nutritional information must be displayed on the menu accessed through
L561@P24: the QR code.

L598@P28: Meaning of ‘legible’
L599@P28: The nutritional information must be displayed so that it can be easily read by customers.
L600@P28: The legislation requires that it is in the same font, and at least the same size as the price and name
L601@P28: of the item . The font colour may be different to the price and name of the corresponding standard
L602@P28: food item . The Food Authority has determined that the colour of the nutritional information should
L603@P28: be contrasted with the background colour and not be less than 70% opacity.
L604@P28: Note: Queensland legislation requires that kJ are displayed in the same colour as the price. Food
L605@P28: businesses operating with more than 50 outlets should confirm they comply with other states’
L606@P28: legislation. The NSW Food Authority is supportive of businesses who choose to make the kJ
L607@P28: information displayed in NSW stores the same colour, size and font a s the price and name of the
L608@P28: standard food item so that they only have to implement one system across Australia.
L609@P28: Vision Australia provide s colour accessibility tools and resources to check colour functionality and
L610@P28: avoid colour barriers . Visit : Colour accessibility tools and resources | Vision Australia. Blindness and
L611@P28: low vision services (visionaustralia.org/business -consulting/digital -access/resources/colour -

L678@P34: Kilojoule information
L679@P34: Kilojoule (kJ) is a unit of measurement for the energy content in food. For Fast Choices, energy

L682@P34: How to display the average kJ content
L683@P34: When displaying the average kJ content for a standard food item it must be:
L684@P34: • legible
L685@P34: • expressed as ‘kJ’
L686@P34: • in the same font, and at least the same font size, as the price displayed for the item ; or if no price
L687@P34: is displayed, as the name of the item
L688@P34: • adjacent to or near the name or price of the item.
L689@P34: If standard food items are displayed on the menu and a tag, the average kJ content needs to be
L690@P34: displayed on both the menu and the tag.
L691@P34: Where the numerals in the price vary in size, the font size used for the kJ content must be at least
L692@P34: the same size as the largest numeral in the price.
L693@P34: Calculating the average kJ content
L694@P34: The legislation requires the average kJ content for each standard food item to be determined using
L695@P34: Standard 1.2.8 and section S11 -2 of Schedule 11 of the Food Standards Code.
L696@P34: The average kJ content can be determined by calculating the average kJ content of the whole item
L697@P34: or per 100g. If the value must be displayed for the whole item, a business must make the necessary
L698@P34: adjustments to ensure that the calculation is done in relati on to the whole item.
L699@P34: There are numerous ways to calculate the average kJ content. These include:
L700@P34: • online Nutrition Panel Calculator developed by Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ)
L701@P34: www.foodstandards.gov.au/business/labelling/nutrition -panel -calculator
L702@P34: • nutritional analysis software
L703@P34: • laboratory analysis
L704@P34: • food composition tables and databases.
L705@P34: The legislation allows the kJ content to be rounded to the nearest 10 kJ.
