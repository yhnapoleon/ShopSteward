L176@P7: Receiving and storing foods
L177@P7: The safety of food begins by ensuring only safe and suitable ingredients are purchased from
L178@P7: suppliers and are stored correctly. Below is some information to assist in complying with the
L179@P7: requirements of the Code relating to the receipt and storage of foods.
L180@P7: Supplier and food information
L181@P7: • Source all ingredients from reputable suppliers.
L182@P7: • Maintain a list of food suppliers in case they need to be contacted. An example of a record sheet
L183@P7: for listing suppliers is provided in Appendix 4.
L184@P7: • For all foods, the label or receipt must contain details of suppliers’ names and addresses. Phone
L185@P7: contact details should also be kept.
L186@P7: • Only receive foods that are within their use -by date or best -before date.
L187@P7: • Inspect the delivery – any items that do not meet receipt requirements should be returned to
L188@P7: supplier.
L189@P7: • Check the stock regularly for date markings, rotate and prioritise stock for sale or disposal
L190@P7: ensuring older food is used first and food is not used /sold beyond its use -by date.
L191@P7: Ready -made sushi
L192@P7: • Only receive sushi that has been transported in refrigerated vehicles.
L193@P7: • Always check the temperature of sushi for each batch received. The temperature must be at 5°C
L194@P7: or below .
L195@P7: • Check the label or paperwork for time and date when sushi was made.
L196@P7: • Once received, sushi must be:
L197@P7: — Kept refrigerated at 5°C or below , or
L198@P7: — Placed on retail display immediately.
L199@P7: • Sushi must be covered during receipt and storage to protect against contamination.
L200@P7: Acidified rice
L201@P7: • Only receive acidified rice that is labelled with the date and time it was made.
L202@P7: • Check the pH of the rice to ensure it has been properly acidified. pH of acidified rice must be at
L203@P7: 4.6 or below (see Appendix 1 and 2).
L204@P7: • Reject the rice if the pH is higher than 4.6 or it was made more than 8 hours ago.
L205@P7: • Record the pH result and the date and time the rice was made on a record sheet. An example of
L206@P7: a record sheet for acidified rice is provided in Appendix 4.
L207@P7: • Only acidified rice can be stored at room temperature . It must be used on the day it was made –
L208@P7: after this, it must be discarded.
L209@P7-8: • Cover acidified rice when not in use to protect against contamination.Food safety g uideline s for the preparation and display of sushi

L211@P8: Potentially hazardous raw ingredients
L212@P8: Potentially hazardous foods and raw ingredients must be stored refrigerated. This includes meat,
L213@P8: chicken, seafood, non -acidified cooked rice and dairy products.
L214@P8: • Only receive potentially hazardous food that have been transported under temperature control.
L215@P8: • Once received, refrigerate all potentially hazardous foods immediately (at 5°C or below ).
L216@P8: • Do not overfill the refrigerator .
L217@P8: • Allow chilled air to circulate around all food items.
L218@P8: • Only use the freshest, suitable to be eaten raw fish in raw fish sushi.
L219@P8: • Store refrigerated raw ingredients separately from sushi and other ready -to-eat foods and
L220@P8: ingredients such as tuna or salmon to be eaten raw.
L221@P8: • Do not store raw foods such as uncooked chicken and meat above ready -to-eat foods in the
L222@P8: refrigerator. This is to prevent the raw juices from dripping onto foods below .
L223@P8: • Cover refrigerated ingredients during receipt and storage to protect against contamination.
L224@P8: Frozen foods
L225@P8: • Only receive frozen food that is in a frozen state.
L226@P8: • If foods are to be kept frozen, place them in a freezer immediately.
L227@P8: • Ensure that frozen food is adequately covered, and the package is intact.
L228@P8: • Label foods stored in freezers with the ‘date of freezing’ using food grade labels that will not
L229@P8: come off in the freezer.
L230@P8: Shelf stable foods

L232@P8: pack of pickles.
L233@P8: • Ensure that the packaging of shelf stable food is intact.
L234@P8-9: • Cover shelf -stable foods during storage to protect against contamination.Food safety g uideline s for the preparation and display of sushi

L236@P9: Preparing sushi
L237@P9: Preparing sushi involves a great deal of handling of both raw and cooked foods. Because sushi is
L238@P9: eaten without any further cooking it is important that it is prepared correctly and safely.
L239@P9: Raw foods can contain bacteria and, if not handled correctly, the numbers of bacteria can grow.
L240@P9: Poor handling of cooked foods can result in them becoming cross -contaminated from raw foods,
L241@P9: and if not stored correctly, the number of bacteria can also grow.
L242@P9: Generally, the food processing area must be kept clean, well maintained and free of pests to avoid
L243@P9: contamination. Below are some main points to assist in proper preparation of sushi and to assist in
L244@P9: avoiding cross -contamination.
L245@P9: Equipment and utensils
L246@P9: • With the exception of bamboo mats, do not use wooden utensils. All utensils must be able to be
L247@P9: easily cleaned and sanitised.
L248@P9: • Clean and sanitise all benches, utensils and equipment before preparing sushi.
L249@P9: • Sushi rolling machines must be cleaned and sanitised regularly during the day to remove the
L250@P9: build -up of rice and destroy pathogenic bacteria. Other utensils and equipment also need to be
L251@P9: cleaned regularly to remove build -up of rice and other ingredients.
L252@P9: • Clean and sanitise chopping boards and utensils in between preparing different foods, especially
L253@P9: when preparing foods that will not be cooked further ( for example, raw fish and cooked teriyaki
L254@P9: chicken).
L255@P9: • Clean and sanitise bamboo and plastic mats daily. If bamboo mats are used , it is recommended
L256@P9: that they are covered with clean cling wrap and the cling wrap is changed regularly.
L257@P9: Personal hygiene
L258@P9: Staff must have the appropriate skills and knowledge for any food handling activities they do. All
L259@P9: food handlers preparing and handling sushi must also ensure they follow good personal hygiene
L260@P9: practices.
L261@P9: • Do not prepare food when sick with vomiting, diarrhoea or fever.
L262@P9: • Take all practicable measures to prevent unnecessary contact with ready -to-eat food.
L263@P9: • Wash hands prior to preparing sushi and after handling non -food articles, using the toilet,
L264@P9: smoking, drinking, eating and touching hair, scalp or body.
L265@P9: • Wash hands between handling of raw ingredients and ready -to-eat foods.
L266@P9: Use of disposable gloves
L267@P9: It is not mandatory for food handlers to use disposable gloves, although if used correctly they can
L268@P9: assist with minimising contamination. When using disposable gloves they must be:
L269@P9-10: • Only used for one continuous task and then discarded.Food safety g uideline s for the preparation and display of sushi
L270@P10: FA 689 /2506 11
L271@P10: • Regularly changed to avoid cross -contamination – this is especially the case when changing
L272@P10: from preparing raw ingredients to handling ready -to-eat foods.
L273@P10: • When taken off they must always be discarded and not kept for use later.
L274@P10: • Removed and discarded before using the toilet, smoking, eating, drinking, or touching the hair,
L275@P10: scalp or body.
L276@P10: Preparation of acidified rice
L277@P10: Proper preparation of acidified rice is important to ensure the rice is safe to use. Rice acidified to a
L278@P10: pH of 4.6 or below will inhibit the growth of pathogenic bacteria.
L279@P10: Appendix 1 lists some sushi rice recipes that if followed should result in proper acidification of the
L280@P10: rice.
L281@P10: • Use a tested, standard recipe for sushi rice to ensure that the target pH is achieved every time.
L282@P10: • Acidify rice as soon as possible after cooking is finished. Mixing the vinegar solution whilst the
L283@P10: rice is still warm also helps the rice to absorb the vinegar/salt solution.
L284@P10: • Mix sushi rice thoroughly and uniformly with the vinegar solution.
L285@P10: • Check the pH of the rice to ensure proper acidification has occurred. An example of a record
L286@P10: sheet for rice acidification is provided in Appendix 4.
L287@P10: • Label the rice container with the time of acidification.
L288@P10: • Once acidified, store and cover the rice when not being used.
L289@P10: • Acidified rice can be stored up to 8 hours at room temperature. At the end of the day, the
L290@P10: remaining rice must be discarded.
L291@P10: Note: If cooked rice is not acidified, it must be stored under refrigeration at 5°C or below at all times.
L292@P10: Preparation of fillings and sushi
L293@P10: • Keep all potentially hazardous raw materials under refrigeration until used – this is especially
L294@P10: the case for raw fish.
L295@P10: • Acidified rice should be at room temperature prior to being used in making the sushi products.
L296@P10: • Thaw frozen foods under refrigeration or use a microwave oven.
L297@P10: • Wash vegetables before use.
L298@P10: • Cook meat and chicken thoroughly. Use a probe thermometer to confirm that the meat is
L299@P10: completely cooked (the core temperature must be 75°C or above).
L300@P10: • Cooked product must be cooled safely . The Code states that when cooling cooked potentially
L301@P10: hazardous food, cool the food :
L302@P10: — From 60°C to 21°C within 2 hours; and
L303@P10: — From 21°C to 5°C within a further 4 hours .
L304@P10: • Store prepared potentially hazardous ingredients ( for example, cooked chicken, tamago) under
L305@P10-11: refrigeration after cooking and when not being used.Food safety g uideline s for the preparation and display of sushi

L399@P14: Appendix 1. Rice acidification
L400@P14: At pH values of 4.6 or below, most pathogenic bacteria do not grow, sporulate or produce toxins
L401@P14: (Hocking, 2003). There are a few exceptions to this, Salmonella spp and enteropathogenic
L402@P14: Escherichia coli , although when the pH of the growth medium or food is adjusted using acetic acid,
L403@P14: the minimum pH for growth increased to above pH 4.6.
L404@P14: Therefore, acidification of the sushi rice using rice vinegar to a pH of 4.6 or below will assist in
L405@P14: stopping the growth of pathogenic bacteria. Sushi rice acidified to a pH of 4.6 or below can be
L406@P14: considered as a non -hazardous food and can be stored at room temperature during preparation. The
L407@P14: pH of the rice must be checked to make sure it has reached the pH of 4.6 or below.

L426@P15: Appendix 2. Measuring pH
L427@P15: pH measuring devices
L428@P15: pH can be measured using a pH meter, pH strips or pH paper.
L429@P15: Image: example of p H meter, pH strips and pH paper .
L430@P15: Pros and cons of each method
L431@P15: 1. pH paper
L432@P15: — pH paper must have a working range from 3.5 to 5.0.
L433@P15: — The paper should be able to read pH in 0.3 units, although it is difficult to distinguish less
L434@P15: than 0.6 of a unit.
L435@P15: — pH paper requires careful handling. Incorrect readings can occur from improper handling
L436@P15: (contamination from hands).
L437@P15: 2. pH strips
L438@P15: — Testing strips for pH must have a working range from 3.5 to 5.0.
L439@P15: — The strips should read pH in units of 0.5, although it is difficult to distinguish less than whole
L440@P15: units.
L441@P15-16: — It is relatively cheap, easy to use and does not require as careful handling as the pH paper.Food safety g uideline s for the preparation and display of sushi
L442@P16: FA 689 /2506 17
L443@P16: 3. Hand held digital pH meter
L444@P16: — It reads pH in 0.1 units with certainty.
L445@P16: — Some hand -held pH meters also measure the sample's temperature and compensate the
L446@P16: measurement for sample temperature.
L447@P16: — It requires calibration before use with at least a single buffer (buffer 4.0 is suitable for
L448@P16: acidified rice).
L449@P16: — It comes with instructions but may require some training of operators.
L450@P16: — A pH meter provides more accurate results than test strips. However , it can be more costly
L451@P16: than pH strips/papers. They must be cleaned regularly to avoid contamination, require
L452@P16: calibration before use and training of operators.
L453@P16: — Follow manufacturer’s instructions for calibration and maintenance of the instrument.
L454@P16: Measuring pH
L455@P16: • Once the vinegar mixture has been evenly mixed, place a small sample (¼ cup) in a clean
L456@P16: container.
L457@P16: • Dip the pH strip or stick directly into the rice and compare to colour chart.
L458@P16: • For pH meters, follow the manufacturer’s instructions.
L459@P16: • Record the pH on the checklist.
L460@P16: • If the pH is greater than 4.6, add more vinegar and mix. Take another pH reading.
L461@P16: • Continue adding vinegar until pH is at 4.6 or below.
L462@P16: • If extra vinegar is needed, sushi rice recipes should be revised to account for the extra vinegar
L463@P16: required.

L468@P17: Calibration procedures
L469@P17: Handheld thermometers should be calibrated monthly and results recorded on a checklist.
L470@P17: Ice Point (0°C)
