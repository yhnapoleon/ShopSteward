L420@P10: • Food businesses must ensure all foods are stored so they are protected from likely contamination
L421@P10: and that the environmental conditions will not adversely affect the safety or suitability of the food.
L422@P10: • There must be separately located storage facilities for items such as chemicals, clothing and
L423@P10: personal belongings that may contaminate food or food contact surfaces.

L435@P11: Potentially hazardous foods and temperature control
L436@P11: One of the most common causes of foodborne illness is the storage and display of potentially hazardous
L437@P11: foods at inadequate temperatures for extended periods. This can lead to the rapid and sustained growth
L438@P11: of food poisoning bacteria.
L439@P11: Examples of potentially hazardous foods include:
L440@P11: • cooked and ready-to-eat meat
L441@P11: • dairy products
L442@P11: • seafood
L443@P11: • prepared salads, raw salad vegetables
L444@P11: • cooked rice and pasta
L445@P11: • processed soya bean products
L446@P11: • other processed foods containing eggs, beans, nuts or other protein-rich foods that contain any of the
L447@P11: above foods such as sandwiches and quiches.
L448@P11: A food business must, when storing and displaying potentially hazardous food, store it under
L449@P11: temperature control. If the food is intended to be stored frozen ensure the food remains frozen during
L450@P11: storage and display. Temperature control means maintaining cold food at a temperature of 5°C or below,
L451@P11: or hot food at 60°C or above. Canned and bottled foods, dried or pickled products and some other
L452@P11: processed foods such as dried pasta, pasteurised juices and dried powder products, are not considered
L453@P11: to be potentially hazardous unless opened or reconstituted.
L454@P11: All food businesses that handle potentially hazardous foods are required to have a readily accessible,
L455@P11: accurate, probe-type thermometer (+/- 1°C accuracy). Ensure the thermometer probe is cleaned and
L456@P11: sanitised before it is used. It is good practice to monitor the temperature of hot or cold foods under
L457@P11: operating conditions to ensure adequate temperature control is being maintained.
L458@P11: Additional information on potentially hazardous food and its management can be found in the Guidance
L459@P11: on the 4-hour/2-hour rule document at www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au.
L460@P11: Cross contamination
L461@P11: Adequate measures must be taken to prevent cross contamination from raw foods to cooked foods. This
L462@P11: includes:
L463@P11: • ensuring there are separate utensils for cooked and raw meats, poultry and seafood,
L464@P11: • covering all food,
L465@P11: • keeping cooked meat and salads separate from raw meat, raw poultry, raw seafood and unwashed
L466@P11: raw vegetables, and
L467@P11: • washing hands after handling raw meats, raw poultry, raw seafood and raw vegetables.
L468@P11: Food display
L469@P11: When displaying food, take all practicable measures to protect the food from likely contamination by

L489@P12: Food Safety Supervisor
L490@P12: A food business needs to appoint a Food Safety Supervisor (FSS) if it is handling and selling food that is:
L491@P12: • ready-to-eat
L492@P12: • potentially hazardous (requires temperature control)
L493@P12: • not sold and served in the supplier’s original package.
L494@P12: At least 1 FSS needs to be appointed for each food vending vehicle. A copy of the FSS certificate must
L495@P12: be kept in the vehicle. You do not need to notify the local council of your FSS.
L496@P12: Mobile food vending vehicles garaged in another state are required to have a NSW FSS certificate if
L497@P12: operating in NSW. NSW FSS certificates can be obtained from the Food Authority for a small fee upon
L498@P12: submission of appropriate training documents.
L499@P12: The FSS should work onsite to oversee food handling but they do not have to be there all the time.
L500@P12: Charitable and community not-for-profit organisations do not need to appoint a Food Safety Supervisor if
L501@P12: they are handling or selling food for the purpose of raising funds solely for community or charitable
L502@P12: causes.
L503@P12: For more information on Food Safety Supervisor requirements go to foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/fss

L531@P13: Staff illness
L532@P13: A person who is unwell or suffering from a contagious illness must not handle food for sale. Symptoms
L533@P13: may include cold or flu symptoms, diarrhoea, vomiting, sore throat with fever, fever or jaundice and
L534@P13: infectious skin conditions.
L535@P13: A food handler must notify their supervisor if they know or suspect they may have contaminated food.
L536@P13: Hand washing
L537@P13: A food handler must wash his or her hands using soap and warm water, then dry them with single-use
L538@P13: towels:
L539@P13: • before commencing or re-commencing handling food,
L540@P13: • immediately before handling ready-to-eat food after handling raw food,
L541@P13: • immediately after using the toilet,
L542@P13: • immediately after smoking, coughing, sneezing, using a handkerchief or tissue, eating, drinking or
L543@P13: touching his or her hair, scalp or a body opening, and before using disposable gloves for handling
L544@P13: food. If wearing gloves, you should change them as often as you are required to wash your hands.
L545@P13: Money handling/touching customers’ hands when serving food
L546@P13: While the likelihood of contamination from customers’ hands when exchanging money is low,
L547@P13: consideration should be given to minimising the risk. Examples include using a disposable glove, or
L548@P13: where sufficient staff is available, nominating one to handle money.

L785@P21: Discharge from toilets, urinals, basins, showers, sinks and dishwashers through a sewer or other means.

L789@P21: Temperature control
L790@P21: Maintaining food at a temperature of:
L791@P21: • 5 °C or below if this is necessary to minimise the growth of infectious or toxigenic microorganisms in
L792@P21: the food so that the microbiological safety of the food will not be adversely affected for the time the
L793@P21: food is at that temperature, or
L794@P21: • 60°C or above, or
L795@P21: • another temperature ‒ if the business demonstrates that maintenance of the food at this temperature
L796@P21: for the period of time for which it is so maintained will not adversely affect the microbiological safety
L797@P21: of the food.
L798@P21: Unsafe and unsuitable food
L799@P21: Food is considered unsafe if it is likely to cause physical harm to a person who might later consume it,
L800@P21: assuming they treated the food correctly after purchase.
L801@P21: Food is considered unsuitable if it is damaged, deteriorated or perished to an extent that affects its
L802@P21: intended use, for example out of date, poor maintenance or poor storage of chemicals or contains a
L803@P21: substance that is foreign to the nature of the food, that is, foreign matter or chemicals which may have
L804@P21: fallen into the food.
L805@P21: Warm water
L806@P21: Not hotter than 50°C in order to comply with Australian/New Zealand Standard 3500.4.2021 (Plumbing
L807@P21: and drainage – heated water services)
