L0@P0: Food Authority
L1@P0: Recent changes to the Food Standards Code introduced new food safety
L2@P0: requirements for food retailers, including supermarkets, delis, greengrocers,
L3@P0: service stations and convenience stores.
L4@P0: In NSW, the changes apply from 8 December 2024 and affect businesses that sell unpackaged,
L5@P0: ready-to-eat food that requires temperature control.
L6@P0: The requirements
L7@P0: Standard 3.2.2A introduces 3 mandatory food safety management tools for food businesses,
L8@P0: depending on their food handling activities:
L9@P0: 1. Food Safety Supervisor
L10@P0: A business must have a Food
L11@P0: Safety Supervisor who:
L12@P0: • is qualified, and
L13@P0: • can give advice to
L14@P0: employees who handle
L15@P0: food.
L16@P0: 2. Food handler training
L17@P0: All staff who handle food must
L18@P0: have appropriate skills and
L19@P0: knowledge of food safety and
L20@P0: hygiene.
L21@P0: 3. Showing food is safe
L22@P0: A business must be able to
L23@P0: show they are managing key
L24@P0: food safety risks by either:
L25@P0: • demonstrating safe food
L26@P0: handling practices, or
L27@P0: • keeping records.
L28@P0: In NSW, local councils inspect most retail food businesses, including food retailers, under the Food
L29@P0: Regulation Partnership with the NSW Food Authority.
L30@P0: The Food Authority is encouraging councils to initially take an educative approach while businesses
L31@P0: adjust to the new requirements.

L34@P1: Who the requirements apply to
L35@P1: The Standard applies to all food services and businesses that sell or serve food that is:
L36@P1: • potentially hazardous (requires temperature control)
L37@P1: • ready-to-eat, and
L38@P1: • not in its original package.
L39@P1: Affected food retailers include:
L40@P1: Delicatessens Greengrocers Service stations and
L41@P1: convenience stores
L42@P1: Supermarkets and
L43@P1: market stalls
L44@P1: Potentially hazardous foods
L45@P1: Potentially hazardous foods have certain characteristics that support the growth of bacteria, or the
L46@P1: production of toxins, and must be kept at 5°C or colder, or 60°C or hotter, to stay safe to eat.
L47@P1: Examples of potentially hazardous food include:
L48@P1: Raw and cooked
L49@P1: meat and poultry
L50@P1: Deli meat, burgers,
L51@P1: curries, kebabs, pâté,
L52@P1: meat pies
L53@P1: Foods with eggs,
L54@P1: beans and nuts
L55@P1: Quiche, aioli,
L56@P1: mayonnaise,
L57@P1: mousses, tofu
L58@P1: Dairy and foods
L59@P1: containing dairy
L60@P1: Milk, dairy desserts,
L61@P1: fresh cream, custard,
L62@P1: cheese
L63@P1: Cooked rice and
L64@P1: pasta
L65@P1: Fried rice, plain rice,
L66@P1: spaghetti,
L67@P1: carbonara, lasagne
L68@P1: Seafood
L69@P1: Sushi, prawns, fish,
L70@P1: mussels, oysters,
L71@P1: shellfish
L72@P1: Sprouted seeds
L73@P1: Alfalfa sprouts, clover
L74@P1: sprouts, chickpea
L75@P1: sprouts
L76@P1: Cut fruits and
L77@P1: vegetables
L78@P1: Cut melon, salads,
L79@P1: pre-cut fruit trays,
L80@P1: vegetable sticks
L81@P1: Foods containing
L82@P1: any of these foods
L83@P1: Sandwiches, pizzas,
L84@P1: rice rolls.
L85@P1: Foods not considered potentially hazardous include raw whole fruit and vegetables, bread, biscuits,
L86@P1: crackers and crispbreads and plain cakes.

L91@P2: Categories under the Standard
L92@P2: Standard 3.2.2A classifies businesses into 2 categories, depending on their activities.
L93@P2: If a business engages in the activities of both category one and category two, it is deemed a category
L94@P2: one business. The requirements are based on the highest-risk activities, and are designed to
L95@P2: strengthen the business’s food safety management.
L96@P2: Category one businesses
L97@P2: Category one businesses process potentially hazardous food into a food that is ready-to-eat and
L98@P2: potentially hazardous and serve it to consumers. Processing means chopping, cooking, drying,
L99@P2: fermenting, heating, thawing and washing, or a combination of these.
L100@P2: Category one businesses must implement all 3 tools:
L101@P2: • have a qualified onsite Food Safety Supervisor
L102@P2: • ensure all food handlers have appropriate skills and knowledge of food safety and hygiene
L103@P2: • be able to show their food is safe.
L104@P2: Examples of category one food retailers
L105@P2: • A supermarket cooks roast chickens onsite for sale. In this example, potentially hazardous food is
L106@P2: processed (cooked) into a food that is potentially hazardous and sold ready-to-eat.
L107@P2: • A supermarket or deli makes and/or cooks food onsite that is sold as ready-to-eat food, such as
L108@P2: sushi, quiches, potato bakes, pasta salads, chopped salads and/or fried chicken. The food is held
L109@P2: on display (either refrigerated, or hot) and the products are packaged by staff for each customer.
L110@P2: • A supermarket seafood section thaws frozen cooked seafood, such as prawns, for sale. In this
L111@P2: example, the potentially hazardous food is processed (thawed) and served ready-to-eat.
L112@P2: • A supermarket bakery makes and sells custard tarts, quiches and fresh cream buns.
L113@P2: Category two businesses
L114@P2: Category two businesses sell or serve unpackaged, potentially hazardous, ready-to-eat food they
L115@P2: have not made themselves. The business might remove packaging before serving, or receive the food
L116@P2: unpackaged (for example, from a manufacturer). These businesses only slice, weigh, repack, reheat
L117@P2: or hot-hold the food, but do not process it in any other way.
L118@P2: Category two businesses must implement tools 1 and 2 in the Standard:
L119@P2: • have a qualified onsite Food Safety Supervisor
L120@P2: • ensure all food handlers have appropriate skills and knowledge of food safety and hygiene.
L121@P2: Examples of category two food retailers
L122@P2: • A supermarket receives bulk deli meats from a manufacturer and slices them on site. The sliced
L123@P2: meat is held in a refrigerated display unit and placed in a bag and weighed for each customer.
L124@P2: • A supermarket deli receives ready-to-eat salads in a bulk bag to portion and serve. The bag is
L125@P2: opened and the salad placed into a container in a refrigerated display unit, where customers can
L126@P2-3: pick an amount to be weighed and packaged for them by staff. Standard 3.2.2A guideline for food retailers
L127@P3: FA560/2311 4
L128@P3: • A convenience store receives packaged pies, pasties and sausage rolls from a local caterer. The
L129@P3: business unpackages these products, reheats them and then hot holds them in a display oven.
L130@P3: The products are placed in a bag for sale to the consumer.
L131@P3: • A greengrocer receives whole fruit and vegetables and slices it into individual portions that are sold
L132@P3: as ready-to-eat, for fruit salad and snack packs of carrots and celery.
L133@P3: • A greengrocer selling unpackaged, ready-to-eat sprouts.
L134@P3: • A greengrocer offers a fresh juice service, where fruit is cut and fed into a juicer. Juices may be
L135@P3: made to order and/or bottled onsite for sale but are not wholesaled.
L136@P3: Exclusions
L137@P3: In NSW, Standard 3.2.2A does not apply to the following:
L138@P3: • the handling or sale of food for the purpose of raising funds solely for community or charitable
L139@P3: causes, and providing food for free
L140@P3: • food businesses licensed with the NSW Food Authority
L141@P3: • businesses that only manufacture or wholesale food with no direct sale to consumers
L142@P3: • businesses that do not serve or sell unpackaged food that is potentially hazardous and ready-toeat
L143@P3: • greengrocers that sell only whole fruit and vegetables.

L147@P4: Tool 1 - Food Safety Supervisor (FSS)
L148@P4: The Food Safety Supervisor (FSS) requirement is not new in NSW – many businesses are already
L149@P4: required to appoint at least one per premises. Prior to the introduction of Standard 3.2.2A,
L150@P4: supermarkets, delicatessens and greengrocers were exempt from the FSS requirement – they now
L151@P4: have until 8 December 2024 to comply.
L152@P4: Food businesses in NSW must appoint a certified Food Safety Supervisor if they prepare and serve
L153@P4: food that is:
L154@P4: • unpackaged
L155@P4: • potentially hazardous (requires temperature control)
L156@P4: • ready-to-eat.
L157@P4: An FSS is someone associated with the business who is certified to have skills and knowledge in food
L158@P4: safety, especially around high-risk foods.
L159@P4: Food Safety Supervisors give businesses a better level of protection. They:
L160@P4: Oversee day-to-day
L161@P4: food handling
L162@P4: Ensure food safety
L163@P4: risks are managed
L164@P4: Share skills and
L165@P4: knowledge
L166@P4: Promote positive
L167@P4: food safety
L168@P4: practices
L169@P4: How to become a Food Safety Supervisor
L170@P4: Food Safety Supervisor training is delivered by approved Registered Training Organisations
L171@P4: (RTOs). Training is delivered online, face-to-face, in the workplace, or a combination, and can
L172@P4: generally be completed in one full day. Course costs, set by the RTOs, range from $99 to $200.
L173@P4: There are 2 learning pathways in NSW to achieve FSS certification – hospitality and retail. Food
L174@P4: retailers can use either pathway to qualify. In NSW the course must include either:
L175@P4: Two hospitality units:
L176@P4: • SITXFSA005 – Use hygiene practices for food safety
L177@P4: • SITXFSA006 – Participate in safe food handling practices
L178@P4: OR
L179@P4: One retail unit: SIRRFSA001 – Handle food safely in a retail environment.
L180@P4: Certification is valid for 5 years, after which the business has 30 working days to ensure their
L181@P4: appointed FSS completes recertification training with an approved RTO.
L182@P4: Nominating a Food Safety Supervisor
L183@P4: The business nominates their own Food Safety Supervisor. They must have the authority and ability to
L184@P4: give direction on the safe handling of food. It may be the owner, manager or other paid staff.
L185@P4: The FSS should work onsite to oversee food handling but they do not have to be there all the time.
L186@P4-5: Larger retailers may choose to certify more than one FSS to cover shift work and leave. Standard 3.2.2A guideline for food retailers

L188@P5: A copy of the FSS certificate must remain on the premises.
L189@P5: Steps to complying with the Food Safety Supervisor requirement
L190@P5: 1. Choose who will be the FSS.
L191@P5: 2. The chosen staff member completes the training and assessment through an approved RTO.
L192@P5: 3. The FSS receives a Statement of Attainment and a NSW FSS certificate. A copy of the certificate
L193@P5: must be kept on the business premises.
L194@P5: 4. After 5 years, the FSS completes recertification training and assessment for an updated NSW FSS
L195@P5: certificate and an updated Statement of Attainment for the required units of competency.

L201@P6: Tool 2 – Food handler training
L202@P6: Food businesses handling and serving unpackaged, potentially hazardous food, must ensure their
L203@P6: food handlers have appropriate skills and knowledge in food safety and hygiene. This is different to
L204@P6: the Food Safety Supervisor requirement. Supermarkets, delis and greengrocers have until 8
L205@P6: December 2024 to comply with this requirement.
L206@P6: Businesses can choose how food handlers are trained. They may use or recognise free online food
L207@P6: safety training programs, past experience, internal training tailored to suit their own procedures, or
L208@P6: courses from vocational training providers.
L209@P6: Additional training is not needed if food handlers can already demonstrate adequate skills and
L210@P6: knowledge for their duties.
L211@P6: If opting for food handlers to undertake a food handler training course, it must cover:
L212@P6: Safe handling of
L213@P6: food
L214@P6: Temperature control for
L215@P6: potentially hazardous
L216@P6: food, understanding the
L217@P6: temperature danger zone
L218@P6: Food contamination
L219@P6: Strategies to separate raw
L220@P6: and cooked food, allergen
L221@P6: management, food
L222@P6: storage, and use of
L223@P6: separate utensils and
L224@P6: chopping boards
L225@P6: Cleaning and
L226@P6: sanitising
L227@P6: Correct procedures for
L228@P6: cleaning then sanitising,
L229@P6: using food-safe
L230@P6: chemicals and/or heat
L231@P6: as a sanitiser
L232@P6: Personal hygiene
L233@P6: Obligations for sick
L234@P6: employees,
L235@P6: handwashing, uniforms
L236@P6: and grooming of hair,
L237@P6: nails and skin.
L238@P6: Free training available
L239@P6: The NSW Food Authority’s free online Food Handler Basics training course can help retailers meet
L240@P6: food handler training obligations under the Food Standards Code.
L241@P6: The interactive short course covers the basic knowledge required and takes about an hour to
L242@P6: complete. It is not the same as Food Safety Supervisor training, which is delivered by certified
L243@P6: registered training organisations.
L244@P6: • Access Food Handler Basics training
L245@P6: Steps to complying with the food handler training requirement
L246@P6: 1. Check whether food handlers have an adequate understanding of the above topics. You may wish
L247@P6: to refer to the factsheets available at foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/resource-centre.
L248@P6: 2. Set aside at least 1 hour for each food handler who requires training to complete the Food
L249@P6: Authority’s free Food Handler Basics course, or develop and deliver in-house training.
L250@P6: 3. It is recommended you keep a record of training food handlers have completed to show authorised
L251@P6-7: officers on request. Standard 3.2.2A guideline for food retailers

L253@P7: Tool 3 – Showing food is safe
L254@P7: Retailers that undertake higher risk food handling need to be able to demonstrate safe food practices
L255@P7: under Standard 3.2.2A of the Code. This requirement ensures businesses are actively monitoring and
L256@P7: managing key risks related to temperature control, processing, and cleaning and sanitising, which are
L257@P7: critical for food safety.  Retailers can meet this requirement by either:
L258@P7: Demonstrating safe
L259@P7: food handling practices
L260@P7: Keeping records
L261@P7: Who this requirement applies to
L262@P7: This requirement applies to businesses that process potentially hazardous food into a food that is
L263@P7: ready-to-eat and potentially hazardous, and serve it to consumers. “Process” is defined as chopping,
L264@P7: cooking, drying, fermenting, heating, thawing and/or washing. Supermarkets, delis and greengrocers
L265@P7: have until 8 December 2024 to comply with this requirement.
L266@P7: What needs to be shown
L267@P7: Businesses must be able to show they are monitoring and managing the following (where applicable):
L268@P7: Activity  Food safety requirement
L269@P7: Receipt   A food business must take all practicable measures to ensure it only
L270@P7: accepts potentially hazardous food (PHF) at a temperature of:
L271@P7: • 5°C or below; or
L272@P7: • 60°C or above, unless the business transporting the food
L273@P7: demonstrates the temperature of the food will not adversely affect its
L274@P7: microbiological safety due to the time taken to deliver it.
L275@P7: What needs to be shown: The temperature of PHF when received OR
L276@P7: the time arrangement with the supplier showing that food safety has not
L277@P7: been adversely affected during food transport.
L278@P7: Storage A food business must, when storing PHF, store it under temperature
L279@P7: control.
L280@P7: What needs to be shown: The temperature of PHF during storage.
L281@P7: Display A food business must, when displaying PHF, display it under temperature
L282@P7: control.
L283@P7: What needs to be shown: That PHF is displayed at 5°C or below; or
L284@P7: 60°C or above, or the total time out of temperature control does not
L285@P7: exceed 4 hours (2 hr/4 hr rule).

L288@P8: Transport A food business must, when transporting food, transport PHF under
L289@P8: temperature control.
L290@P8: What needs to be shown: That PHF is transported at 5°C or below; or
L291@P8: 60°C or above, or total time out of temperature control does not exceed 4
L292@P8: hours (2 hr/4 hr rule).
L293@P8: Pathogen reduction
L294@P8: during food
L295@P8: processing (cooking)
L296@P8: A food business must, when processing food, where a process step is
L297@P8: needed to reduce to safe levels any pathogens that may be present in the
L298@P8: food, use a process step that is reasonably known to achieve the
L299@P8: microbiological safety of the food.
L300@P8: What needs to be shown: That pathogen reduction or killing step has
L301@P8: been used on PHF to achieve safety of the food (destroy pathogens
L302@P8: potentially present). See Processing food safely for guidance.
L303@P8: Minimising time during
L304@P8: food processing
L305@P8: A food business must, when processing PHF that is not undergoing a
L306@P8: pathogen control step, ensure that the time the food remains at
L307@P8: temperatures that permit the growth of infectious or toxigenic
L308@P8: microorganisms in the food is minimised.
L309@P8: What needs to be shown: The time that ready-to-eat PHF is in the
L310@P8: temperature danger zone (between 5°C and 60°C) is minimised and does
L311@P8: not make the food unsafe (see the 2-hour/4hour rule).
L312@P8: Cooling food A food business must cool PHF:
L313@P8: • within 2 hours – from 60°C to 21°C; and
L314@P8: • within a further 4 hours – from 21°C to 5°C unless the food business
L315@P8: demonstrates that the cooling process used will not adversely affect
L316@P8: the microbiological safety of the food.
L317@P8: What needs to be shown: That cooked PHF has cooled to prescribed
L318@P8: temperatures within the required timeframes.
L319@P8: Reheating food
L320@P8: A food business must, when reheating previously cooked and cooled
L321@P8: potentially hazardous food to hold it hot, use a heat process that rapidly
L322@P8: heats the food to a temperature of 60°C or above, unless the food
L323@P8: business demonstrates that the heating process used will not adversely
L324@P8: affect the microbiological safety of the food.
L325@P8: What needs to be shown: That cooked and cooled PHF is reheated
L326@P8: rapidly (within 2 hours) to 60°C or above.
L327@P8: Cleaning and
L328@P8: sanitising
L329@P8: A food business must ensure the following equipment is in a clean and
L330@P8: sanitary condition in the circumstances set out below:
L331@P8: • eating and drinking utensils - immediately before each use; and
L332@P8: • food contact surfaces of equipment - whenever food that will come into
L333@P8: contact with the surface is likely to be contaminated.
L334@P8: What needs to be shown: That eating and drinking utensils and food
L335@P8: contact surfaces have been cleaned and sanitised when required (see
L336@P8-9: Cleaning and sanitising surfaces and utensils (PDF, 384KB)).Standard 3.2.2A guideline for food retailers

L338@P9: Keeping records
L339@P9: ‘Records’ are documents or objects kept for the information they contain, or can be obtained from
L340@P9: them. Examples include writing on invoices or templates, photos or footage, data logger information,
L341@P9: and verified written instructions, such as a validated recipe or standard operating procedure (SOP).
L342@P9: If making records, they should include the date (and time, if appropriate) and the food or activity it
L343@P9: relates to. Records should be made each day the business handles unpackaged potentially hazardous
L344@P9: food, unless it can be shown in another way requirements are being met. Once made, records must
L345@P9: be kept for 3 months.
L346@P9: Templates available
L347@P9: A range of free templates are available to help businesses record food temperatures and their
L348@P9: cleaning and sanitising activities on the Food Authority website at Showing food is safe.
L349@P9: Demonstrating safe practices
L350@P9: Records are not needed if the business can show in another way it has adequately managed food
L351@P9: safety risks.
L352@P9: This could include having a written instruction sheet or standard operating procedure, and/or being
L353@P9: able to walk and talk an authorised officer through their food handling practices and processes.
L354@P9: Steps to complying with the showing food is safe requirement
L355@P9: • Ensure food handlers are capable of demonstrating safe food handling practices and processes to
L356@P9: an authorised officer, or
L357@P9: • Have a written instruction sheet or standard operating procedure that is followed for each of the
L358@P9: activities above, or
L359@P9: • Keep records of important food safety information, including how you have safely received, stored,
L360@P9: processed, displayed, and transported potentially hazardous food, and cleaned and sanitised.
