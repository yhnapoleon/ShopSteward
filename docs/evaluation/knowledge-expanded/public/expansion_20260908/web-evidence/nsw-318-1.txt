L0@P0: Food Authority
L1@P0: Food suppliers, including manufacturers and importers, must have procedures in
L2@P0: place for the recall and withdrawal of unsafe or unsuitable food. Retailers must
L3@P0: remove recalled food from sale.
L4@P0: What is a food recall?
L5@P0: A food recall is used to remove from sale a food that poses an immediate risk to public health and
L6@P0: safety. Food recalls are usually initiated by the manufacturer, distributor or importer and must be
L7@P0: reported to state and commonwealth government authorities.
L8@P0: What is a withdrawal?
L9@P0: A withdrawal is action taken to remove a food product from sale even if there is no public health and
L10@P0: safety issue. This is usually done for two reasons:
L11@P0: 1. the product has a quality defect or has a labelling irregularity that is not a public health risk
L12@P0: 2. as a precaution, pending further investigation of a potential public health risk. If a public health risk
L13@P0: is established, then the food must be recalled.
L14@P0: Withdrawals do not need to be notified to authorities.
L15@P0: Types of food recalls
L16@P0: A food recall is action taken to remove a food product from sale to minimise or prevent a risk to public
L17@P0: health and safety. There are three types of recalls:
L18@P0: Trade level – involves removing the product from distribution centres, wholesalers and retail outlets.
L19@P0: This type of recall may involve hospitals, restaurants and catering establishments that sell food
L20@P0: manufactured for immediate consumption or food that is prepared on the premises. This type of recall
L21@P0: is limited to foods that are sold wholesale or, if sold at retail, sold for immediate consumption.
L22@P0: Consumer level – involves recovery of the product from all points in the production, distribution and
L23@P0: retail networks/chains including any affected product in the possession of consumers.
L24@P0: Mandatory recall – where the CEO of the Food Authority issues an order requiring the supplier to
L25@P0: recall a food product.

L42@P1: Initiating a food recall
L43@P1: FSANZ coordinates all food recalls on a national level. The NSW Food Authority is responsible for
L44@P1: monitoring food recalls. and liaising with manufacturers, wholesalers and importers in NSW.
L45@P1: The majority of food recalls are initiated by businesses when food production monitoring has identified
L46@P1: a fault, in response to public complaints, or as a result of government testing.
L47@P1: If a food recall is required the FSANZ and NSW Food Authority Recall Coordinators will work with the
L48@P1: manufacturer, importer or distributor to ensure the recall is conducted effectively.
L49@P1: Current list of food recalls
L50@P1: Details of recent national consumer food recalls can be found at
L51@P1: http://www.foodstandards.gov.au/industry/foodrecalls/recalls/Pages/default.aspx.
L52@P1: Food retailers
L53@P1: It is the responsibility of all food retailers to remove all recalled product from sale. Retailers who
L54@P1: continue to sell a recalled product are in breach of regulations.
