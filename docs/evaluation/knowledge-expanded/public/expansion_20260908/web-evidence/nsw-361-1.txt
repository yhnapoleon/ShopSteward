L33@P2: Introduction
L34@P2: This document explains how retail food businesses can use the 2-hour/4-hour rule as an alternative
L35@P2: method of complying with Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code (Food Standards Code)
L36@P2: requirements to keep potentially hazardous foods under temperature control while on display, during
L37@P2: short-term storage and preparation.
L38@P2: Application
L39@P2: The document does not cover all requirements of the Food Standards Code, in particular requirements
L40@P2: relating to premises and equipment. Businesses must read the Food Standards Code to ensure they comply
L41@P2: with all aspects of the Food Standards Code as it relates to their business.
L42@P2: For businesses licensed with the NSW Food Authority under Food Regulation 2015, there may be specific
L43@P2: temperature control requirements which businesses must comply with.
L44@P2: For sushi retailers, the Food Authority has prepared the Food Safety Guidelines for the Preparation and
L45@P2: Display of Sushi, available at www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au, for how the 2-hour/4-hour rule should be
L46@P2: applied.

L52@P3: Definitions
L53@P3: 2-hour/4-hour rule An alternative method of satisfying the temperature control requirements
L54@P3: specified in the Food Standards Code.
L55@P3: The principal concepts of the 2-hour/4-hour rule are that when potentially
L56@P3: hazardous ready-to-eat food has been kept between 5°C and 60°C for:
L57@P3: • up to 2 hours, it can be refrigerated below 5°C, or kept hot above 60°C, or
L58@P3: used immediately
L59@P3: • between 2 and 4 hours, it must be used immediately
L60@P3: • up to a total of 4 hours or more, it must be thrown out.

L65@P3: the food business, including records of compliance and other related action,

L108@P5: The use of time as a control for potentially hazardous food
L109@P5: Because food poisoning bacteria take time to grow to numbers that cause food poisoning, the Food Standards Code
L110@P5: provides an alternative to holding food below 5°C or above 60°C. It allows food businesses to hold food between 5°C
L111@P5: and 60°C for short, measured periods of time.
L112@P5: Introducing the 2-hour/4-hour rule
L113@P5: Studies have been done that show food can be safely held out of temperature control for short periods of
L114@P5: time without significantly increasing the risk of food poisoning. The time for which food can be safely held
L115@P5: between 5°C and 60°C is commonly referred to as the ‘2-hour/4-hour rule’ and is applied as follows:
L116@P5: Total time food is between 5°C and 60°C
L117@P5: ‘Use immediately’ means that the food must be sold within that 2-hour period, or cooked or processed to
L118@P5: reduce or eliminate any pathogenic bacteria present in the food.
L119@P5: The NSW Food Authority and local councils recognise the 2-hour/4-hour rule as a validated alternative means
L120@P5: of compliance with temperature control requirements in the Food Standards Code. However, it must be
L121@P5: used correctly.
L122@P5: This guideline has been prepared to help retail businesses apply the 2-hour/4-hour rule correctly to ensure
L123@P5: compliance with Food Standards Code requirements.
L124@P5: 1 hour
L125@P5: 3 hours 2 hours
L126@P5: 4 hours
L127@P5: 0 to 2 hours
L128@P5: Use immediately or
L129@P5: refrigerate ≤ 5°C or
L130@P5: keep hot ≥ 60°C
L131@P5: 2 to 4 hours
L132@P5: Use immediately
L133@P5: After 4 hours
L134@P5: Throw away
L135@P5: After the first 2 hours (red zone)
L136@P5: the food must be either used up
L137@P5: within 2 hours or thrown out.
L138@P5: It cannot be returned to
L139@P5: refrigeration or reheated at 60°C
L140@P5: or above (unless cooking for
L141@P5: immediate consumption).
L142@P5: Within the first 2 hours (green zone)
L143@P5: there are three options for the food:
L144@P5: - it can be used immediately,
L145@P5: - returned to refrigeration at or
L146@P5: below 5°C, or
L147@P5: - reheated to 60°C or above.

L150@P6: How to use the 2-hour/4-hour rule
L151@P6: To ensure that the 2-hour/4-hour rule is being applied correctly to the retail display or temporary storage of
L152@P6: potentially hazardous food, there are certain requirements that must be met. You must be able to show how
L153@P6: you are complying with the rule if asked by an authorised officer from the Food Authority or a local council
L154@P6: Environmental Health Officer.
L155@P6: To demonstrate that the 2-hour/4-hour rule is being applied correctly, you will need to:
L156@P6: • ensure the Food Standards Code requirements are followed for potentially hazardous food and
L157@P6: ingredients during:
L158@P6: o receival,
L159@P6: o storage, and
L160@P6: o preparation.
L161@P6: • ensure cold foods are not displayed in direct sunlight or at temperatures above 25°C, as this will allow
L162@P6: bacteria to quickly increase in numbers.
L163@P6: • implement a documented system for:
L164@P6: o monitoring the length of time food is displayed out of temperature control,
L165@P6: o ensuring the food is appropriately identified, and
L166@P6: o ensuring food is disposed of appropriately after 4 hours.
L167@P6: Food which has been in the temperature danger zone for less than 2 hours (during preparation + storage +
L168@P6: display) can be returned to the refrigerator at or below 5°C or heated to above 60°C and brought out again
L169@P6: at a later time. However, the total time in the temperature danger zone must not be longer than 4 hours.
L170@P6: Whatever system you choose to use, you must be able to demonstrate what you have done.
L171@P6: The documented system can take the form of:
L172@P6: • a set of work instructions on how the 2-hour/4-hour rule is applied by your business (see Appendix 1
L173@P6: for an example), or
L174@P6: • keeping records demonstrating adherence to the time temperature requirements (see Appendix 2 for
L175@P6: an example).
L176@P6: If you decide to use the 2-hour/4-hour rule for temperature control but are not able to satisfactorily
L177@P6: demonstrate that you are applying the rule correctly, then you are in breach of Food Standards Code
L178@P6: requirements. It could lead to enforcement action being taken against you.
L179@P6: It is the responsibility of the food business to ensure all food handlers are adequately trained in the use of
L180@P6-7: the documented work instructions or the completion of records if the 2-hour/4-hour rule is to be applied.8
L181@P7: Ensure potentially hazardous food and ingredients are handled correctly
L182@P7: To use the 2-hour/4-hour rule, you must ensure that potentially hazardous foods and ingredients have
L183@P7: been handled correctly during receival, storage and preparation. If you cannot be sure of this, then you
L184@P7: cannot satisfactorily demonstrate the use of the 2-hour/4-hour rule.
L185@P7: Receival of food
L186@P7: Only receive potentially hazardous foods that have been transported under temperature control.
L187@P7: • Once you receive potentially hazardous foods, you must place it under refrigerated storage at 5°C or
L188@P7: less, or keep it hot above 60°C, or place it on display immediately with the time recorded or
L189@P7: according to your work instructions.
L190@P7: • You should always check the temperature of food for each batch received. The temperature must be
L191@P7: below 5°C or above 60°C (as appropriate).
L192@P7: • Any temperature outside this range should be recorded and the food either rejected or, if there is
L193@P7: evidence that the food has only been out of temperature control for a short period of time, an
L194@P7: assessment made as to its safety prior to its use and this time taken off the allowable 4 hours out of
L195@P7: temperature control during display, preparation, or storage. Any assessment or corrective action
L196@P7: should be recorded.
L197@P7: Storage
L198@P7: You must keep all potentially hazardous foods and ingredients under temperature control until you use or
L199@P7: display them.
L200@P7: • Food must be covered during storage to protect against contamination.
L201@P7: • Cool room temperatures should be checked and recorded periodically to ensure they are running at
L202@P7: 5°C or below.
L203@P7: Preparation of ready-to-eat foods
L204@P7: Because ready-to-eat food is eaten without any further cooking, it is important that you handle it correctly
L205@P7: and safely. If your business only has a limited amount of storage and display space, you should only
L206@P7: prepare an amount of food that you can adequately store or display under temperature control.
L207@P7: • When preparing foods such as sandwiches, limit the amount of time the potentially hazardous
L208@P7: ingredients (e.g., cooked chicken, ham, fresh cut lettuce) are out of temperature control.
L209@P7: • Where sandwich ingredients are on display for ready-made sandwiches, the ingredients must be kept
L210@P7: under temperature control or have the 2-hour/4-hour rule applied to them.
L211@P7: • It is important to maintain good personal hygiene when preparing ready-to-eat foods.
L212@P7: The total allowable time food can stay out of temperature control is 4 hours, so any time during transport,
L213@P7-8: storage or preparation the food spends between 5°C and 60°C must be counted towards this time.9
L214@P8: Display and use of cooked foods
L215@P8: If potentially hazardous food has not been cooled safely after cooking, the number of bacteria may be able
L216@P8: to grow, and it may not be safe for this food to have the 2-hour/4-hour rule applied to it.
L217@P8: Cold display You must ensure that cooked foods have been cooled in accordance with Food Standards
L218@P8: Code requirements – that is, cooled to below 21°C within 2 hours and below 5°C within a
L219@P8: total of 6 hours.
L220@P8: Hot display You must display cooked foods at 60°C or above, or according to the 2-hour/4-hour rule with
L221@P8: the time commencing immediately when the coolest part of the food drops below 60°C (i.e.,
L222@P8: into the temperature danger zone).
L223@P8: Bain marie and hot display cabinet temperatures should be checked and recorded periodically to ensure
L224@P8: they are able to maintain food at or above 60°C. If they operate at a lower temperature, the 2-hour/4-hour
L225@P8: rule is applied.
L226@P8-9: You must keep cooked foods separate from raw foods to avoid them becoming cross contaminated.10

L401@P13: Why do foods need to be kept under temperature control?
L402@P13: The temperature range between 5°C and 60°C is the ‘temperature danger zone’ where food poisoning
L403@P13: bacteria can grow.
L404@P13: The legal requirement in Standard 3.2.2 of the Food Standards Code requires potentially hazardous food to
L405@P13: be displayed under temperature control so that bacteria don’t grow in the food. This means:
L406@P13: • you must minimise the time that food spends in the temperature danger zone to protect your
L407@P13: customers from food poisoning
L408@P13: • you need to keep refrigerated food cold (5ºC or below)
L409@P13: • you need to keep hot foods at hot temperatures (60ºC or above)
L410@P13: • you can keep foods at another temperature, but only if it is safe to do so.
L411@P13: If you want to display potentially hazardous food at temperatures between 5°C and 60°C you must
L412@P13-14: demonstrate this will not affect the safety of the food.15
L413@P14: Potentially hazardous foods
L414@P14: In Standard 3.2.2 potentially hazardous food is defined as food that has to be kept at certain temperatures
L415@P14: to minimise the growth of any pathogenic microorganisms that may be present in the food or to prevent
L416@P14: the formation of toxins in the food. Examples of potentially hazardous foods include:
L417@P14: • raw and cooked meat/poultry or foods containing raw or cooked meat/poultry; for example, burgers,

L435@P14: Shelf stable foods are not subject to the temperature control requirements. Shelf stable foods can be
L436@P14: displayed at ambient (room) temperatures for the duration of the shelf life of the food. Examples include:
L437@P14: • whole fruits and vegetables
L438@P14: • canned food*
L439@P14: • dried foods
L440@P14: • pickled or preserved foods
