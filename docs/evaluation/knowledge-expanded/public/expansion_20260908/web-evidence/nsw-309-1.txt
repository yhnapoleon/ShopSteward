L69@P3: Introduction
L70@P3: Fast Choices is the NSW Government’s nutritional information menu labelling program for business
L71@P3: chains and franchises selling takeaway, ready -to-eat (RTE) foods. The program aims to help
L72@P3: consumers make healthier choices by displaying kilojoule (kJ) values for standard food items.
L73@P3: In NSW, the Fast Choices program is legislated in the Food Act 2003 and Food Regulation 2025
L74@P3: (together, the legislation). The legislation defines a ‘standard food outlet ’ as required to display the
L75@P3: kJ content of ‘standard food items ’ along with a reference statement.
L76@P3: Standard food outlets typically belong to large chains that are company owned or operate a
L77@P3: franchise model, or a mixture of both, and shar e a common name, branding scheme and menu. They
L78@P3: capture a diverse range of businesses, including supermarkets, quick service restaurants, caf és and
L79@P3: beverage outlets, salad bars and other food court and home delivery businesses.
L80@P3: Kilojoules provide a consistent baseline for consumers to compare the energy content of potential
L81@P3: food choices . The reference statement provides a guide for total recommended daily kJ
L82@P3: consumption from all foods . Together these two elements form the nutritional information
L83@P3: requirements in the legislation.
L84@P3: Nutritional information menu labelling by standard food outlets helps consumers make healthier
L85@P3: food choices and is intended to help address the incidence of overweight and obesity in the NSW
L86@P3: population. In 2017 -18, it was estimated that obesity costs Australia $11.8 billion annually 1. While
L87@P3: these costs are dated, the NSW Population Health Survey and the Australian Bureau of Statistics
L88@P3: National Health Survey show an increasing trend in the number of overweight and obese adults. One
L89@P3: of the many contributing f actors is the consumption of energy -dense, nutrient -poor foods.
L90@P3: Australians spend about 42 cents in every food dollar eating out of home, with over a third of that
L91@P3: spent on fast food. Serving sizes when eating out also tend to be larger and contain more sa turated
L92@P3: fat and salt than meals prepared at home 2.
L93@P3: Consumers place a premium on their right to choose what they eat . Industry and government can
L94@P3: support this expectation with accurate information so consumers can make healthier choices. The
L95@P3: Fast Choices program is part of NSW Government’s range of programs to promote healthier eating .
L96@P3: Businesses are required to comply with the legislation if they meet the criteria for a ‘standard food
L97@P3: outlet’.
L98@P3: Nutritional information labelling legislation has also been introduced in the Australian Capital
L99@P3: Territory, Queensland, South Australia, and Victoria. Businesses that meet the criteria in each
L100@P3: jurisdiction (in NSW 20 outlets) or have more than 50 outlets nationally need to review each
L101@P3: jurisdiction ’s legislation for minor differences .

L245@P10: Standard food item s
L246@P10: The legislation gives a broad definition for what is a standard food item. All standard food items are:
L247@P10: • controlled for portion size and content , but may be offered in different portion sizes (for
L248@P10: example, small, medium and large )
L249@P10: • sold unpackaged
L250@P10: • made to order according to a standard recipe at the premises
L251@P10: • listed or shown on a menu, or displayed for sale in a cabinet
L252@P10: • may be combination meals that contain a selection of default standard food items .
L253@P10: A standard food item may be modified to accommodate a customer’s allergies or preferences
L254@P10: without needing to calculate a new kilojoule (kJ) figure for the item sold . This does not avoid the
L255@P10: need to provide a kJ value for the standard food item made to the standard recipe.

L260@P10: item’.

L370@P16: Customisable food
L371@P16: Customisable food items are not considered standard food items. These products are displayed with
L372@P16: the intent that customers will select most , or all , of the ingredients as part of the ordering process.
L373@P16: Customisable food items include build -your -own pizzas, sandwiches , burritos and wraps made by
L374@P16: the customer selecting individual ingredients from a display or list of options. These types of food do
