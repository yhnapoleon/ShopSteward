L258@P8: Receiving and storing eggs & other food
L259@P8: Food safety begins by ensuring only safe and suitable ingredients are purchased from a supplier and
L260@P8: that these items are stored correctly. The following information is provided to assist meeting the
L261@P8: requirements of the Food Standards Code regarding the receipt and storage of food.
L262@P8: Receiving eggs
L263@P8: • Only accept eggs that are:
L264@P8: — clean,
L265@P8: — free from visible cracks and not leaking,
L266@P8: — stamped,
L267@P8: — supplied in clean packaging, and
L268@P8: — correctly labelled (i.e. with the supplier’s name and address, date marking).
L269@P8: • Check the eggs when they arrive and always reject eggs that are broken, cracked or dirty, or that are
L270@P8: supplied in dirty, defective, re-used or improperly labelled packaging. Accepting eggs in poor
L271@P8: condition places your business at risk.
L272@P8: Supplier and food information
L273@P8: • A list of food suppliers should be maintained in case they need to be contacted.
L274@P8: • For all foods, the label or receipt needs to contain details of suppliers’ names and addresses. Phone
L275@P8: contact details should also be kept.
L276@P8: • Items that do not meet the above receiving and information requirements should be returned to the
L277@P8: supplier.
L278@P8: Storage of whole eggs
L279@P8: • Store whole eggs (egg in shell) in a refrigerator or cool room.
L280@P8: • Avoid temperature fluctuations and only take out what is required for service.
L281@P8: • Don’t store fresh egg pulp that has been collected (pooled) in a container.
L282@P8: • Ensure proper stock rotation so that the oldest stock is used first (as long as they are still within the
L283@P8: best before or use-by-date).
L284@P8: • Discard any cartons that contain spilt raw egg to avoid contamination.
L285@P8: • Eggs should not be re-packed into cartons with labels that do not correctly reflect the origin, weight or
L286@P8: egg type.
L287@P8: Other foods
L288@P8: Potentially hazardous foods must be stored under refrigeration at or below 5°C. This includes meat,
L289@P8: chicken, seafood, dairy products and eggs.
L290@P8: • Only receive foods that are within their ‘Use-by’ date or ‘Best Before’ date.
L291@P8: • Only receive potentially hazardous food that has been transported under temperature control.
L292@P8: • Once received, all potentially hazardous foods must be placed under refrigerated storage at or below
L293@P8: 5°C.
L294@P8: • Refrigerated raw ingredients must be stored separately from ready-to-eat foods and ingredients.
L295@P8: • Raw foods such as uncooked chicken and meat must not be placed above ready-to-eat foods in the
L296@P8-9: refrigerator to prevent the raw juices from dripping onto the ready-to-eat foods.Food Safety Guidelines for the Preparation of Raw and Lightly Cooked Egg Products

L303@P9: Egg handling practices – practical steps to control Salmonella
L304@P9: Preparing foods can involve a great deal of handling of both raw and cooked foods. Because raw egg
L305@P9: products are eaten without any further cooking it is important that they are prepared correctly and safely.
L306@P9: Raw foods can contain bacteria and if the food is not handled correctly, the numbers of bacteria can
L307@P9: grow. Poor handling of cooked foods can result in cross-contamination from raw foods and if not stored
L308@P9: correctly, the number of bacteria can also grow.
L309@P9: Below are some main points to assist in proper preparation of foods, particularly raw egg products, and
L310@P9: to help avoid cross-contamination. For further information, refer to Standard 3.2.2 of the Food Standards
L311@P9: Code.
L312@P9: Premises
L313@P9: The cleanliness of the premises, fixtures and fittings assist in minimising cross contamination. In
L314@P9: particular it is important to have an acceptable hand washing facility that includes:
L315@P9: — warm running water,
L316@P9: — soap,
L317@P9: — single-use hand towels, for example paper towels.
L318@P9: Equipment, utensils, and food contact surfaces
L319@P9: • All equipment and utensils must be in good condition and able to be easily cleaned and sanitised.
L320@P9: • Mechanical equipment, such as blenders and food processors, should be able to be easily taken
L321@P9: apart for cleaning and sanitising. If they cannot be taken apart, equipment like stick blenders may not
L322@P9: be appropriate for processing raw eggs.
L323@P9: • Food contact surfaces such as equipment, benches and utensils must be cleaned and sanitised
L324@P9: before use.
L325@P9: • Boards and utensils must be cleaned and sanitised between preparing different foods, especially
L326@P9: when preparing foods that will not be further cooked (for example raw egg products).
L327@P9: • Use a sanitised egg separator to separate egg yolk from egg whites. Eggs must not be separated
L328@P9: using their shells as the shells may be contaminated with Salmonella on the outside surface.
L329@P9: Personal Hygiene
L330@P9: All food handlers must take all reasonable measures not to handle food or surfaces likely to come into
L331@P9: contact with food in a way that is likely to compromise the safety and suitability of food.
L332@P9: Food handlers must ensure they follow good personal hygiene practices:
L333@P9: • People who are sick with vomiting, diarrhoea or fever must not prepare or serve food.
L334@P9: • Take all practicable measures to prevent unnecessary contact with ready-to-eat food.
L335@P9: • Wash hands prior to preparing food and after handling non-food articles, using the toilet, smoking,
L336@P9: drinking, eating and touching hair, scalp or body.
L337@P9-10: • Wash hands between handling of raw ingredients and ready-to-eat foods.Food Safety Guidelines for the Preparation of Raw and Lightly Cooked Egg Products

L339@P10: Use of disposable gloves
L340@P10: It is not mandatory for food handlers to use disposable gloves, although if used correctly they can assist
L341@P10: with minimising contamination.
L342@P10: When using disposable gloves, they must be:
L343@P10: • Only used for one continuous task and then discarded.
L344@P10: • Regularly changed to avoid cross-contamination – this is especially the case when changing from
L345@P10: preparing raw ingredients to handling ready-to-eat foods.
L346@P10: • Always discarded and not kept for later use once taken off.
L347@P10: • Removed and discarded before using the toilet, smoking, eating, drinking or touching the hair, scalp
L348@P10: or body.
L349@P10: Temperature control
L350@P10: • Temperature control throughout the operation is critical in minimising microbial growth, for example
L351@P10: raw egg product must be at or below 5˚C. This includes all operations during receipt, processing,
L352@P10: storage and display.
L353@P10: • If a raw egg product is out of temperature control (i.e. not at or below 5˚C), there must be
L354@P10: documented evidence that the ‘2-hour/4-hour’ rule is being met.
L355@P10: 2-hour/4-hour rule
L356@P10: When potentially hazardous ready-to-eat food has been kept between 5˚C and 60˚C for:
L357@P10: up to 2 hours, it can be refrigerated below 5˚C, or kept hot above 60˚C, or used
L358@P10: immediately,
L359@P10: between 2 and 4 hours, it must be used immediately, or
L360@P10: up to a total of 4 hours or more, it must be thrown out.
L361@P10: Safe Food Australia – Appendix 2 (2016)
L362@P10: For a more detailed information on the 2-hour/4-hour rule, refer to the ‘Guidance on the 2-hour/4-hour
L363@P10: rule’ document on the NSW Food Authority’s website www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/resource-centre.
L364@P10: Temperature measuring device
L365@P10: • Businesses handling potentially hazardous foods must have a temperature measuring device.
L366@P10: • Thermometers must be easily accessible and able to accurately measure temperatures to +/- 1°C.
L367@P10: • Thermometers should be calibrated to ensure accuracy.
L368@P10: • Appendix 4 provides information on calibrating thermometers.
L369@P10: Handling of eggs
L370@P10: Because eggs can be contaminated with Salmonella on the surface, the handling of eggs is critical.
L371@P10: When handling eggs, follow these guidelines:
L372@P10: • Do not use dirty, cracked or unstamped eggs.
L373@P10: • Do not wash eggs. Washing makes them more susceptible to contamination. Discard dirty and
L374@P10: cracked eggs.
L375@P10: • Visually inspect eggs before use to ensure there are no hairline cracks.
L376@P10: • Use raw egg pulp immediately – i.e. do not pool or store raw egg batches.
L377@P10: • Use a sanitised egg separator.
L378@P10-11: • Regularly prepare fresh batches of raw egg mixture:Food Safety Guidelines for the Preparation of Raw and Lightly Cooked Egg Products

L385@P11: Raw or lightly cooked egg products – process controls
L386@P11: If a food business decides to prepare raw egg containing foods that do not undergo a pathogen control
L387@P11: step, the foods should be prepared in line with safe food handling practices to slow the growth of
L388@P11: Salmonella and minimise the risk of foodborne illness.
L389@P11: A pathogen control step is a process such as cooking, pasteurisation or retorting to reduce any
L390@P11: pathogens that may be present in the food to safe levels.
L391@P11: Preparation of acidified raw egg products
L392@P11: Correct preparation of acidified raw egg product (using vinegar, lemon juice or other acids) improves
L393@P11: product safety:
L394@P11: — Product acidified to a pH of less than 4.2 inhibits the growth of pathogenic bacteria, including
L395@P11: Salmonella.
L396@P11: — Acidification should occur as part of the preparation step and should be checked to ensure proper
L397@P11: acidification has occurred. An example worksheet is provided in Appendix 1.
L398@P11: — It is important that the pH is measured and recorded as evidence to show that all practicable
L399@P11: measures are being taken to process safe and suitable food.
L400@P11: — Once acidified, the product must be covered when not being used.
L401@P11: — It is important that the product is kept at or below 5°C or that the ‘2-hour/4-hour’ rule is observed.
L402@P11: — Acidified product must be discarded at the end of the day and a new batch prepared daily.
L403@P11: Product must not be stored for longer than 24 hours.
L404@P11: Preparation of desserts
L405@P11: Using pH as a means of control may not work with desserts (although some desserts may include the
L406@P11: use of acids or lemon juice).
L407@P11: Other ways to help make non-acidified raw or lightly cooked egg desserts safer are to:
L408@P11: — Use eggs that are commercially pasteurised in shell, liquid, frozen or dried.
L409@P11: — Use eggs pasteurised in-house using a sous vide method or other in-house pasteurisation
L410@P11: process (see below).
L411@P11: — Consider the use of other controls such as lowering the water activity (aw).
L412@P11: In shell egg pasteurisation using a sous vide method
L413@P11: Eggs can be pasteurised in shell using a sous vide method. An example recipe for sous vide pasteurised
L414@P11: shell egg is to hold the eggs in a water bath at temperature of 57°C for at least 75 minutes (Baldwin,
L415@P11: 2022). The pasteurised eggs can then be used in sauces or desserts, just like raw eggs.
L416@P11: Correct procedures should be followed to ensure the eggs are safe to use:
L417@P11: — Every batch of sous vide cooking should be documented. An example worksheet is provided in
L418@P11: Appendix 2.

L490@P13: Storage and display of raw egg foods
L491@P13: Food must be stored and displayed correctly to prevent cross-contamination. This includes:
L492@P13: • Food must be stored in a way that is protected from contamination and in the condition that will not
L493@P13: adversely affect the safety and suitability of the food.
L494@P13: • Label the food with a ‘Use-by date’ to ensure only fresh batches are used.
L495@P13: • Do not top up or mix batches.
L496@P13: • For acidified raw egg products:
L497@P13: — make fresh batches daily,
L498@P13: — store at or less than 5°C, and
L499@P13: — discard at the end of the day or store no longer than 24 hours.
L500@P13: • For sous vide pasteurised eggs:
L501@P13: — store at or less than 5°C in their shells,
L502@P13: — label with the date of pasteurisation, and
L503@P13: — discard within 10 days of pasteurisation.
L504@P13: • If any raw egg product is brought out of refrigeration and held between 5° and 60°C, then the times
L505@P13: and temperatures should be documented to demonstrate compliance with the 2-hour/4-hour rule.
L506@P13: Businesses are strongly advised to keep raw egg products in a refrigerator or cool room at 5°C or
L507@P13: less when not being used for service.
L508@P13: Documentation
L509@P13: Each business preparing and selling raw or lightly cooked egg products should document the following
L510@P13: information. The information should be made available for verification by an authorised officer.
L511@P13: • Recipe of each raw or lightly cooked egg product – where possible, the recipe should aim to achieve
L512@P13: a pH of less than 4.2.
L513@P13: • Intended use – for example, salad dressing or dips etc.
L514@P13: • Name of the egg producer and supplier, delivery date of the eggs and the relevant use-by date or
L515@P13: batch codes.
L516@P13: • Name(s) of the chef/cook/maker of the raw egg product(s).
L517@P13: • Time and date of each batch of raw or lightly cooked egg product and the quantity – this information
L518@P13: should also be recorded on each container of the product made at that time.
L519@P13: • Storage conditions – raw egg products should be kept at 5°C or below at all times other than during
L520@P13: manufacture or serving; refrigerator temperatures should be checked and recorded daily with a

L548@P15: Appendix 1. Acidification of raw egg products
L549@P15: At pH values of 4.2 or less, pathogenic bacteria do not grow, form spores, or produce toxins. Lowering
L550@P15: the pH of a product to 4.2 or less could be used as a control measure for preventing the growth of
L551@P15: Salmonella.
L552@P15: Acidification of raw egg product using vinegar, lemon juice or other acids to a pH of 4.2 or less will stop
L553@P15: the growth of Salmonella bacteria but other bacteria, yeasts or moulds can still spoil the product.
L554@P15: The pH of the raw egg product must be checked to make sure it has reached the 4.2 limit.
L555@P15: Steps for measuring pH
L556@P15: The pH of a raw egg product can be measured using a pH meter, pH strips or pH paper, as follows:
L557@P15: • Once the raw egg product has been prepared, place a small sample (¼ cup) in a clean container.
L558@P15: • Dip the pH paper/strip directly into the raw egg product and compare with the colour chart.
L559@P15: • If using a pH meter, follow the manufacturer’s instructions.
L560@P15: • Record the pH on the ‘Raw egg product acidification’ check sheet.
L561@P15: • If the pH is greater than 4.2, add more vinegar, lemon juice or other acids to the product, and mix.
L562@P15: • Take another sample and repeat the pH reading.
L563@P15: • Continue adding vinegar, lemon juice or other acids until pH is less than 4.2. If extra vinegar, lemon
L564@P15: juice or other acids is needed, raw egg product recipes should be revised to account for the extra
L565@P15: vinegar, lemon juice or other acids required.

L631@P20: Appendix 4. Temperature calibration and measurement
L632@P20: Calibration procedures
L633@P20: Hand-held thermometers should be calibrated monthly, and results recorded on a checklist.
L634@P20: Ice Point (0°C)
L635@P20: 1. Fill a small container with crushed ice.
L636@P20: 2. Add a little water to the container to make an ice slurry.
L637@P20: 3. Place the thermometer in the centre of the container so that the point of the probe is in contact with
L638@P20: the ice.
L639@P20: 4. Allow the temperature reading of the thermometer to reach a steady reading.
L640@P20: 5. Record the reading and calculate the difference from 0°C.
L641@P20: 6. Thermometers with a deviation of more than 1°C should be discarded.
L642@P20: Boiling Water Point (100°C)
L643@P20: 1. Fill a small container with boiling water.
L644@P20: 2. Immediately place the thermometer in the centre of the container so that the point of the probe is in
L645@P20: the centre.
L646@P20: 3. Allow the temperature reading of the thermometer to reach a steady reading.
L647@P20: 4. Record the reading and calculate the difference from 100°C.
