L58@P3: Introduction
L59@P3: The NSW Food Authority (Food Authority) is the state government agency responsible for ensuring food
L60@P3: in NSW is safe and correctly labelled, and that consumers are able to make informed choices about the
L61@P3: food they eat.
L62@P3: Food Authority food safety officers and local government environmental health officers are authorised
L63@P3: officers under section 37 of the NSW Food Act 2003 and have wide powers, which include the power to
L64@P3: enter and inspect premises and inspect any records relating to the sale of food.
L65@P3: While the NSW Food Authority aims to educate industry and assist them with meeting their
L66@P3: responsibilities, under the Act the Food Authority can, and will, prosecute businesses if they fail to
L67@P3: comply.
L68@P3: This Guideline was developed by the Food Authority to assist seafood retailers in meeting the food
L69@P3: safety and labelling requirements outlined in the Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code (the
L70@P3: Code), which is law in Australia.
L71@P3: This document may not list all the requirements your business needs to comply with. Businesses should
L72@P3: refer to the NSW Food Act 2003, NSW Food Regulation 2015 and the Code for complete requirements.
L73@P3: NSW legislation can be found at legislation.nsw.gov.au and the Code at foodstandards.gov.au.
L74@P3: Purpose and scope
L75@P3: This guideline was designed specifically to assist seafood retailers in understanding the requirements of
L76@P3: the Code that are relevant to their business. It addresses food safety issues and labelling requirements
L77@P3: encountered by seafood retailers. It also addresses other issues relevant to seafood retailers, such as
L78@P3: notification requirements, product substitution, disposal, temporary events and charity fish auctions.
L79@P3: Certain seafood businesses, such as those involved in the wholesale and transport of seafood, must be
L80@P3: licensed with the NSW Food Authority under the seafood safety scheme, and must ensure their business
L81@P3: meets the requirements of the scheme, in addition to those outlined in this document.
L82@P3: Businesses that perform wet storage operations will be expected to implement controls that achieve the
L83@P3: requirements of the NSW Shellfish Industry Manual available at foodauthority.nsw.gov.au.
L84@P3: This guideline does not address occupational health and safety or environmental requirements.

L89@P4: Definitions
L90@P4: Batch
L91@P4: A quantity of bivalve molluscs which is harvested, depurated or handled from the same lease number
L92@P4: and with the same harvest date.
L93@P4: Clean
L94@P4: Clean to touch and free of extraneous visible matter and objectionable odour.
L95@P4: Control
L96@P4: A measure that prevents, eliminates or reduces to an acceptable level, a food safety hazard.
L97@P4: Depuration
L98@P4: A process using a controlled environment to reduce the level of certain pathogenic organisms that may
L99@P4: be present in live shellfish and crustaceans.
L100@P4: Food business
L101@P4: A business, enterprise or activity (other than primary food production) that involves:
L102@P4: a. the handling of food intended for sale; or
L103@P4: b. the sale of food;
L104@P4: regardless of whether the business, enterprise or activity concerned is of a commercial, charitable or
L105@P4: community nature or whether it involves the handling or sale of food on one occasion only.
L106@P4: Hazard
L107@P4: A biological, chemical or physical agent in, or condition of, food that has the potential to cause an
L108@P4: adverse health effect in humans.
L109@P4: Impervious
L110@P4: Will not absorb water, moisture or grease.
L111@P4: Potable water
L112@P4: Water that is acceptable for human consumption. The Australian Drinking Water Guidelines are used as
L113@P4: a reference guide for potable water.
L114@P4: Potentially hazardous food
L115@P4: Food that has to be kept at certain temperatures to minimise the growth of any pathogenic microorganisms that may be present in the food or to prevent the formation of toxins in the food.
L116@P4: Ready-to-eat food
L117@P4: Food that is ordinarily consumed in the same state as that in which it is sold and does not include nuts in
L118@P4: the shell and whole, raw fruits and vegetables that are intended for hulling, peeling or washing by the
L119@P4: consumer.
L120@P4: Sanitise
L121@P4: To apply heat or chemicals, heat and chemicals, or other processes, to a surface so that the number of
L122@P4: micro-organisms on the surface is reduced to a level that:
L123@P4: a. does not compromise the safety of food with which it may
L124@P4: b. come into contact; and
L125@P4: c. b. does not permit the transmission of infectious disease.
L126@P4: Seafood
L127@P4: All aquatic vertebrates and aquatic invertebrates intended for human consumption, but excludes
L128@P4: amphibians, mammals, reptiles, and aquatic plants.
L129@P4: Seafood business (Food Regulation 2015)
L130@P4: A business involved in the handling of seafood, including (but not limited to) the carrying on of any of the
L131@P4-5: following activities:Guideline for seafood retailers

L133@P5: a. the culture, harvesting and collecting of shellfish and gastropods,
L134@P5: b. the depuration of shellfish,
L135@P5: c. aquaculture,
L136@P5: d. the processing of seafood, including (but not limited to) skinning, gill and gutting, filleting,
L137@P5: shucking, cooking,
L138@P5: e. smoking, preserving and canning,
L139@P5: f. the packaging of seafood,
L140@P5: g. the storage of seafood,
L141@P5: h. the transportation of seafood,
L142@P5: i. the wholesaling of seafood.
L143@P5: It does not include the retail sale of seafood.
L144@P5: Shellfish
L145@P5: All edible species of molluscan bivalves such as oysters, clams, scallops (except when the consumed
L146@P5: product is only the adductor muscle), pipis and mussels, either shucked or in the shell, fresh or frozen,
L147@P5: whole or in part or processed.
L148@P5: Temperature control
L149@P5: Maintaining seafood at a temperature of:
L150@P5: a. 5°C or below if this is necessary to minimise the growth of infectious or toxigenic microorganisms
L151@P5: in the food so that the microbiological safety of the food will not be adversely affected for the time
L152@P5: the food is at that temperature; or
L153@P5: b. 60°C or above; or
L154@P5: c. another temperature – if the food business demonstrates that maintenance of the food at this
L155@P5: temperature for the period of time for which it will be so maintained, will not adversely affect the
L156@P5: microbiological safety of the food.
L157@P5: Wet storage
L158@P5: The temporary storage of shellfish in tanks containing natural seawater or saline water made from sea
L159@P5-6: salt and potable water. Wet storage may be used to store, remove sand or to add salt to shellfish.Guideline for seafood retailers

L161@P6: Notification of your food business
L162@P6: Seafood retailers need to notify their local council of their business and food activity details, such as
L163@P6: trading name, contact details, location(s), owner details and information indicating the nature of the
L164@P6: business.
L165@P6: Councils collect this information in different ways. You may need to apply for a service, permit or
L166@P6: approval, or complete a notification form. A good place to start is your local council’s website. Contact
L167@P6: them if you are unsure.
L168@P6: Businesses that wholesale seafood need to be licensed with the Food Authority. For more information on
L169@P6: licensing, visit foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/industry/seafood
L170@P6: Design and construction
L171@P6: Standard 3.2.3 of the Code outlines how your premises must be constructed and maintained. It is an
L172@P6: outcome-based standard that details requirements for floors, walls, ceilings, fixtures, fittings and
L173@P6: equipment and hand wash facilities.
L174@P6: When constructing a new premises you must contact your local council and it is recommended that you
L175@P6: refer to the document Australian Standard AS 4674-2004 Construction and Fitout of Food Premises.
L176@P6: This Standard can be purchased from Standards Australia (saiglobal.com/shop).
L177@P6: To comply with Standard 3.2.3 you will need to demonstrate the following:
L178@P6: • The layout of the premises should adequately separate food handling areas and equipment from
L179@P6: possible sources of contamination, for example wash up areas, garbage storage areas, and chemical
L180@P6: storage.
L181@P6: • The design and layout of the premises should allow for easy cleaning, that is, minimising cracks and
L182@P6: crevices and areas in which dirt and debris can accumulate.
L183@P6: • The construction of the premises should exclude dirt, dust and other contaminants and not harbour
L184@P6: or permit the entry of pests.
L185@P6: • Materials used in the construction of food premises should allow for easy cleaning and, if necessary,
L186@P6: sanitising. Materials used in food premises should be smooth and impervious to moisture, for
L187@P6: example stainless steel, or tiles. Bare timber items are not permitted as they are not impervious to
L188@P6: moisture and do not allow for easy cleaning and sanitising. Treated or sealed timber may comply with
L189@P6: requirements in the Code.
L190@P6: • Fixtures, fittings and equipment should be able to be easily and effectively cleaned and food contact
L191@P6: surfaces should be able to be sanitised if necessary.
L192@P6: • Designated (separate) hand wash facilities need to be located within areas where food handlers work
L193@P6: and adjacent to any toilets. Hand wash facilities need to be permanent fixtures and connected to a
L194@P6: supply of warm running potable water and provided with a supply of single-use towels and soap.
L195@P6: Note: This list is not exhaustive and there are other requirements listed in this Standard, for example
L196@P6: lighting and ventilation. Refer to SafeFood Australia – A Guide to the Food Safety Standards available at

L199@P7: Standard 3.2.2A – Food Safety Management Tools
L200@P7: Seafood retailers that sell or serve ready-to-eat food that requires temperature control must meet
L201@P7: requirements under Standard 3.2.2A of the Code.
L202@P7: The Standard classifies businesses into 2 categories, depending on their activities.
L203@P7: Category one
L204@P7: Businesses that process unpackaged, potentially hazardous food, into a food that is also potentially
L205@P7: hazardous and ready-to-eat, are required to:
L206@P7: • have a qualified Food Safety Supervisor (already a requirement for some businesses),
L207@P7: • ensure all food handlers have adequate skills and knowledge in food safety and hygiene, and
L208@P7: • be able to show their food is safe.
L209@P7: Category two
L210@P7: Businesses that only slice, weigh, repack, reheat or hot-hold ready-to-eat potentially hazardous food
L211@P7: they have not made themselves, are required to:
L212@P7: • have a qualified onsite Food Safety Supervisor
L213@P7: • ensure all food handlers have adequate skills and knowledge in food safety and hygiene.
L214@P7: More information on each requirement is outlined below. See also foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/safetytools.
L215@P7: Food Safety Supervisor
L216@P7: Seafood retailers will need to appoint a certified Food Safety Supervisor (FSS) in their business if the
L217@P7: food they prepare and serve is:
L218@P7: • unpackaged
L219@P7: • ready-to-eat
L220@P7: • potentially hazardous (needs temperature control).
L221@P7: Food Safety Supervisors oversee day-to-day food handling operations and ensure risks are managed.
L222@P7: They must be certified within the past 5 years and have skills and knowledge in food safety, especially
L223@P7: around high-risk foods. FSS training is delivered by approved Registered Training Organisations (RTOs).
L224@P7: See foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/fss for more information.
L225@P7: Food handler training
L226@P7: Seafood retailers selling unpackaged, potentially hazardous, ready-to-eat food, must ensure their food
L227@P7: handlers have skills and knowledge in food safety and food hygiene.
L228@P7: To meet their obligations, businesses can choose how food handlers are trained, but the training should
L229@P7: cover:
L230@P7: • safe handling of food
L231@P7: • understanding of food contamination
L232@P7: • cleaning and sanitising of food premises and equipment
L233@P7-8: • personal hygiene. Guideline for seafood retailers
L234@P8: FA613/2402 9
L235@P8: Various online food safety training programs, courses from vocational training providers, past
L236@P8: experience, or training developed by the business, may be used or recognised.
L237@P8: Additional training may not be needed if food handlers can already demonstrate adequate skills and
L238@P8: knowledge in the above areas.
L239@P8: For more information on the requirements, and to access the Food Authority’s free food handler training
L240@P8: course, see foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/handlerbasics
L241@P8: Showing food is safe
L242@P8: Food businesses that process unpackaged, potentially hazardous food into a food that is ready-to-eat
L243@P8: and potentially hazardous, must be able to show their food is safe under Standard 3.2.2A of the Code.
L244@P8: These businesses must be able to demonstrate they are monitoring and managing key food safety risks
L245@P8: related to receiving, storing, processing, displaying and transporting potentially hazardous food, and
L246@P8: cleaning and sanitising.
L247@P8: The requirements can be met by either:
L248@P8: • demonstrating safe food handling practices, such as having a written instruction sheet or standard
L249@P8: operating procedure, and/or being able to walk and talk an authorised officer through their food
L250@P8: handling practices, or
L251@P8: • keeping records.
L252@P8: For more information on this requirement, go to Showing food is safe.
L253@P8: Health and hygiene of food handlers
L254@P8: Standard 3.2.2 Clauses 13-18 of the Code outline the health and hygiene requirements for food
L255@P8: handlers. These include food handlers notifying supervisors of any disease or illness they may be
L256@P8: suffering from as well as hand washing and personal hygiene requirements.
L257@P8: To comply with this Standard you and your staff will need to demonstrate the following:
L258@P8: • Staff have the required skills in, and knowledge of food safety and food hygiene relative to their work
L259@P8: activities. These skills may include good hand washing practices, knowledge of how to safely handle
L260@P8: food, knowledge of how to avoid the risk of cross contamination and knowledge of temperature
L261@P8: control.
L262@P8: • Staff demonstrate good hand washing practices.
L263@P8: • Staff are not permitted to smoke in food handling areas.
L264@P8: • Staff with communicable illnesses do not work or are assigned to non-food handling areas and are
L265@P8: assigned to non-food handling duties.
L266@P8: • Staff have clean uniforms or clothing.
L267@P8: • Utensils or other suitable methods are used when handling food to minimise the risk of cross
L268@P8: contamination.
L269@P8: • The hand washing facilities have soap, paper towel (or other approved means) and warm running
L270@P8: potable water.
L271@P8: • A probe thermometer that can be inserted into the food must be readily available and accurate to +/-
L272@P8: 1°C. The temperature of potentially hazardous food must be monitored regularly and temperatures
L273@P8-9: recorded. Guideline for seafood retailers

L275@P9: Purchasing and receiving food safely
L276@P9: Standard 3.2.2 of the Code outlines the measures a business must take to help ensure that food is
L277@P9: received safely. These requirements include ensuring the food has been protected from likely
L278@P9: contamination, food is received at the correct temperature and information is available on who has
L279@P9: supplied or manufactured the food and what the food is.
L280@P9: The following points will help you demonstrate compliance with this Standard:
L281@P9: • The temperature of potentially hazardous food, such as seafood, should be checked upon receipt
L282@P9: and only accepted if the temperature is equal to or below 5°C. This is generally only possible if the
L283@P9: transport vehicle is refrigerated or sufficient ice has been used.
L284@P9: • Frozen product deliveries should only be accepted if the product is hard frozen (no thawing).
L285@P9: • Food should only be accepted if there is no evidence of contamination of the product, for example,
L286@P9: packaging is undamaged and ready-to-eat seafood has not been in contact with raw seafood.
L287@P9: • Sufficient information should be supplied with the product to identify who has supplied,
L288@P9: manufactured, packed or imported it, as well as identifying what the product is.
L289@P9: • Under the Food Regulation 2015, NSW seafood wholesalers (refer to definition of seafood business
L290@P9: on page 5-6) are required to be licensed with the Food Authority. You can check that your supplier is
L291@P9: licensed by requesting a copy of their Food Authority licence. Seafood caught by recreational fishers
L292@P9: (including fish from charity fish auctions) cannot be sold commercially.
L293@P9: • You must be able to demonstrate who you purchased food from. You must keep information relating
L294@P9: to the name and business address of the vendor, manufacturer or packer (or if the food is imported,
L295@P9: the name and business of the importer) of any food for sale.
L296@P9: Safe storage of seafood
L297@P9: Standard 3.2.2 Clause 6 of the Code outlines the requirements for storing food safely. These
L298@P9: requirements are to protect food from contamination and store the food under temperature control.
L299@P9: The following practices will help you to demonstrate compliance with this Standard for
L300@P9: the storage of seafood, other than live seafood:
L301@P9: • Raw and ready-to-eat food (including oysters) should be stored separately to ensure no contact
L302@P9: between raw and cooked product. It is best practice to store raw food below ready-to-eat food to
L303@P9: minimise the risk of cross contamination through dripping blood or juices.
L304@P9: • Product in storage should be covered to help minimise the risks of cross contamination.
L305@P9: • Seafood needs to be stored at temperatures equal to or below 5°C. This can be achieved through
L306@P9: active refrigeration such as a fridge or coolroom and with the assistance of ice.
L307@P9: • Frozen seafood needs to remain ‘hard frozen’ during storage.
L308@P9: • Use a clean and sanitised thermometer at least daily to verify that product temperatures are equal to
L309@P9: or below 5°C.
L310@P9: • Do not overload coolrooms or fridges, allow for sufficient air flow around the food to cool it down.
L311@P9: • To assist the cooling process it may be necessary to remove food from foam boxes when stored in
L312@P9: the coolroom or fridge.
L313@P9: • Ensure that food and food contact surfaces do not come into contact with the floor of the coolroom,
L314@P9: for example, store food above the floor on shelving.
L315@P9-10: • Ensure waste products are stored in clearly labelled bins or tubs and away from edible products. Guideline for seafood retailers

L317@P10: Storage of live seafood
L318@P10: Live seafood must be stored in such a way that the conditions under which it is stored will not adversely
L319@P10: affect the safety and suitability of the seafood. Potential hazards to live seafood include temperature
L320@P10: abuse and inappropriate environmental conditions, for example water quality, pH, salinity, humidity,
L321@P10: overcrowding and packaging conditions.
L322@P10: Seafood that is stored live includes fish such as Barramundi, Silver Perch, and reef fish; crustaceans
L323@P10: such as rock lobsters and crabs, and molluscs such as abalone, mussels and oysters.
L324@P10: Refer to Safe Seafood Australia – A Guide to Standard 4.2.1 Primary Production and Processing
L325@P10: Standard for Seafood (available at foodstandards.gov.au/publications/safeseafoodaustralia) and Master
L326@P10: Fish Merchant Association (MFMA) Code of Conduct for the Welfare on Live Seafood (available from
L327@P10: mfma.com.au/industry-issues/code-for-live-seafood) for specific requirements for storage of live seafood.
L328@P10: Wet storage of shellfish
L329@P10: The wet storage of bivalve molluscs is considered a high-risk activity and therefore extra precautions
L330@P10: should be taken to ensure they are safe to consume. Bivalve molluscs feed by drawing water across the
L331@P10: gill surface, which traps particles from the water and transports them to the mouth for ingestion. As they
L332@P10: feed, bacteria, viruses and harmful substances that are in the water will be accumulated in the shellfish.
L333@P10: See the Food Authority’s Food Safety Guidelines for the wet storage of shellfish at retail (PDF, 53 KB)
L334@P10: for basic measures to prevent contamination of bivalve molluscs during wet storage.
L335@P10: Wholesale wet storage of shellfish requires additional measures and more formal requirements. New
L336@P10: requirements for wholesale wet storage are in development and expected to be published in mid-2024.
L337@P10: Use of ice in retail seafood premises
L338@P10: When using ice in retail seafood premises to help maintain temperature control of food, ensure that the
L339@P10: ice is made from potable water (such as town water).
L340@P10: If you make your own ice, ensure the ice machine is cleaned out regularly and sanitised. Ensure filters
L341@P10: are also cleaned regularly.
L342@P10: If purchasing ice from a supplier ensure that the ice is ‘food grade’.
L343@P10: As ice comes into contact with the food ensure it is handled hygienically. Ice should not be reused.
L344@P10: Histamine
L345@P10: Histamine fish poisoning (or Scromboid poisoning) is a type of food poisoning caused by elevated levels
L346@P10: of histamine being present in the fish.
L347@P10: Naturally occurring bacteria in fish produce an enzyme which converts histadine in the fish to histamine.
L348@P10: Histamine fish poisoning usually occurs in certain species of fish, such as tuna, sardines, mackerel,
L349@P10: swordfish and marlin.
L350@P10: Histamine is not destroyed by cooking. Therefore, the best way to keep histamine at a minimum is to
L351@P10: ensure proper temperature control.
L352@P10: In some cases, low levels of histamine may already be present in the fish when you receive it. To
L353@P10: minimise histamine formation, you should always:
L354@P10: • purchase from reputable suppliers who store the fish on ice or under refrigeration
L355@P10: • receive product at refrigeration temperatures (equal to or below 5°C)
L356@P10: • place the fish under refrigeration as soon as it is received
L357@P10: • keep the fish at refrigerated temperatures when not being used
L358@P10-11: • if the fish is frozen, thaw the fish under refrigeration.Guideline for seafood retailers
L359@P11: FA613/2402 12
L360@P11: When displaying fish for sale, always ensure there is enough ice on the product and refrigeration units
L361@P11: are set at a temperature equal to or below 5°C.
L362@P11: For more information on histamine refer to the Controlling Histamine Food Poisoning’ fact sheet (PDF,
L363@P11: 203 KB).
L364@P11: Seafood processing
L365@P11: Standard 3.2.2 Clause 7 of the Code outlines the requirements for handling and processing food safely.
L366@P11: Processing includes steps such as thawing, filleting, gilling and gutting, cooking and marinading.
L367@P11: The following practices will help you to demonstrate compliance with this Standard:
L368@P11: • It is best practice to process ready-to-eat product in a designated area separate from where raw
