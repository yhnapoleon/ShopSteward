L216@P7: Seafood retailers will need to appoint a certified Food Safety Supervisor (FSS) in their business if the
L217@P7: food they prepare and serve is:
L218@P7: • unpackaged
L219@P7: • ready-to-eat

L241@P8: Showing food is safe
L242@P8: Food businesses that process unpackaged, potentially hazardous food into a food that is ready-to-eat
L243@P8: and potentially hazardous, must be able to show their food is safe under Standard 3.2.2A of the Code.
L244@P8: These businesses must be able to demonstrate they are monitoring and managing key food safety risks
L245@P8: related to receiving, storing, processing, displaying and transporting potentially hazardous food, and
L246@P8: cleaning and sanitising.
L247@P8: The requirements can be met by either:
L248@P8: • demonstrating safe food handling practices, such as having a written instruction sheet or standard
L249@P8: operating procedure, and/or being able to walk and talk an authorised officer through their food
L250@P8: handling practices, or
L251@P8: • keeping records.
L252@P8: For more information on this requirement, go to Showing food is safe.
L253@P8: Health and hygiene of food handlers
L254@P8: Standard 3.2.2 Clauses 13-18 of the Code outline the health and hygiene requirements for food
L255@P8: handlers. These include food handlers notifying supervisors of any disease or illness they may be
L256@P8: suffering from as well as hand washing and personal hygiene requirements.
L257@P8: To comply with this Standard you and your staff will need to demonstrate the following:
L258@P8: • Staff have the required skills in, and knowledge of food safety and food hygiene relative to their work
L259@P8: activities. These skills may include good hand washing practices, knowledge of how to safely handle
L260@P8: food, knowledge of how to avoid the risk of cross contamination and knowledge of temperature
L261@P8: control.
L262@P8: • Staff demonstrate good hand washing practices.
L263@P8: • Staff are not permitted to smoke in food handling areas.
L264@P8: • Staff with communicable illnesses do not work or are assigned to non-food handling areas and are
L265@P8: assigned to non-food handling duties.
L266@P8: • Staff have clean uniforms or clothing.
L267@P8: • Utensils or other suitable methods are used when handling food to minimise the risk of cross
L268@P8: contamination.
L269@P8: • The hand washing facilities have soap, paper towel (or other approved means) and warm running
L270@P8: potable water.
L271@P8: • A probe thermometer that can be inserted into the food must be readily available and accurate to +/-

L275@P9: Purchasing and receiving food safely
L276@P9: Standard 3.2.2 of the Code outlines the measures a business must take to help ensure that food is
L277@P9: received safely. These requirements include ensuring the food has been protected from likely
L278@P9: contamination, food is received at the correct temperature and information is available on who has
L279@P9: supplied or manufactured the food and what the food is.
L280@P9: The following points will help you demonstrate compliance with this Standard:
L281@P9: • The temperature of potentially hazardous food, such as seafood, should be checked upon receipt
L282@P9: and only accepted if the temperature is equal to or below 5°C. This is generally only possible if the
L283@P9: transport vehicle is refrigerated or sufficient ice has been used.
L284@P9: • Frozen product deliveries should only be accepted if the product is hard frozen (no thawing).
L285@P9: • Food should only be accepted if there is no evidence of contamination of the product, for example,
L286@P9: packaging is undamaged and ready-to-eat seafood has not been in contact with raw seafood.
L287@P9: • Sufficient information should be supplied with the product to identify who has supplied,
L288@P9: manufactured, packed or imported it, as well as identifying what the product is.
L289@P9: • Under the Food Regulation 2015, NSW seafood wholesalers (refer to definition of seafood business
L290@P9: on page 5-6) are required to be licensed with the Food Authority. You can check that your supplier is
L291@P9: licensed by requesting a copy of their Food Authority licence. Seafood caught by recreational fishers
L292@P9: (including fish from charity fish auctions) cannot be sold commercially.
L293@P9: • You must be able to demonstrate who you purchased food from. You must keep information relating
L294@P9: to the name and business address of the vendor, manufacturer or packer (or if the food is imported,
L295@P9: the name and business of the importer) of any food for sale.
L296@P9: Safe storage of seafood
L297@P9: Standard 3.2.2 Clause 6 of the Code outlines the requirements for storing food safely. These
L298@P9: requirements are to protect food from contamination and store the food under temperature control.
L299@P9: The following practices will help you to demonstrate compliance with this Standard for
L300@P9: the storage of seafood, other than live seafood:
L301@P9: • Raw and ready-to-eat food (including oysters) should be stored separately to ensure no contact
L302@P9: between raw and cooked product. It is best practice to store raw food below ready-to-eat food to
L303@P9: minimise the risk of cross contamination through dripping blood or juices.
L304@P9: • Product in storage should be covered to help minimise the risks of cross contamination.
L305@P9: • Seafood needs to be stored at temperatures equal to or below 5°C. This can be achieved through
L306@P9: active refrigeration such as a fridge or coolroom and with the assistance of ice.
L307@P9: • Frozen seafood needs to remain ‘hard frozen’ during storage.
L308@P9: • Use a clean and sanitised thermometer at least daily to verify that product temperatures are equal to
L309@P9: or below 5°C.
L310@P9: • Do not overload coolrooms or fridges, allow for sufficient air flow around the food to cool it down.
L311@P9: • To assist the cooling process it may be necessary to remove food from foam boxes when stored in
L312@P9: the coolroom or fridge.
L313@P9: • Ensure that food and food contact surfaces do not come into contact with the floor of the coolroom,
L314@P9: for example, store food above the floor on shelving.
L315@P9-10: • Ensure waste products are stored in clearly labelled bins or tubs and away from edible products. Guideline for seafood retailers

L317@P10: Storage of live seafood
L318@P10: Live seafood must be stored in such a way that the conditions under which it is stored will not adversely
L319@P10: affect the safety and suitability of the seafood. Potential hazards to live seafood include temperature
L320@P10: abuse and inappropriate environmental conditions, for example water quality, pH, salinity, humidity,
L321@P10: overcrowding and packaging conditions.
L322@P10: Seafood that is stored live includes fish such as Barramundi, Silver Perch, and reef fish; crustaceans
L323@P10: such as rock lobsters and crabs, and molluscs such as abalone, mussels and oysters.
L324@P10: Refer to Safe Seafood Australia – A Guide to Standard 4.2.1 Primary Production and Processing
L325@P10: Standard for Seafood (available at foodstandards.gov.au/publications/safeseafoodaustralia) and Master
L326@P10: Fish Merchant Association (MFMA) Code of Conduct for the Welfare on Live Seafood (available from
L327@P10: mfma.com.au/industry-issues/code-for-live-seafood) for specific requirements for storage of live seafood.
L328@P10: Wet storage of shellfish
L329@P10: The wet storage of bivalve molluscs is considered a high-risk activity and therefore extra precautions
L330@P10: should be taken to ensure they are safe to consume. Bivalve molluscs feed by drawing water across the
L331@P10: gill surface, which traps particles from the water and transports them to the mouth for ingestion. As they
L332@P10: feed, bacteria, viruses and harmful substances that are in the water will be accumulated in the shellfish.
L333@P10: See the Food Authority’s Food Safety Guidelines for the wet storage of shellfish at retail (PDF, 53 KB)
L334@P10: for basic measures to prevent contamination of bivalve molluscs during wet storage.
L335@P10: Wholesale wet storage of shellfish requires additional measures and more formal requirements. New
L336@P10: requirements for wholesale wet storage are in development and expected to be published in mid-2024.
L337@P10: Use of ice in retail seafood premises
L338@P10: When using ice in retail seafood premises to help maintain temperature control of food, ensure that the
L339@P10: ice is made from potable water (such as town water).
L340@P10: If you make your own ice, ensure the ice machine is cleaned out regularly and sanitised. Ensure filters
L341@P10: are also cleaned regularly.
L342@P10: If purchasing ice from a supplier ensure that the ice is ‘food grade’.
L343@P10: As ice comes into contact with the food ensure it is handled hygienically. Ice should not be reused.
L344@P10: Histamine
L345@P10: Histamine fish poisoning (or Scromboid poisoning) is a type of food poisoning caused by elevated levels
L346@P10: of histamine being present in the fish.
L347@P10: Naturally occurring bacteria in fish produce an enzyme which converts histadine in the fish to histamine.
L348@P10: Histamine fish poisoning usually occurs in certain species of fish, such as tuna, sardines, mackerel,
L349@P10: swordfish and marlin.
L350@P10: Histamine is not destroyed by cooking. Therefore, the best way to keep histamine at a minimum is to
L351@P10: ensure proper temperature control.
L352@P10: In some cases, low levels of histamine may already be present in the fish when you receive it. To
L353@P10: minimise histamine formation, you should always:
L354@P10: • purchase from reputable suppliers who store the fish on ice or under refrigeration
L355@P10: • receive product at refrigeration temperatures (equal to or below 5°C)
L356@P10: • place the fish under refrigeration as soon as it is received
L357@P10: • keep the fish at refrigerated temperatures when not being used
L358@P10-11: • if the fish is frozen, thaw the fish under refrigeration.Guideline for seafood retailers
L359@P11: FA613/2402 12
L360@P11: When displaying fish for sale, always ensure there is enough ice on the product and refrigeration units
L361@P11: are set at a temperature equal to or below 5°C.
L362@P11: For more information on histamine refer to the Controlling Histamine Food Poisoning’ fact sheet (PDF,
L363@P11: 203 KB).
L364@P11: Seafood processing
L365@P11: Standard 3.2.2 Clause 7 of the Code outlines the requirements for handling and processing food safely.
L366@P11: Processing includes steps such as thawing, filleting, gilling and gutting, cooking and marinading.
L367@P11: The following practices will help you to demonstrate compliance with this Standard:
L368@P11: • It is best practice to process ready-to-eat product in a designated area separate from where raw
L369@P11: product is handled to reduce the risk of cross contamination. Designated knives, chopping boards
L370@P11: and tongs are advisable for the handling of ready-to-eat product.
L371@P11: • Ensure all equipment such as benches, knives, chopping boards and display trays, are cleaned and
L372@P11: sanitised prior to use (particularly if processing ready-to-eat product).
L373@P11: • If thawing frozen product, ensure that the time the product is out of refrigeration is minimised. It is
L374@P11: best practice to thaw frozen ready-to-eat products, for example smoked salmon and prawns, in the
L375@P11: coolroom (see the Safe Food Australia guide for further information on safe methods of thawing).
L376@P11: • Minimise the time seafood is kept out of refrigeration while being processed. Only bring out of the
L377@P11: coolroom enough product that can be processed within 2 hours.
L378@P11: • Ensure chemicals, garbage bins and other contaminants are kept away from seafood being
L379@P11: processed.
L380@P11: • When cooking seafood, ensure the product is thoroughly cooked. The flesh should be white and set.
L381@P11: A clean and sanitised thermometer can be used to confirm that the temperature in the centre of the
L382@P11: product has reached 65°C.
L383@P11: • When cooling cooked ready-to-eat product the food needs to be cooled quickly. The food must be
L384@P11: cooled from its cooking temperature to below 21°C within 2 hrs and from 21°C to equal to or below
L385@P11: 5°C within a further 4 hours.
L386@P11: • See the additional section below for safe processing of oysters.
L387@P11: Chapter 1.3 in the Code, Substances added to food, sets out what substances can be added to food and
L388@P11: how much can be added.
L389@P11: The receival, storage and use of these substances, as well as other ingredients, must also comply with
L390@P11: the requirements of the Code. It is important to note that some ingredients may not be considered
L391@P11: ‘potentially hazardous’ when first received, for example unopened marinade, but once opened may be
L392@P11: deemed ‘potentially hazardous’ and therefore must be stored under temperature control.
L393@P11: Displaying seafood safely
L394@P11: Standard 3.2.2 Clause 8 of the Code outlines the minimum requirements for the displaying of seafood.
L395@P11: This clause requires you to take all practicable measures to protect the food from likely contamination
L396@P11: and to display the food under temperature control.
L397@P11: The following practices will help you demonstrate compliance with this clause:
L398@P11: • Seafood must be displayed under temperature control. This means that seafood on display must be
L399@P11: maintained equal to or below 5°C using refrigeration or sufficient ice. This also means that frozen
L400@P11-12: product is displayed ‘hard frozen’ with no evidence of thawing. Guideline for seafood retailers
L401@P12: FA613/2402 13
L402@P12: • Do not overload display cases.
L403@P12: • Raw seafood needs to be adequately separated from ready-to-eat seafood while on display to
L404@P12: prevent cross contamination of ready-to-eat product with juices and/ or contaminated ice. This can be
L405@P12: achieved by using Perspex dividers or separate containers.
L406@P12: • Separate utensils need to be used for handling raw and ready-to-eat product to minimise the risk
L407@P12: cross contamination.
L408@P12: • Material used to display ready-to-eat seafood must be clean and free from contamination and made
L409@P12: of smooth, impervious material. Foam fish box lids are not impervious and can absorb contaminants
L410@P12: such as raw product juices, therefore should not be used to display ready-to-eat product.
L411@P12: • See the additional section of labelling of displayed seafood.
L412@P12: Raw whole fish may be stored in tubs on ice inside the premises, however all other product such as
L413@P12: fillets and ready-to-eat product must be stored in refrigerated display cabinets. All product must be
L414@P12: adequately protected from contamination, such as from customers, insects or dust, and stored under
L415@P12: temperature control.
L416@P12: Cleaning and sanitation
L417@P12: Standard 3.2.2 Clauses 19 and 20 of the Code outline the requirements for cleaning and sanitising for
L418@P12: food businesses. These clauses require you to maintain the food premises and equipment to a standard
L419@P12: of cleanliness where there is no accumulation of garbage, dirt, food waste, grease or any visible matter.
L420@P12: It also requires you to sanitise the food contact surfaces of equipment and utensils. Sanitising occurs
L421@P12: after cleaning, and requires the application of heat and/or chemicals to equipment so that the number of
L422@P12: bacteria are reduced to an acceptable level.
L423@P12: To comply with this Standard you will need to demonstrate the following:
L424@P12: • All walls, floors, ceilings, coolrooms, freezers, drains and other fixtures are clean and free of visible
L425@P12: food scraps, mould, rust, grease, rubbish and other debris.
L426@P12: • All benches, ice machines, display cases, chopping boards, utensils and other equipment are clean
L427@P12: and free of visible food scraps, mould, rust, grease, rubbish and other debris.
L428@P12: • You have implemented a method of sanitising food contact surfaces, for example knives, chopping
L429@P12: boards, tubs and benches. Chemical sanitisers can be used for this purpose; provided the label
L430@P12: states that they are suitable for use in food preparation areas. Sanitising of food contact surfaces will
L431@P12: only be effective if the surface has been thoroughly cleaned first.
L432@P12: • Ensure all cleaning chemicals are suitable for use in a food preparation area. Check also whether the
L433@P12-13: cleaning chemical needs to be rinsed off prior to food preparation commencing. Guideline for seafood retailers

L435@P13: Labelling of seafood
L436@P13: The Code outlines the requirements for labelling of food for retail sale and wholesale. Labelling
L437@P13: requirements vary depending on the nature of the food and whether the food is packaged or
L438@P13: unpackaged.
L439@P13: There are several fact sheets regarding labelling available at foodauthority.nsw.gov.au. Should you
L440@P13: require additional information on how to comply with the labelling requirements of the Code, you can
L441@P13: contact the Food Authority on 1300 552 406.
L442@P13: You will need to demonstrate the following when labelling product in your premises:
L443@P13: Unpackaged seafood
L444@P13: • All unpackaged seafood for retail sale on your premises (includes both fresh and processed seafood)
L445@P13: must include a label indicating the country or countries where the seafood was harvested, processed
L446@P13: or manufactured (Country of origin labelling), for example Vietnamese Basa must be labelled
L447@P13: ‘Product of Vietnam’. (Country of origin labelling is administered under Australian Consumer Law.
L448@P13: See: https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/advertising-and-promotions/country-of-origin-claims.)
L449@P13: • Product harvested, processed or manufactured in Australia is also subject to Country of Origin
L450@P13: labelling requirements.
L451@P13: • Oysters must be labelled with the name of the harvest area. It is also recommended that labelling
L452@P13: include the estuary name.
L453@P13: • The label must be in English, be clearly visible to customers and the lettering on the label must be a
L454@P13: minimum of 9mm in height. If the seafood is in an ‘assisted service’ refrigerated display cabinet (an
L455@P13: enclosed or partially enclosed cabinet that requires a person to serve the food to the purchaser), the
L456@P13: lettering must be a minimum of 5mm in height.
L457@P13: • If the seafood contains more than 1 ingredient, indicate that the seafood is a mix of local and/ or
L458@P13: imported ingredients*.
L459@P13: • If the seafood is mixed with other ingredients, for example it is crumbed, pickled or marinated,
L460@P13: indicate that the seafood is a mix of local and/ or imported ingredients*.
L461@P13: *Please note that the order or local/ imported ingredients on your label depends on which of these
L462@P13: comprises the major part, for example if there is more imported product, then the sign should say that
L463@P13: this is a mix of imported and local ingredients.
L464@P13: Packaged food
L465@P13: • All packaged food for retail sale needs to be correctly labelled (food that is made and packaged on
L466@P13: the premises is exempt). Information that needs to be included on the label of packaged food
L467@P13: includes; name of food, lot or batch number (a date mark is sufficient), date mark (‘use by’ – or ‘best
L468@P13: before’), name and address of supplier, ingredients in order of ingoing weight, nutrition information
L469@P13: panel, country of origin and any relevant warning statements about the presence of allergens.
L470@P13: • Be aware that any food manufactured elsewhere that is taken out of its original packaging and
L471@P13: repackaged on the premises will require a label.

L479@P14: Safe processing of oysters
L480@P14: Oysters are considered a particularly high-risk food as they are a raw, ready-to-eat product and as such
L481@P14: may not undergo any cooking step prior to being eaten.
L482@P14: The absence of a cooking step (which normally kills any food poisoning bacteria) means that oysters
L483@P14: must be handled and opened hygienically with good temperature control being maintained throughout
L484@P14: storage, processing and display.
L485@P14: The Product Record (PR) number is a unique number that traces oysters back to a location, time and
L486@P14: date of harvest. If you open and package oysters, this number can be very useful to maintain traceability
L487@P14: and demonstrate the oysters you sell are safe.
L488@P14: The following are best practice procedures for handling and opening of oysters:
L489@P14: Receival
L490@P14: • Only purchase and receive oysters from an oyster farmer or processor who is licensed with the Food
L491@P14: Authority. If purchasing oysters from another state, the business must comply with the requirements
L492@P14: of that state.
L493@P14: • Ensure that all unopened oysters received from NSW suppliers are labelled with the batch number or
L494@P14: PR number, date of harvest or date of depuration/wet storage, farmer’s name and address, estuary
L495@P14: of harvest and lease numbers, the species of oyster and the storage requirements for that product
L496@P14: (for traceability purposes it is important to keep copies of this information, for example invoices).
L497@P14: • Unopened Sydney Rock oysters are to be received at less than 25°C if received within 72 hours of
L498@P14: harvest and at less than 21°C once 72 hours has elapsed. All other unopened bivalve shellfish are to
L499@P14: be received at less than 10°C if 24 hours has elapsed since harvest.
L500@P14: Storage
L501@P14: • Unopened Sydney Rock oysters are to be stored at less than 21°C. All other unopened bivalve
L502@P14: shellfish are to be received and stored at less than 10°C.
L503@P14: • Opened oysters need to be stored equal to or below 5°C.
L504@P14: • Unopened oysters should be stored away from pooling water which may cause them to open or
L505@P14: gape. Unopened oysters should be stored away from other raw product.
L506@P14: • Opened oysters need to be stored away from raw product to minimise the risk of cross
L507@P14: contamination.
L508@P14: • If storing multiple batches of unopened or opened oysters, it is best practice to ensure that each
L509@P14: batch is labelled with the PR number.
L510@P14: Processing
L511@P14: • To assist with traceability, it is best practice for only one batch (PR number) of oysters to be
L512@P14: processed and displayed at a time.
L513@P14: • Ensure all benches, aprons, opening blocks, knives, display trays etc are cleaned and sanitised
L514@P14: before use.
L515@P14: • Ensure the processor washes their hands thoroughly.
L516@P14: • When rinsing oysters after opening, oysters should be rinsed under running, potable (town) water to
L517@P14: remove any dirt from the exterior of the shell that may contaminate the product. (Dipping oysters into
L518@P14: a bucket, bowl or sink of water has been shown to increase the risk of contamination by food
L519@P14: poisoning bacteria and therefore this practice does not comply with the requirements of the Code). A
L520@P14-15: shower rose fitting to the opening sink is recommended for rinsing oysters. Guideline for seafood retailers
L521@P15: FA613/2402 16
L522@P15: • Opened oysters should not be stacked on top of each other (as there is a risk of contamination of the
L523@P15: oyster meat). If layering oysters on trays it is recommended that food grade paper and plastic sheets
L524@P15: be used in between layers.
L525@P15: • Once opened, oysters need to be stored or displayed equal to or below 5°C.
L526@P15: Display
L527@P15: • Opened oysters must be displayed equal to or below 5°C (sufficient ice may be required around the
L528@P15: oyster to help achieve this temperature).
L529@P15: • Opened oysters need to be adequately separated from raw product when on display to minimise the
L530@P15: risk of cross contamination.
L531@P15: Disposal of seafood
L532@P15: Food that is unsafe or unsuitable for human consumption must not be sold and must not contaminate
L533@P15: safe and suitable seafood. ‘Food for disposal’ refers to product that has been recalled or returned, or is
L534@P15: unsafe or unsuitable seafood.
L535@P15: To comply with Standard 3.2.2 of the Code you will need to demonstrate the following:
L536@P15: A food business must ensure that ‘food for disposal’ is held and kept separate until it is:
L537@P15: • destroyed or otherwise used or disposed of so that it cannot be used for human consumption
L538@P15: • returned to its supplier
L539@P15: • processed in a way that ensures its safety and suitability
L540@P15: • ascertained to be safe and suitable for sale.
L541@P15: To comply with this standard, the business must also clearly identify any seafood that is held and kept
L542@P15: separate for the reasons above.
L543@P15: Food transportation
L544@P15: Vehicles used to transport seafood must be designed and constructed to protect seafood from
L545@P15: contamination and be able to be effectively cleaned.
L546@P15: • Floors and walls of vehicle to be smooth, impervious with no open joints.
L547@P15: • Coolroom panels or insulated fibreglass for walls with aluminium plate or fibreglass floors are
L548@P15: suitable.
L549@P15: The Code requires food to be transported under temperature control. Temperature control requires
L550@P15: product to be transported equal to or below 5°C, or if the product is frozen it is to remain hard frozen
L551@P15: during transport. This can be achieved by using a refrigerated transport vehicle and/ or using sufficient
L552@P15: quantities of ice.
L553@P15: Food must also be protected from birds, insects, dust and other sources of contamination during
L554@P15: transport. This can be achieved by ensuring food is adequately covered during loading, transport and
L555@P15-16: unloading. Guideline for seafood retailers

L557@P16: Temporary events and mobile vendors
L558@P16: A temporary event is any occasion which is not permanent in nature, where food or produce for human
L559@P16: consumption is sold or offered for sale to consumers. Examples include but are not limited to fairs,
L560@P16: festivals, farmers’ markets, shows and markets.

L568@P16: Charity fish auctions
L569@P16: Recreational fishers in NSW are not permitted to sell or auction their catch under the Fisheries
L570@P16: Management Act 1994. However, organisations can apply for a permit from NSW Department of Primary
L571@P16: Industries (DPI), which authorises the sale of fish by auction for a charitable purpose.
L572@P16: Auction organisers must submit details of fish sold and financial records of the auction.
L573@P16: Once a permit has been issued, organisers of charity fish auctions must notify their local council.
L574@P16: Organisers of charity fish auctions must comply with Standards 3.2.2 and 3.2.3 of the Code.
