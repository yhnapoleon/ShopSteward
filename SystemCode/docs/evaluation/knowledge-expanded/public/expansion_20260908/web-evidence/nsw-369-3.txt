L374@P9: Potentially hazardous foods and temperature control
L375@P9: One of the most common causes of foodborne illness is the storage and display of potentially
L376@P9: hazardous foods at inadequate temperatures for extended periods. This can lead to the rapid and
L377@P9: sustained growth of food poisoning bacteria.
L378@P9: Examples of potentially hazardous foods include:
L379@P9: • cooked meat
L380@P9: • dairy products
L381@P9: • seafood
L382@P9: • prepared salads, raw salad vegetables
L383@P9: • cooked rice and pasta
L384@P9: • processed soya bean products
L385@P9: • other processed foods containing eggs, beans, nuts
L386@P9: • other protein-rich foods that contain any of the above foods, such as sandwiches and quiches.
L387@P9: A food business must, when storing and displaying potentially hazardous food, store it under
L388@P9-10: temperature control. If the food is intended to be stored frozen, ensure the food remains frozen Guidelines for food businesses at temporary events
L389@P10: FA721/2509 11
L390@P10: during storage and display. Temperature control means maintaining cold food at a temperature of
L391@P10: 5°C or below, or hot food at 60°C or above.
L392@P10: Canned and bottled foods, dried or pickled products and some other processed foods, such as dried
L393@P10: pasta, pasteurised juices and dried powder products, are not considered to be potentially hazardous
L394@P10: unless opened or reconstituted.
L395@P10: All food businesses that handle potentially hazardous foods are required to have a readily
L396@P10: accessible, accurate, probe-type thermometer (+/- 1°C accuracy). Ensure the thermometer probe is
L397@P10: cleaned and sanitised before it is used. It is good practice to monitor the temperature of hot or cold
L398@P10: foods under operating conditions to ensure adequate temperature control is being maintained.
L399@P10: Additional information on potentially hazardous food and its management can be found in
L400@P10: Potentially hazardous foods: Foods that require temperature control for safety (PDF, 352 KB) and
L401@P10: Guidance on the 4-hour/2-hour rule (PDF, 260 KB) at foodauthority.nsw.gov.au.
L402@P10: Cross contamination
L403@P10: Adequate measures must be taken to prevent cross contamination from raw foods to cooked foods.
L404@P10: This includes:
L405@P10: • ensuring there are separate utensils for cooked and raw meats, poultry and seafood
L406@P10: • covering all food unless it is being processed or prepared – unprotected food must not be left
L407@P10: unattended
L408@P10: • keeping cooked meat and salads separate from raw meat, raw poultry and raw seafood
L409@P10: • washing hands after handling raw meats, raw poultry, raw seafood and raw vegetables
L410@P10: • storing food, cooking and eating utensils off the floor
L411@P10: • having appropriate cleaning equipment (mops, buckets, brooms) for multi-day events and allweather conditions
L412@P10: • changes of footwear so that in wet weather circumstances muddy shoes are not worn in the stall
L413@P10: • stall holders should consider allocating single tasks (food preparation, cleaning, serving or
L414@P10: handling money) to individuals as a strategy to reduce the potential for cross contamination
L415@P10: especially during peak service periods.
L416@P10: Transport of food to events
L417@P10: All food and packaging for the event must be transported so that dust, pests and other likely
L418@P10: sources of contamination are excluded. Whole fruits and vegetables and grains which are to be
L419@P10: further processed generally do not need to be transported under temperature control (see the Food
L420@P10: Authority’s guideline Potentially hazardous foods: Foods that require temperature control for safety)
L421@P10: (PDF, 352 KB).
L422@P10: Foods that are required to be stored under temperature control or kept frozen must be placed under
L423@P10: strict temperature control or frozen during transportation. Food-grade plastic or insulated
L424@P10: containers with tight-fitting lids may be used for some foods. Where food comes into contact with
L425@P10: the surface of the container, it should be impervious to moisture, constructed of a food-grade
L426@P10: material and be capable of being easily cleaned and sanitised. Lids should not be removed from food
L427@P10: containers when in transit or unattended.
L428@P10: Commercial food transport vehicles should be suitable for this purpose.
L429@P10: Food storage
L430@P10: • Food business operators must ensure that all foods are stored so that they are protected from
L431@P10: likely contamination, and that the environmental conditions will not adversely affect the safety

L434@P11: • There must be separately located storage facilities for items such as chemicals, clothing and
L435@P11: personal belongings, which may contaminate food or food contact surfaces.
L436@P11: • Food including packaged drinks and alcohol should be stored at least 150mm above the floor
L437@P11: and kept out of direct sunlight.
L438@P11: • Potentially hazardous foods like poultry, meat, dairy products, seafood and egg-based products
L439@P11: must be stored under temperature control. If intended to be stored frozen, the food must remain
L440@P11: frozen during storage. (See Potentially hazardous foods: Foods that require temperature control

L442@P11: • Refrigeration facilities should be large enough to hold potentially hazardous foods under

L444@P11: overstock refrigerators or portable coolrooms, as the air will not be able to circulate freely
L445@P11: around the foods. Food must be stored off the floor in cool rooms and freezers.
L446@P11: • Coolrooms, fridges and freezers should be located out of direct sunlight. For multi-day events,
L447@P11: access to coolrooms should be via a hard surface, such as duckboards.
L448@P11: • Cold foods should be stored at or below 5°C.
L449@P11: • Hot food appliances (bain maries and display cases) should keep hot foods at or above, 60°C at
L450@P11: all times.
L451@P11: Food display
L452@P11: When displaying food, a business must take all practicable measures to protect the food from likely
L453@P11: contamination by customers, dust, fumes or insects. This may mean using plastic food wrap, sealed
L454@P11: containers, sneeze barriers, food covers or other effective measures.
L455@P11: Crockery, wrappings and packaging
L456@P11: • Single-use, disposable eating and drinking utensils may only be used if they meet the
L457@P11: requirements of the Plastics Reduction and Circular Economy Act 2021. More information is
L458@P11: available at epa.nsw.gov.au/Your-environment/Plastics/Bans-guidance.
L459@P11: • If re-usable dinnerware or tableware is used, a business must ensure that an appropriate method
L460@P11: for cleaning and sanitising is used (machine dishwashers are recommended).
L461@P11: • Crockery items that are chipped, cracked, broken or are in a state of disrepair must not be used
L462@P11: for food.
L463@P11: • Packaging material must be food-grade and unlikely to cause food contamination. Only clean
L464@P11: and unprinted paper, food wrap or packaging must be used for wrapping or storing food.
L465@P11: Single-use items
L466@P11: The Plastics Reduction and Circular Economy Act 2021 bans the supply of single-use plastic straws,
L467@P11: cutlery, stirrers, bowls, plates and other items.
L468@P11: Permitted single-use straws, eating utensils and other items that come into contact with food and a
L469@P11: person’s mouth must be protected from contamination until use and not re-used.

L473@P11: • ready-to-eat

L499@P12: Showing that your food is safe
L500@P12: Food businesses that process unpackaged, potentially hazardous food into a food that is ready-toeat and potentially hazardous must be able to show their food is safe under Standard 3.2.2A of the
L501@P12: Code. These businesses must be able to demonstrate they are monitoring and managing key food
L502@P12: safety risks related to receiving, storing, processing, displaying and transporting potentially
L503@P12: hazardous food, and cleaning and sanitising.

L505@P12: • demonstrating safe food handling practices, such as having a written instruction sheet or

L508@P12: • keeping records.

L512@P12: Symptoms may include cold or flu symptoms, diarrhoea, vomiting, sore throat, fever, jaundice and
L513@P12: infectious skin conditions.

L517@P13: Requirements for food handlers
L518@P13: When engaged in any food handling operation, a food handler must:
L519@P13: • not contaminate food or food contact surfaces with their body or clothing
L520@P13: • prevent unnecessary contact with ready-to-eat food
L521@P13: • wear only clean outer clothing
L522@P13: • cover all dressing and bandages on exposed body parts with a waterproof dressing
L523@P13: • not eat over uncovered food or food contact surfaces
L524@P13: • not sneeze, blow or cough over uncovered food or surfaces likely to come into contact with food
L525@P13: • not spit, smoke or use tobacco while working in the food stall
L526@P13: • not urinate or defecate except in a toilet.
L527@P13: Hand washing
L528@P13: A food handler must wash their hands using soap and warm water, then dry them with single use,
L529@P13: towels:
L530@P13: • before commencing or re-commencing handling food
L531@P13: • immediately before handling ready-to-eat food after handling raw food
L532@P13: • immediately after using the toilet
L533@P13: • immediately after smoking, coughing, sneezing, using a handkerchief or tissue, eating, drinking
L534@P13: or touching their hair, scalp or a body opening, and before using disposable gloves for handling
L535@P13: food.
L536@P13: Food handlers wearing gloves should change gloves as often as they are required to wash their
L537@P13: hands.
L538@P13: Money handling/touching customers’ hands when serving food
L539@P13: While the likelihood of contamination from customers’ hands when exchanging money is low,
L540@P13: consideration should be given to minimising the risk. Examples include using a disposable glove or
L541@P13: nominating one staff member to handle money only.
L542@P13: Cleaning and sanitising
L543@P13: • The food stall must be maintained to a standard of cleanliness where there is no accumulation of
L544@P13: garbage or recycled matter (except in appropriate containers), food waste, dirt, grease or other
L545@P13: visible matter.
L546@P13: • All fixtures, fittings and equipment must be maintained and cleaned so there is no accumulation
L547@P13: of food waste, dirt, grease or other visible matter.
L548@P13: • Eating and drinking utensils must be in a clean, sanitary and undamaged condition immediately
L549@P13: before each use.
L550@P13: • Food contact surfaces (such as equipment and utensils) must be kept in a clean and sanitary
L551@P13: condition to reduce the likelihood of contaminating food. ‘Sanitary’ means cleaning first,
L552@P13: followed by heat and/or chemical treatment at the right concentration, or some other process to
L553@P13: reduce the number of bacteria to a level unlikely to compromise the safety of the food. ‘Foodgrade’ chlorine-based sanitisers can be used for this purpose.
L554@P13: • The containers used for chemical storage should be appropriately labelled.
L555@P13: • Chemicals must not be stored near food or any packaging likely to come in contact with food to
L556@P13-14: avoid the risk of contamination. Guidelines for food businesses at temporary events

L614@P15: requires electrical appliances and leads to be tested at least annually and identification tags

L617@P15: and comply with the appropriate provisions of ‘AS/NZS 1596-2014: The Storage and Handling of
L618@P15: LP Gas’ and ‘AS 5601-2022: Gas Installations’. A current compliance plate should be attached to
L619@P15: the vehicle for new installations or any changes made to existing gas appliances.
L620@P15: • All portable gas appliances and appliances connected to Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG)
L621@P15: cylinders must be certified to comply with Australian Standards by a certifier accredited by NSW
L622@P15: Fair Trading. Refer to the NSW Fair Trading webpage on gas appliances:

L634@P15: • Fire safety equipment should be easily accessible. The extinguisher should be suitable for
