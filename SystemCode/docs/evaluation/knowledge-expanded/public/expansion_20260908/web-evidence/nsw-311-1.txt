L0@P0: Food Authority
L1@P0: Food grade packaging
L2@P0: As packaging and containers used to store food are in direct contact with the
L3@P0: food, they need to be suitable and ‘food grade’. If the packaging is made from
L4@P0: inappropriate materials, it could make the food unsafe.
L5@P0: Packaging must be suitable for its intended use
L6@P0: The Food Standards Code details specific requirements for surfaces in contact with foods, including
L7@P0: containers and packaging in which food is processed or stored. They must be:
L8@P0: • adequate for the production of safe and suitable food
L9@P0: • fit for their intended use.
L10@P0: For a food contact surface to be considered food grade it must be able to be effectively cleaned, and
L11@P0: must be made from a material that will not migrate into, contaminate or taint the food. The
L12@P0: requirements for a plastic to be considered food grade are listed in the Australian Standard for Plastics
L13@P0: materials for food contact use (AS 2070-1999).
L14@P0: This Standard applies to manufacturers of plastics materials for food contact and specifies procedures
L15@P0: to be used during the various stages of production. The requirements apply to such items as
L16@P0: packages, domestic containers, wrapping materials, utensils or any other plastics items intended to
L17@P0: come into contact with food.
L18@P0: The Food Standards Code also specifies the maximum allowable levels in foods for certain
L19@P0: compounds commonly used in making food packaging (such as tin in canned foods, acrylonitrile and
L20@P0: vinyl chloride – used in the production of plastics – in any food).
L21@P0: The US Food & Drug Administration (FDA)
L22@P0: maintains a database listing the approved food
L23@P0: contact substances that have been demonstrated to
L24@P0: be safe for their intended use.
L25@P0: Most containers will outline on the label whether or
L26@P0: not it is food grade and what it is suitable for (such
L27@P0: as the labels below). If no such indication is made
L28@P0: on the label or you are unsure whether or not the
L29@P0: container is food grade, either contact the
L30@P0: manufacturer or assume the container is not food
L31@P0-1: grade. Figure 1 The meaning of label images.Food grade packaging

L33@P1: Cleaning
L34@P1: Any surface that comes into contact with food must be able to be easily and effectively cleaned and,
L35@P1: where necessary, sanitised.
L36@P1: Any container or packaging used to store food should be smooth, and free of cracks, chips, crevices,
L37@P1: ridges or grooves that could harbour bacteria and hinder attempts to easily and effectively clean it.
L38@P1: Any defects in a food contact surface can allow the build-up of food scraps, and harbour bacteria
L39@P1: which can contaminate the food.
L40@P1: Suitable packaging materials
L41@P1: Food contact surfaces must be made of material that will not contaminate food and are impervious to
L42@P1: grease, food particles and water.
L43@P1: Containers and packaging must be made from materials that will not contaminate food by allowing
L44@P1: chemicals to migrate from the packaging into the food, or by giving the food an unacceptable taste or
L45@P1: odour.
L46@P1: Some materials are not suitable for use with acidic foods. Examples include:
L47@P1: • lead in ceramic, china and crystal utensils, solders/flux (flowing agents) and pewter
L48@P1: • galvanised metal
L49@P1: • copper and copper alloys
L50@P1: • unglazed earthenware is unsuitable for eating and drinking utensils.
L51@P1: The material used in food packaging must be able to withstand the uses it would normally be exposed
L52@P1: to, such as microwaving, or storing hot food or frozen food.
L53@P1: Containers and packaging made specifically for food contact use are recommended because they are
L54@P1: made from suitable materials tested for contact with food, and are usually designed for easier cleaning
L55@P1: and sanitising.
L56@P1: Reuse of containers
L57@P1: Containers that have been used as packaging for food must not be reused unless they can be
L58@P1: properly cleaned and, if necessary, sanitised to prevent cross contamination.
L59@P1: Some food grade containers, such as ice cream containers, may be suitable for reuse on a short-term
L60@P1: basis, but they are not ideal. The number of times these containers can be safely reused is limited by
L61@P1: how well they retain their properties after being washed in hot water and detergent. With constant
L62@P1: reuse the containers may become brittle and crack. This increases the risk of the container breaking
L63@P1: and contaminating the food. Brittle containers are not considered suitable and should be discarded.
L64@P1: Where jars and lids are reused, they should be:
L65@P1: • inspected prior to use (and reuse) and discarded if they have damaged seals, or are cracked or
L66@P1: soiled with anything likely to taint or contaminate food (such as any non-food chemicals)
L67@P1: • thoroughly cleaned so that no residue is left on the jars and lids (a dedicated automatic glassware
L68@P1: washer would be ideal, but careful washing by hand may also be effective).
L69@P1: • thoroughly sanitised (this might not be required if the food products are to be hot-filled at or above
L70@P1-2: 85°C).Food grade packaging

L72@P2: Single use items are not to be reused
L73@P2: The Food Standards Code specifically prohibits the reuse of single use items in contact with food.
L74@P2: Single use items are generally defined as those where the manufacturer of the item intends for it to be
L75@P2: only used once in connection with food handling (such as plastic takeaway containers). There may be
L76@P2: other non-food contact uses where these containers can be reused.
L77@P2: Outer containers
L78@P2: Outer containers are sometimes used to exclude pests from an opened package of food, such as bags
L79@P2: of flour. These outer containers need to protect the food from contamination and include a tight-fitting
L80@P2: lid. Although outer containers may not directly be in contact with food, they still must be suitable for
L81@P2: their intended use and able to be easily cleaned. A rubbish bin would not be considered suitable for
L82@P2: this purpose.
