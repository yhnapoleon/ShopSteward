L38@P2: Why is a Food Safety Supervisor (FSS) required?
L39@P2: NSW and Australian food laws require certain businesses in the hospitality and retail food service
L40@P2: sector to appoint at least one trained FSS per business premises .
L41@P2: These laws include the NSW Food Act 20 03, NSW Food Regulation 2025 and the Australian New
L42@P2: Zealand Food Standards Code.
L43@P2: The aim of the requirement is to prevent individuals from becoming ill from food poisoning as a
L44@P2: result of incorrect handling and preparation of food.
L45@P2: Statistics show incorrect food handling practices in hospitality and retail businesses account for
L46@P2: around 40 -50% of foodborne illness outbreaks in NSW, costing the community hundreds of millions
L47@P2: of dollars each year in healthcare and lost revenue.
L48@P2: Appointing an FSS gives food businesses a better level of onsite protection for food safety and
L49@P2: gives consumers peace of mind when buying food in NSW.
L50@P2: Which businesses need a Food Safety Supervisor?
L51@P2: Under Standard 3.2.2A of the Food Standards Code, t he FSS requirement applies to all b usinesses
L52@P2: that handle food that is potentially hazardous (requires temperature control ), is ready -to-eat and is
L53@P2: not sold or served in its original packaging. These businesses include:
L54@P2: • restaurants
L55@P2: • c afés
L56@P2: • takeaway outlets
L57@P2: • c aterers
L58@P2: • bakeries
L59@P2: • pubs , c lubs and h otels
L60@P2: • mobile food vendors and temporary market stalls .
L61@P2: From 8 December 2024 the FSS requirement also appl ied to:
L62@P2: • school canteens
L63@P2: • children’s services that provide meals, including out of school hours care
L64@P2: • correctional centres
L65@P2: • delicatessens
L66@P2: • greengrocers
L67@P2: • supermarkets
L68@P2-3: • coffee vendors that sell unpackaged, potentially hazardous, ready -to-eat food Guideline to Food Safety Supervisor (FSS) requirements

L70@P3: • boarding schools.
L71@P3: The Standard 3.2.2A Food Safety Tools quiz can help businesses and organisations determine
L72@P3: whether they require an FSS. See www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/safetytools for more information.
L73@P3: What is a Food Safety Supervisor?
L74@P3: A Food Safety Supervisor is a person who:
L75@P3: • is formally trained to recognise and prevent risks associated with food handling in food
L76@P3: business es
L77@P3: • holds a current NSW Food Authority FSS certificate (no more than 5 years old) as per the Food
L78@P3: Act – Section 106B(1a)
L79@P3: • is not a n FSS for any other food premises or mobile catering business
L80@P3: • trains and supervises other people in the business about safe food handling practices , and
L81@P3: • has the authority and ability to manage and give direction on the safe handling of food.
L82@P3: Who can be a Food Safety Supervisor?
L83@P3: The FSS is nominated by the owner of a food business and may be the business owner, manager, or
L84@P3: employee ( for example, the chef) provided they meet the criteria detailed above. For a small
L85@P3: business, it may be most appropriate for the business owner to be the FSS.
L86@P3: The FSS must have authority to supervise others in the business to ensure safe food handling occurs
L87@P3: as per the Food Act – Section 106B(1c).
L88@P3: When do I have to appoint my Food Safety Supervisor?
L89@P3: Businesses must appoint an FSS from the time potentially hazardous , ready -to-eat food is first
L90@P3: handled and sold. Contact your local council for more information on starting a food business.
L91@P3: How many Food Safety Supervisors do I need?
L92@P3: Businesses need to appoint at least one FSS per premises as per the Food Ac t – Section 106B(1b) .
L93@P3: Businesses with several premises cannot use the same FSS for each premises - they must nominate
L94@P3: a different FSS for each premises.
L95@P3: Larger businesses , or those that operate longer hours , may choose to nominate several people to be
L96@P3-4: trained and appointed as an FSS to help cover shift work and annual leave.Guideline to Food Safety Supervisor (FSS) requirements

L98@P4: Does the Food Safety Supervisor have to be at the business all the time?
L99@P4: No, although it is best practice that the FSS is on the premises at all times when food handling is
L100@P4: undertaken. If the FSS is away , for example not on shift, on annual leave, or sick, the business must
L101@P4: still maintain food safety. The FSS must facilitate this by:
L102@P4: • sharing food safety knowledge with other staff
L103@P4: • displaying signage and posters with key food safety messages
L104@P4: • developing work instructions that staff can follow to continue handling food safely . Examples
L105@P4: include writing on paper or electronic templates, and written instructions that have been verified,
L106@P4: such as a validated recipe or standard operating procedure (SOP) such as a cleaning schedule.
L107@P4: Larger businesses or those that operate longer hours may choose to nominate several people to be
L108@P4: trained and appointed as an FSS to help cover shift work and leave.
L109@P4: Training and assessment requirements
L110@P4: A FSS need s to complete training and assessment in units of competency within the Vocational
L111@P4: Education and Training (VET) system . The legislation requires recertification training every 5 years –
L112@P4: for more information see the FSS recertification section below.
L113@P4: There are 2 pathways to choose from to achieve FSS certification, hospitality or retail, depending on
L114@P4: the business type :
L115@P4: Two hospitality units:
L116@P4: • SITXFSA005 – Use hygiene practices for food safety
L117@P4: • SITXFSA006 – Participate in safe food handling practices
L118@P4: OR
L119@P4: One retail unit:
L120@P4: • SIRRFSA001 – Handle food safely in a retail environment.
L121@P4: Businesses should choose the unit s of competenc y that are more relevant to their business type ( for
L122@P4: example, a fast food chain can be considered as a retail business; a restaurant or café can be
L123@P4: considered as a hospitality business).
L124@P4: There is no requirement for the FSS to undertake units beyond the minimum competencies listed
L125@P4: above.
L126@P4: The units of competency must incorporate mandatory key focus areas as determined by the Food
L127@P4: Authority and listed on its website. The current focus areas include allergen management, cleaning
L128@P4: and sanitising, safe egg handling and Food Act offences. The se key focus areas were developed in
L129@P4-5: response to common high risk food safety issues in the retail and hospitality sectors.Guideline to Food Safety Supervisor (FSS) requirements

L131@P5: Where can the F ood Safety Supervisor be trained?
L132@P5: Training will only be recognised if it is delivered by a Registered Training Organisation (RTO) that
L133@P5: has been approved by the Food Authority to deliver F SS training and issue NSW FSS certificates .
L134@P5: Approved RTOs are listed on the Food Authority’s website . Businesses can also contact the ir local
L135@P5: council to find out where the nearest approved RTO is.
L136@P5: What is an RTO?
L137@P5: A Registered Training Organisation (RTO) is registered by national and in some cases state and
L138@P5: territory training authorities to deliver nationally recognised training and conduct assessments to
L139@P5: issue nationally recognised qualifications.
L140@P5: RTOs may include government departments, TAFE colleges, adult and community education
L141@P5: providers, private providers, community organisations, schools, higher education institutions,
L142@P5: industry bodies or other organisations meeting the registration requirement s.
L143@P5: In all instances RTO’s must be approved by the NSW Food Authority to issue NSW FSS certificates.
L144@P5: How is the training delivered and how long will it take?
L145@P5: FSS training can generally be completed in one full day ( for example, through face -to-face training
L146@P5: and assessment ). However, the duration of the course will depend on the type of delivery option
L147@P5: offered by approved RTOs , which can include:
L148@P5: • face -to-face
L149@P5: • online
L150@P5: • workplace based
L151@P5: • correspondence
L152@P5: • a combination of the above.
L153@P5: RTOs must issue NSW FSS certificates within 10 working days of successfully completing the
L154@P5: course.
L155@P5: How much will training cost?
L156@P5: Training costs are set by individual approved RTOs. RTOs will issue the NSW FSS certificate and
L157@P5-6: cannot charge more than $30 to issue it. Guideline to Food Safety Supervisor (FSS) requirements

L159@P6: Food Safety Supervisor certificate
L160@P6: The NSW FSS certificate is issued by the approved RTO , in addition to a
L161@P6: Statement of Attainment , after successful completion of the training
L162@P6: and assessment requirements .
L163@P6: A FSS certificate states that the person it is issued to is qualified to be
L164@P6: a FSS . The FSS certificate is valid for 5 years from the date of issue.
L165@P6: It is a legal requirement that all businesses keep a copy of their FSS
L166@P6: certificate on the premises and produce it for inspection on request by
L167@P6: an authorised officer. The Food Authority encourages businesses to
L168@P6: display their FSS certificate.
L169@P6: Penalties for non -compliance
L170@P6: There are 2 offences that carry penalties if a business does not comply :
L171@P6: • fail to appoint a F SS - business does not appoint at least one FSS per premises (or per business
L172@P6: for mobile caterers) within 30 days of the original FSS ceasing to act in that role
L173@P6: • fail to keep a copy of the FSS certificate on the premises and produce it for inspection - business
L174@P6: does not keep a copy of the certificate on the premises and does not produce it for inspection on
L175@P6: request by an authorised officer .
L176@P6: Penalty notice infringements for the above offences can be up to $330 for an individual (for
L177@P6: example, a sole trader) and $660 for a registered company.
L178@P6: These offences can also be prosecut ed in serious cases. Refer to the Food Act 2003 for the
L179@P6: maximum penalties that apply.
L180@P6: Will previous training be recognised?
L181@P6: People who have completed all required units of a vocational qualification from a single RTO in
L182@P6: Australia ( For example, Cert IV in Commercial Cookery) within the past 6 months can apply to the
L183@P6: Food Authority for an FSS certificate. They will need to complete the FSS certificate request form
L184@P6: available on the Food Authority website , together with a certified copy of their Qualification and
L185@P6: Transcript and $30 payment to the Food Authority.
L186@P6: See www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/retail/fss -food -safety -supervisors for more i nformation ,
L187@P6: including eligibility.
L188@P6: How is Food Safety Supervisor training different to general food safety skills
L189@P6: and knowledge training?
L190@P6: It is mandatory for all food handlers to have general food handling skills and knowledge appropriate
L191@P6-7: to their role in the business. This is specified in the Food Standards Code.Guideline to Food Safety Supervisor (FSS) requirements
L192@P7: FA 774 /2603 8
L193@P7: The FSS training is ‘accredited’ training as it is tied to qualifications within the nationally recognised
L194@P7: Vocational Education and Training (VET) system.
L195@P7: FSS training is an additional requirement for at least 1 person in certain hospitality and retail food
L196@P7: service businesses. It does not replace skills and knowledge requirements for all food handlers.
L197@P7: The NSW FSS requires the national units of competency and mandatory key focus areas .
L198@P7: Is the Food Safety Supervisor solely responsible for food safety in a business?
L199@P7: No. Everyone who works in a food business has a responsibility to handle food safely.
L200@P7: Business owners/proprietors must:
L201@P7: • put effective food safety processes in place
L202@P7: • notify their food business with the relevant authority (usually the local council )
L203@P7: • ensure their business complies with the Food Standards Code, including 3.2.2A and other food
L204@P7: safety requirements
L205@P7: • appoint an FSS
L206@P7: • ensure a copy of the current FSS certificate is on the premises at all times. The Food Authority
L207@P7: encourages businesses to display their FSS certificate.
L208@P7: • ensure staff are trained in the skills and knowledge they require for their role
L209@P7: • keep food safety risk management records (if required) or be able to demonstrate how the
L210@P7: business keeps food safe
L211@P7: • be aware they are liable if a food safety breach does occur .
L212@P7: Food Safety Supervisor s:
L213@P7: • are nominated by the business owner and work under the direction of the owner
L214@P7: • supervise food handling in the business to make sure it is done safely
L215@P7: • must understand the overall food safety processes of the business as it applies to all staff
L216@P7: • are subject to individual liability and personal hygiene provisions (under Standard 3.2.2 Div ision 4
L217@P7: Subdivision 1 - 13,14,15).
L218@P7: Food handlers:
L219@P7: • must have skills and knowledge relevant to their food duties to keep and sell safe food . For
L220@P7: example, a chef need s greater food safety skills and knowledge than a waiter or kitchen hand
L221@P7: • are subject to individual personal hygiene provisions (under Standard 3.2.2 Div ision 4 Subdivision
L222@P7-8: 1- 13,14,15).Guideline to Food Safety Supervisor (FSS) requirements

L224@P8: What if a Food Safety Supervisor changes their name, or damages or loses
L225@P8: their certificate?
L226@P8: If a FSS’s personal details change , such as their name , they should first contact the RTO that issued
L227@P8: the FSS certificate and request a new one . The FSS will need to find out from the RTO what
L228@P8: documentation must be submitted before a certificate is reissued.
L229@P8: If an FSS certificate is lost or damaged, the RTO that issued the certificate will be able to issue a
L230@P8: replacement.
L231@P8: RTOs may charge a fee for the reissue of an FSS certificate. This cost is set by the RTO and not the
L232@P8: Food Authority.
L233@P8: If the RTO that issued the certificate has ceased to operate or is no longer approved under the NSW
L234@P8: FSS program, the Food Authority can issue a replacement certificate .
L235@P8: The business and FSS should act as soon as possible to replace an FSS certificate, as it is a legal
L236@P8: requirement that all businesses that require a FSS keep a copy of the certificate on the premises.
L237@P8: What if the Food Safety Supervisor leaves the business?
L238@P8: As the FSS certificate holder must be working in the business, the business owner must appoint a
L239@P8: new Food Safety Supervisor within 30 operational days ( for example, days that food is processed
L240@P8: and sold) of that FSS leaving or ceasing to act in that role .
L241@P8: Summary of roles
L242@P8: Role of the business
L243@P8: • Appoint at least one trained FSS per premises (or per business for mobile caterers).
L244@P8: • Ensure the FSS obtains the FSS certificate by completing FSS training and assessment .
L245@P8: • Keep a copy of the FSS certificate on the premises at all times. Display is recommended .
L246@P8: • Ensure the FSS certificate is valid (no t more than 5 years old ).
L247@P8: Role of local councils
L248@P8: Upon routine inspections of food businesses , authorised officers can ask to see a copy of the FSS
L249@P8: certificate. This must be clear and legible.
L250@P8: Role of RTOs
L251@P8: • Be approved by the NSW Food Authority to deliver NSW FSS training and assessment in the
L252@P8: required units of competency as well as the mandatory key focus areas and issue FSS
L253@P8: certificates .
L254@P8-9: • Comply with the Food Authority’s conditions of approval and RTO approval criteria.Guideline to Food Safety Supervisor (FSS) requirements

L256@P9: Food Safety Supervisor recertification
L257@P9: NSW FSS certificates are valid for 5 years from the date of issue, after which they need to be
L258@P9: renewed.
L259@P9: When a NSW FSS holder’s certification expires, the certificate holder has 90 days to replace their
L260@P9: expired certificate by completing a recertification course through an approved RTO offering that
L261@P9: course.
L262@P9: The recertifying RTO may recognise the applicant ’s prior learning (units of competency) as part of
L263@P9: the recertification course.
L264@P9: If a certificate holder’s certificate expired more than 90 days ago, they must complete the full
L265@P9: course again, including the required units of competency and key focus areas.
L266@P9: This is different to the requirements for a food business where the business must appoint a new FSS
L267@P9: within 30 days if:
L268@P9: • the appointed FSS leaves the business, or
L269@P9: • the appointed FSS certificate expires.
L270@P9: The new FSS may be a previously appointed FSS who completes recertification.
L271@P9: Steps for businesses to comply with the Food Safety Supervisor requirements
L272@P9: 1. Determine whether your business needs an FSS .
L273@P9: 2. Choose who will be the FSS for your business .
L274@P9: 3. The chosen FSS comple tes the training and assessment through an approved RTO.
L275@P9: 4. The FSS receives a Statement of Attainment and a NSW FSS certificate. A copy is kept on the
L276@P9: premises .
L277@P9: 5. After 5 years, the FSS completes recertification training and assessment for an updated NSW
L278@P9: FSS certificate and an updated Statement of Attainment for the required units of competency.
