L0@P0: Food Authority
L1@P0: Labelling requirements for food sold in Australia and New Zealand are set out in Chapter 1 and
L2@P0: Chapter 2 of the Food Standards Code (the Code).
L3@P0: Food labelling provides consumers with important information about the food they are buying and
L4@P0: consuming. The information on the label must:
L5@P0: • be visible on the outside of the package
L6@P0: • be legible and in English
L7@P0: • be unambiguous
L8@P0: • not mislead the consumer.
L9@P0: Exemptions from labelling requirements apply to unpackaged food and some packaged foods – see
L10@P0: Labelling exemptions below.
L11@P0: Under food law, advertising information is equivalent to labelling.
L12@P0: Information a food label must contain
L13@P0: The food label must identify the following basic information:
L14@P0: • a name for the food which is prescribed by the Code, or a name/description that describes the
L15@P0: true nature of the food
L16@P0: • the production ‘lot’ of the food prepared under the same conditions and during a particular span
L17@P0: of time (date coding can in some circumstances satisfy the requirement for a lot number)
L18@P0: • the name and street address in Australia or New Zealand of the supplier, packer, manufacturer,
L19@P0: vendor or importer of the food
L20@P0: • warning and advisory statements and declarations of the presence of substances that may
L21@P0: adversely affect the health of people with allergies and food sensitivities
L22@P0: • a list of the ingredients
L23@P0: • a statement of the shelf life of the product as either a ‘use-by’ or a ‘best before’ date if the food
L24@P0: has a shelf life of less than 2 years (see related factsheet Labelling – Date marking, storage
L25@P0: conditions and directions for use (PDF, 147 KB))
L26@P0: • directions for use and storage where these are needed for reasons of health and safety or to
L27@P0: ensure shelf life is achieved
L28@P0: • a nutrition information panel (NIP), which shows the quantity of the basic nutrients contained in
L29@P0: the food, per serving and per 100g of that food. Certain packaged foods are exempt from the
L30@P0: requirement to carry a NIP, such as alcoholic beverages, water, herbs and spices, and prepared
L31@P0: sandwiches.
L32@P0: Additional information such as characterising ingredients may also be required. See
L33@P0: foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/food-labelling for more information.

L36@P1: Portion-packed foods
L37@P1: Retail businesses often purchase bulk-packed foods, such as cheese wheels, smallgoods, nuts and
L38@P1: pulses, which are then divided into portions and repackaged on their premises. The retailer displays
L39@P1: these foods in self-service cabinets. Although portion-packed foods require labelling information (as
L40@P1: listed above), there are some additional options available to retailers to comply with the Code.
L41@P1: Retailers may be able to choose to:
L42@P1: • attach a label to each portion-packed food, or
L43@P1: • display labelling information on a sign/booklet or tearaway pamphlet near the portion-packed food.
L44@P1: The information must be legible, and the consumer should be able to readily identify the portionpacked food to which the information relates. Information that may change frequently or is essential to
L45@P1: protect consumer safety (for example, the use-by date, lot code, storage conditions, allergens) should
L46@P1: be declared on a label that is attached to the portion-packed food.
L47@P1: Labelling exemptions
L48@P1: Some food offered for retail sale is exempt from general labelling requirements. Food does not need to
L49@P1: be labelled if it is:
L50@P1: • unpackaged
L51@P1: • in an ‘inner’ package, not designed for sale without the outer package
L52@P1: • made and packaged on the premises from which it is sold. For a food to be considered ‘made’ as
L53@P1: well as ‘packaged’ on the premises, it must have been processed on the premises in a way that
L54@P1: changed the nature of the food, before being packaged on the premises. Merely dividing and
L55@P1: repackaging portions of food from bulk to individual smaller pieces for sale does not in itself qualify
L56@P1: the food for a labelling exemption
L57@P1: • packaged in the presence of the purchaser. A retailer may sell food without a label on the package
L58@P1: where the customer witnesses the packaging of the food as any questions about the food can be
L59@P1: put to the person serving the food for sale
L60@P1: • packaged food displayed in an assisted service cabinet, which requires food to be served on
L61@P1: request from the purchaser
L62@P1: • whole or cut vegetables and fruit, in a package that displays the nature and the quality of the food
L63@P1: (sprouting seeds are excluded from this exemption)
L64@P1: • delivered packaged and ready for consumption at the order of the consumer
L65@P1: • sold at a fundraising event (see related factsheet Food safety requirements for charitable and notfor-profit organisations (PDF,158 KB)).
L66@P1: Even if the food is exempt, the retailer must comply with requirements for certain warning statements
L67@P1: and details of food that has been genetically modified or irradiated.
L68@P1: With the exception of the presence of royal jelly, genetically modified foods and irradiated foods
L69@P1: require the information to be displayed in conjunction with the food or providing the information to the
L70@P1-2: purchaser on request.Labelling – general requirements

L72@P2: Enforcement
L73@P2: The NSW Food Authority is responsible for administering the labelling and information provisions in
L74@P2: the Code and Food Act 2003 (NSW).
L75@P2: The Food Authority is not responsible for:
L76@P2: • the presence and accuracy of total weights and measures statements, which is the responsibility of
L77@P2: the National Measurement Institute (however, the Food Authority is responsible for percentage
L78@P2: ingredient statements)
L79@P2: • barcodes
L80@P2: • recycling codes
L81@P2: • composition and labelling of pet food
L82@P2: • therapeutic products (including dietary aids and supplements) registered by the Therapeutic
L83@P2: Goods Administration, which are governed by that agency
L84@P2: • country of origin labelling
L85@P2: • compliance with Australian Consumer Law.
L86@P2: As food labelling is regulated by a wide range of laws it is recommended that food businesses
L87@P2: consider engaging a labelling consultant if they are uncertain of their obligations.
